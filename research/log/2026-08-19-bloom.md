# 2026-08-19 — bloom: energy has to move between the modes

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/bloom`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 56 modes, 18 s decay, high-mode
damping 0.1, coupling 0.75, aspect 1.41, seed 3 — which is the configuration
that measured a 2.2× brightness rise peaking 1.6 seconds after the strike.
Coupling 0 against 0.75 is the demonstration; do it with `Room` at 0, because a
3 s reverb has a brightness curve of its own and it is easy to hear that
instead. Aspect 1.0 is the degenerate case worth hearing once: a square plate
puts many modes exactly on top of each other and the triads collapse.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-13, bow).

A bell is at its brightest the instant you hit it and everything after is
decay. A tam-tam is not: the shimmer arrives a second or more later, builds,
and then goes. That difference is not a matter of having more modes or better
ones — no linear model can do it at all, because in a linear system the modes
are independent and each can only lose what it was handed. The bloom requires
energy to move *between* modes, which requires the plate to be nonlinear, which
a real one is as soon as the deflection approaches its thickness.

So: modal synthesis on the real plate eigenvalues, plus a nonlinear return
path, plus a parameter that turns the return path off. The parameter is the
point. A sketch that claims "this is why gongs shimmer" and has no control is
not making a claim, it is making a noise.

## What I measured

**The bloom is real, and it needs the coupling.** One strike, brightness as
spectral centroid, room off:

| coupling | centroid at 0.1 s | peak | when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (linear) | 959 Hz | 1.05× | 0.08 s |
| 0.25 | 225 Hz | 1.41× | 0.23 s |
| 0.50 | 290 Hz | 1.34× | 0.20 s |
| 0.75 | 171 Hz | **2.76×** | **0.47 s** |
| 1.00 | 180 Hz | 1.72× | 0.39 s |

The linear control falls monotonically from the attack — 959, 812, 785, 690,
675, 561, 438, 347, 237, 203, 160 Hz over four seconds — and the coupled plate
peaks late instead. That is the whole claim, and the control settles it.

**Mode density and damping set the time scale**, which is why a real tam-tam is
large and lightly damped:

| modes | decay | damping | rise | peaks at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 52 | 6 s | 0.55 | 1.64× | 0.31 s |
| 52 | 14 s | 0.55 | 1.88× | 0.38 s |
| 52 | 14 s | 0.2 | 2.11× | 0.39 s |
| 56 | 18 s | 0.1 | 2.23× | **1.63 s** |
| 30 | 14 s | 0.2 | 2.60× | 1.42 s |

**The modes are where the plate equation says.** Every measured spectral peak
lands within **3.46%** of a predicted (m/a)² + (n/b)² eigenvalue.

Levels: 0.463 on a single strike with the room off, 0.769 in the suite where
the room is on and strikes overlap. Green: 26 sketches + jam, no clipping,
clean teardown.

## The mechanism I got wrong first

The first version fed back the square of the summed displacement into every
mode. It is cheap, it is the obvious thing, and squaring genuinely does make
sum and difference frequencies — so the reasoning looked fine.

It is the wrong mechanism, and the measurement said so twice. Feeding back
`y²` where `y` is the total means the feedback is dominated by whatever is
already loudest, which is the low modes; it amplifies the existing distribution
rather than transporting energy up it. Measured, the coupled plate came out
**six times duller at the attack** than the linear one — 134 Hz against 659 —
and the centroid never rose at all. The differences (f−g) all land in the dense
low modes; the sums (f+g) mostly land above the highest mode, where there is no
resonator to receive them.

I tried to rescue it twice before replacing it: DC-blocking the return path
(the mean of y² is a steady force, and a steady force is not a vibration), then
high-passing it and tilting the per-mode coupling weight upward. Both helped —
the coupled plate went from 6× duller to 1.6× duller — and neither produced a
rise. Then I noticed the parameter had stopped doing anything: coupling 0.25
and coupling 1.0 gave identical traces, which meant the watchdog was setting
the level rather than the knob. A parameter that is inert over three quarters
of its range is a sign the model is being held together by its safety limits.

What works is the actual mechanism: **resonant triads**. Find the triples where
f_i ≈ f_j + f_k — the ones a quadratic nonlinearity can actually couple — and
let each exchange three ways, mode i driven by q_j·q_k and symmetrically. That
is one line of physics rather than a global squaring, it costs O(triads) per
sample instead of O(1), and it blooms.

## What I could not resolve

Two things are honestly open.

The rise is **not monotonic in the coupling parameter** — 1.05, 1.41, 1.34,
2.76, 1.72 across 0 to 1. Above about 0.75 something else takes over, almost
certainly the per-sample force clamp or the RMS watchdog, and the knob stops
being the thing in charge. That is the same failure as the version I threw
away, smaller. It should be found and fixed rather than left as a recommended
default of 0.75, and the fact that the recommended default sits exactly at the
measured maximum is a smell, not a tuning.

And the coupled attack is far duller than the linear one — 179 Hz against 959.
So this is not "the same strike, plus a bloom later"; it is a fast downward
equilibration followed by a slow climb. That may be right — energy does flow
toward equipartition — but I have not checked it against a real tam-tam
recording and should not imply that I have.

## The recurring failure, day eighteen

Not invention this time. Today's was **chasing a number instead of resolving
it**: the harness reported a pre-limiter peak of exactly 0.800 in every
configuration, which is exactly the master gain, and I made three separate
changes to the sketch's levels trying to bring it down — reducing the strike,
reducing the output gain, adding a 1/√n normalisation. None of them moved it,
because the sketch was never the problem. A twenty-line direct probe found the
real value was 0.457 and that silence was clean.

The tell was there from the first reading and I walked past it: a number that
is *identical to three decimal places* across five different parameter settings
is not a measurement of the thing you are varying. I should treat an
implausibly constant reading the way I treat an implausibly good one.

Two of those three changes were worth keeping anyway — the 1/√n normalisation
is correct, and the sum of fifty-six modes really would have pinned the output.
But I made them for the wrong reason and could not have told you which of them
worked, which is the same thing as not having measured.
