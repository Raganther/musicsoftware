# 2026-07-28 — smart jam: global key, scene morph, deeper voices

**Touched:** `src/runtime/jam.ts`, `src/core/voices.ts`,
`sketches/poly-synth`, `sketches/euclidean-drift`, `sketches/chord-loom`

## Global key

The whole feature fell out of a convention nobody planned as an API: every
pitched sketch happens to expose `root` (number) + `scale` (select) params
under those exact names. So the jam broadcasts into the params it finds —
sketches needed zero changes, and any future sketch that follows the
convention joins the key system automatically. Convention-as-contract.

Decisions that mattered:

- **Broadcast a pitch class, not a note.** Each channel retunes to the
  octave of the target nearest its current root, clamped to its own param
  range. The bass stays bass, the harp stays where it rings — everyone
  agrees on D without agreeing on which D. Verified: roots 45 (A2) and
  52 (E3) both landed on 50 (D3), their nearest D.
- **Key changes land on the bar line** (toggleable). A mid-phrase retune
  reads as a glitch; the same retune on the downbeat reads as a chord
  change. The pending chip pulses until it lands.
- **Per-strip link chips.** Unlink a channel to hold it in the old key —
  deliberate bitonality as a performance gesture, and the "complementary
  scales" answer: split the rack, then resolve it on a bar line.
- Clicking the lit key releases the jam from the global key entirely.

## Scene morph

Scene recall now glides instead of jumping: numeric params, levels, filters,
bpm and swing interpolate over ½–4 bars (smoothstep), so a scene change is a
*transition you can perform*. Two things must NOT glide, learned by thinking
rather than debugging for once: `seed` (lerping it re-rolls the piece every
frame) and `root` (a chromatic walk through nonsense). Numeric-but-categorical
params snap; everything else glides. Mutes became fades: unmute fades in from
silence, mute fades out then latches. Morphing *bpm* between scenes gives
accelerando/ritardando for free, which no grid sequencer interface I know
offers as a first-class gesture.

## Deeper voices

PolySynth gained filterType (lowpass/highpass/bandpass), sine sub-oscillator,
stereo spread (golden-angle voice placement — wide but deterministic, no rng
needed), velocity→filter and keytrack. All default to previous behaviour.
Surfaced as new params in poly-synth (all five), euclidean-drift (wave, tone,
width) and chord-loom (brightness, width).

One real acoustics bug found by measurement: the `sqrt(Q)` loudness
compensation was calibrated for lowpass resonance, but a biquad bandpass
peaks at unity regardless of Q — compensating it made bandpass mode
mysteriously quiet (measured 0.025 vs 0.052 fixed). Filter-type-dependent
compensation now.

## What surprised me

- The key row is the closest thing the lab has to *conducting*: one click
  and the whole band modulates on the next downbeat. It changes what a jam
  feels like more than any sound parameter added today.
- Morph + muted-channel scenes compose into an arrangement workflow: store
  A with drums muted, store B with them in, and the 2-bar morph writes the
  transition. Scenes stopped being presets and became sections.
