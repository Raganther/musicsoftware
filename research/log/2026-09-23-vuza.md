# 2026-09-23 — vuza: a canon that fills 72 pulses and repeats none of them

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/vuza`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — `neither (Vuza)`, seed 7, spread 1,
contour 2.5, decay 0.42, 126 bpm. Seeds 7 and 13 give different entry sets on
the same subject and are both verified aperiodic at load. The demonstration is
the `Which half loops` select: three canons with the same 6 × 12 split on the
same 72 pulses, differing only in which half is a loop.

## What I tried

Sequencing and rhythm, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-18 with
`nest`. The idea was already written down, left over from `tiling` on
2026-08-10:

> Vuza canons — the rhythms that tile but whose entry set is *not* periodic.
> The search in `tiling` almost finds them.

A tiling canon is a subject `a` and a set of entries `b` with `a ⊕ b = Z_n`:
every pulse struck exactly once, no gaps and no collisions. `tiling` searched
for `b` given a drawn `a` and never asked the next question, which is whether
the canon it found is secretly a **loop**. A set is *periodic* when some nonzero
shift maps it to itself, so a periodic subject is a shorter rhythm repeated and
a periodic entry set is the voices coming in on a cycle.

Hajós asked in 1949 whether every factorisation of a cyclic group has a periodic
factor. For most n it does. The smallest n where it does not is **72**, and the
canons that get through are named after Vuza, who classified them in 1991. So
there is a rhythm nobody can write below 72 pulses, and above it you can hear
one.

## What I measured

Everything is `sketches/vuza/canon.ts`, run in node before anything made a sound.

**The sweep, and it is complete.** Every cyclic length from 2 to 60, every split
whose smaller half has at most six notes — which for all of them except two *is*
every split, because a factor pair of n ≤ 60 always has a side of six or fewer
unless n is 49 or 56. Subjects are counted up to translation:

| n | splits enumerated | subjects tried | aperiodic tiling subjects | **both halves aperiodic** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24 | 2×12 3×8 4×6 | 2,047 | 103 | **0** |
| 36 | 2×18 3×12 4×9 6×6 | 331,807 | 1,713 | **0** |
| 48 | 2×24 3×16 4×12 6×8 | 1,551,282 | 6,970 | **0** |
| 60 | 2×30 3×20 4×15 5×12 6×10 | 5,495,791 | 23,873 | **0** |
| **72** | 2×36 3×24 4×18 6×12 | 13,079,620 | 50,063 | **1,296** |

Totals for 2 → 60: **11,913,350 subjects, 56,722 aperiodic tiling subjects, and
0 Vuza canons**, in 74.5 s. The only splits anywhere in the sweep that were not
enumerated are 7×7 at 49, 7×8 at 56, and 8×9 at 72. The first two are orders
Hajós's own classification already settles — 49 = 7² and 56 = 2³·7, and both
p^a and p^a·q are always good — so the third is the one real gap, at
C(71,7) = 1.3 × 10⁹ subjects.

**And where they exist, they are vanishingly rare.** The 6 × 12 split at 72,
counted properly: **47,821** canonical subjects are aperiodic and tile, and of
those exactly **3** have an aperiodic partner —

```
{0,2,10,18,56,64}   432 aperiodic entry sets = 6 up to translation
{0,2,16,18,34,56}   432 aperiodic entry sets = 6 up to translation
{0,8,18,26,40,58}   432 aperiodic entry sets = 6 up to translation
```

Three in forty-eight thousand, one in sixteen thousand. The property is not
generic even at the length that first permits it. (The first of those, shifted
by 16, is `{0,8,16,18,26,34}`, the subject the sketch plays.)

**A subject can have a great deal of repeated structure and not be periodic,
and the difference is the whole point.** `{0,8,16,18,26,34}` is
`{0,8,16} ⊕ {0,18}`, so **48 of the 72 pulses keep their owner under a shift of
8** — and yet no shift maps the set to itself. Having a repeat inside it is not
the same as being a repeat.

**From the sound, every pulse and every voice.** With the contour flat, each of
the twelve voices holds one pitch, so the k-th lowest pitch heard *is* voice k
and nothing about root, scale or panning enters the test. Onsets are placed on
the pulse grid by time, pitched by autocorrelation, and compared against
`ownership()` computed in node:

| Which half loops | seed | onsets | per cycle | pitches | matched | control | pulses spanned | doubled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| neither (Vuza) | 7 | 149 | 72.0 | 12 | **149/149** | 31/149 | 149 | **0** |
| neither (Vuza) | 13 | 149 | 71.9 | 12 | **149/149** | 31/149 | 149 | **0** |
| the subject loops | 7 | 149 | 72.0 | 12 | **149/149** | 14/149 | 149 | **0** |
| the entries loop | 7 | 149 | 72.0 | 12 | **149/149** | 34/149 | 149 | **0** |

149 onsets over 149 consecutive pulses with nothing doubled is the tiling read
straight off the recording. The control is the sharpest one available — for a
Vuza row it is a *different* Vuza canon, same subject and same 6 × 12 split,
different entries — and it sits at chance.

**The aperiodicity itself, off the sound.** Take the heard pulse → voice map,
shift it by d, and ask whether renaming the voices makes it identical. This is
what "the canon repeats" means to a listener, and it needs no access to the sets:

| Which half loops | shifts that work, of 2 3 4 6 8 9 12 18 24 36 |
| --- | --- |
| neither (Vuza) | **none** |
| the subject loops | 2 3 4 6 8 9 12 18 24 36 |
| the entries loop | 6 12 18 24 36 |

Identical to the same table computed from the sets in node, and the Vuza row is
the claim: 72 pulses go by and no shift of any length brings the piece back onto
itself.

Levels: **0.492** at the defaults and **0.747** worst over thirteen settings.
`npm run smoke` reads 0.383 for the same sketch, because its meter samples an
analyser periodically rather than capturing every block — the capture number is
the one to trust, and both are inside the band.

## What went wrong

**A scale name that does not exist, and a silent sketch that measured
perfectly.** The default was `majorPentatonic`; the scale is called
`pentatonicMajor`. `degree()` threw on every mount, `setup` never finished, and
the page made no sound at all — while every table in node looked immaculate,
because none of them touch the sketch. `tsc` cannot catch it: a select's default
is declared as a plain string, so a typo in it is well-typed. The harness's
console capture found it in one run. **Typechecking a param default proves
nothing about the param.**

**An alignment bug shaped exactly like my hypothesis.** The first audio run
compared the heard voice sequence against the model *by position in the list*
and reported 35/140 for the Vuza canon against 129/140 for the periodic
controls. That is a tidy, publishable-looking result — the aperiodic canon is
harder to read back — and it is entirely an artefact. A single missed onset
shifts every later one, which a repeating sequence absorbs and an aperiodic one
cannot. Put the onsets on the pulse grid by *time* instead and all four go to
149/149. **A comparison that fails in precisely the way your hypothesis
predicts is not evidence for it.**

**The pitch detector had a peak that was an edge.** The lowest lag in the
autocorrelation search range is always a local maximum, because nothing is
computed below it — and for the lowest voice that false peak read as 1396 Hz,
five octaves up. Nine of thirteen notes of voice 0 were misattributed. The tell
was the pitch histogram: thirteen classes where there are twelve voices, one of
them with a partial count. Start the scan one lag in.

**And the first survey did 79 million pieces of pointless work.** At n = 48 it
enumerated every entry set of every subject including the periodic ones, which
cannot possibly be Vuza canons — 88 s where 4.6 s would do. Filter on the
property you are testing before the expensive step, not after.

## Next

- [ ] **The 8 × 9 split at 72.** 1.3 × 10⁹ subjects by brute force. Enumerating
      the two sides together, rather than filtering complete subjects, would cut
      it enough to close the one gap in the sweep.
- [ ] **108 and 120**, the next two non-Hajós orders, to check that the sweep's
      zeroes really do stop where the classification says. Both have splits below
      six notes, so most of each is reachable with what is already written.
- [ ] Vuza canons have **no notation**. Three subjects at 72, six entry sets
      each up to translation: that is eighteen objects and no name for any of
      them. A way to write one down is a compositional tool, not just a label.
- [ ] Nest one inside itself. `nest` recurses a two-voice split into a tree; a
      Vuza canon is a *finite* exact partition, and subdividing one voice of it
      by another canon is the same move on a different object.
- [ ] Fill the subject with a melody rather than one contour, so the twelve
      entries are a real canon and the interlocking is what keeps it from
      colliding with itself. This is the `tiling` follow-on that was never done.
- [ ] Sweep the *density*: 6 × 12 is one of five splits at 72, and the only one
      of the four enumerated that yields anything — 2 × 36, 3 × 24 and 4 × 18 all
      came back empty, and 8 × 9 is the split that was never searched, so
      "the only one" is not a claim I can make yet. Whether a listener could tell
      6 × 12 from 8 × 9 at all is a separate and equally unmeasured question.
- [ ] The honest gap: the sketch demonstrates that nothing repeats, and whether
      a listener *notices* is not tested. Same gap `veil` and `dichotic` left.
