# 2026-09-18 — nest: a hocket for eight voices, which is impossible

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/nest`, `src/core/theory.ts` (+ `hocket`, which
now imports from it)
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 5 voices, balanced, root split
0.618034, seed 3, E pentatonic minor from root 50. The demonstration is one
toggle: `Flatten`. Off, five voices share a pulse with every step carrying
exactly one note. On, the *same five densities* are played as plain Beatty
sequences and it falls apart into doubling and holes. Nothing else changes.

## What I tried

Sequencing and rhythm, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-13 with
`hocket`, which is also where this comes from.

That sketch established both halves of the Beatty story. Rayleigh: two voices at
densities d and 1−d hit every step exactly once — 0 collisions and 0 gaps over
200,000 steps. Uspensky (1927): **there is no such partition into three or
more**, and a 40-restart search confirmed it, reaching 0.0000 with two voices and
0.2400 with three.

The note it left: *"Beatty sequences nest — the steps a voice does not play are
themselves a Beatty sequence, so the split recurses. A hocket whose parameter is
a tree, sidestepping Uspensky entirely."*

Each node hands its stream of steps to two children by the rule that worked for
two. Every leaf is a voice. The densities stop being a list and become a tree.

## What I measured

**The counting function first**, because nesting is built on it: to index *into*
a Beatty sequence you need not just membership but how many members fall at or
below m. `beattyCount` against actually counting: **0 disagreement** over six
densities × 20,000 steps.

**It tiles exactly, for any number of voices.**

| voices | shape | depth | steps | unowned or doubled |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 | balanced | 1–3 | 200,000 | **0** |
| 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 | chain | 1–7 | 200,000 | **0** |

Ten configurations, zero defects. The leaf densities are the products down the
tree — worst departure **3.4e−6**, which is 1/M sampling — and they sum to
1.000000000000.

**And the comparison is the whole sketch.** Take the three densities a
three-leaf tree produces, and play them as *flat* Beatty sequences:

| | doubled | empty | defect rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| flat, three Beatty sequences | 65,765 | 65,766 | **0.6577** |
| the same densities, nested | 0 | 0 | **0.0000** |

**Uspensky is not violated, it is sidestepped — and the gaps prove it.** The
three-distance theorem gives a real Beatty rhythm exactly **two** distinct
inter-onset gaps:

| voices | distinct gaps per voice |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | 2, 2 |
| 3 | 3, 4, **2** |
| 4 | 3, 4, 4, 4 |
| 8 | 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8 |

A nested voice has more than two, so it is not a Beatty sequence and the theorem
does not reach it. The single voice in the three-leaf tree that still reads 2 is
the one split off at the root and never subdivided — genuinely a Beatty sequence,
and the only one.

**The tree's shape is a compositional parameter, not a detail.** Balanced and
chain with four voices both tile exactly and agree on who plays only **39.7%** of
steps, with different densities (0.256/0.362/0.158/0.224 against
0.618/0.158/0.082/0.141) and different gap fingerprints (3,4,4,4 against
2,4,8,8).

**From the sound.** Per-step energy off a recording, 238 steps:

| voices | steps carrying a note | empty | notes per step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 238 | 0 | 1.0000 |
| 3 | 238 | 0 | 1.0000 |
| 5 | 238 | 0 | 1.0000 |
| 8 | 238 | 0 | 1.0000 |

Eight voices sharing a pulse with no step doubled and no step empty, which for
flat Beatty sequences is impossible.

And what the sketch dispatches, against the model, over 3, 5 and 8 voices:
**0.0000 empty and 0.0000 doubled** nested every time; flattened at three voices,
**0.3250 / 0.3250** against a predicted 0.3286 / 0.3286.

Levels: 0.504 pre-limiter at the defaults.

## What went wrong

**The level clipped and the smoke gate did not catch it.** Nine notes overlap at
a 1.2 s decay with one note per step, and that measured **1.203** pre-limiter.
The suite passed throughout, because it plays every sketch at its defaults and
the defaults are fine — a param that stacks voices needs its worst case computed,
which is the same lesson `arc` and `foreshadow` are already in this file for.
Scaling the gain by decay/step fixed it at 0.504.

**I guessed a cause for the attribution error, tested it, and it got worse.**
Reading *which* voice plays each step is 95.38% right at three voices (with the
next-best alignment at 2.5%, so the fold is not in doubt) and falls to 74.79% at
five and 52.10% at eight. Each voice has an inharmonic partial at 2–3.7× its own
fundamental, and at eight voices four of those land within 3% of another voice's
fundamental — so I moved the analysis window to the back half of each step, where
the partials have decayed.

It went **down**: 84.87% at three voices and 44.96% at five. The partial-collision
story is dead, however plausible and however real the collisions are. The
remaining candidate is that a note rings for 0.13 s of a 0.139 s step and so
overlaps the next one, which I have not tested. I would rather leave it named and
untested than write a third story.

Worth saying what does not depend on it: the tiling claim is a count of notes per
step, not an attribution, and that is 238 of 238 four times over.

**And the control looked broken because my diagnostic was stale.** Flattening at
three voices read 0.0000 empty steps against a predicted 0.3286 — total
disagreement, where five voices agreed. The cause was neither: I read the
sketch's own dispatch ring 400 ms after flipping the toggle, which is about three
steps of a 4096-long buffer, so I was looking almost entirely at what it had
dispatched *before* the change. Waiting six seconds gives 0.3250 against 0.3286.
A ring buffer is a low-pass filter on your own experiment.

**A model module the harness imports cannot use the `@core` alias.** Promoting
`beatty` to core on its second use — which is the convention here — broke the
pattern of running a sketch's model directly in node, because node cannot resolve
a Vite alias. The fix is a relative path with the `.ts` extension, which both
Vite and node accept; the constraint is that a module meant to run outside the
app has to say where things actually are.

## Next

- [ ] Unbalanced trees by hand. `balanced` and `chain` are two points in a space
      of Catalan-many shapes, and the shape changes the rhythm completely while
      leaving the exactness alone. That is a compositional object with no
      existing notation.
- [ ] Let a node's density drift while the piece plays. Everything below it
      re-partitions continuously, so one slider re-voices a whole texture without
      ever breaking the tiling — which no mixer can do.
- [ ] The gap counts grow with depth (2 → 3,4 → 7,8). Predicting the count from
      the tree rather than measuring it should be a small piece of combinatorics,
      and it would say what the fingerprint of a given tree *is*.
- [ ] Nest in *pitch* rather than time: the same recursion over an interval
      instead of a pulse gives a chord whose notes partition an octave the way
      these partition a bar.
- [ ] `tiling` finds entry points that fill a cycle exactly and `hocket` fills an
      infinite one with two voices. This fills an infinite one with any number.
      All three are the same search at different levels of generality and have
      never been put side by side.
- [ ] Does the *ear* hear eight interlocking voices as eight, or as one stream?
      `hocket`'s two are clearly two. Eight at one note per step is a texture,
      and where the count stops being followable is the real question and is not
      answerable from inside the program.
