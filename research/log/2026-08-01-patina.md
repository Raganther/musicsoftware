# 2026-08-01 — patina: an instrument with an arrow of time

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/patina`
**Seeds worth keeping:** seed 31 is the reference instrument. Seed 31 with
Obsession 0.8, Recovery 0.35 is the demo — the tonic is destroyed inside
30 seconds and the melody is *forced* off it. Seed 31 with Recovery 0 is the
consumable version: about four minutes of playing and the instrument is gone.

## What I tried

First daily from **genuinely strange** — the only family in the rotation
never taken as the primary, and the brief asked for real risk. Took the
wildest unstruck line in ideas.md: *an instrument that gets slightly worse
the longer you play it.*

Every note you play wears **that pitch** out: it detunes, dulls, softens its
attack, loses level, and starts to rattle. Rested notes recover slowly. The
wear is per-pitch, so the instrument ends up shaped by what you played on it,
and the bar chart of wear is a portrait of the performance. Each seed is a
different physical instrument — seeded manufacturing defects (a few notes
weak from new) and a seeded direction of pitch drift.

This inverts the usual bargain. Normally practice makes an instrument yours;
here overuse spends it.

## What I measured

Isolated the effect — auto-player off, recovery off — then hammered one key
26 times and compared the same note fresh vs worn on the master bus:

| | fresh | worn (~72%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| spectral centroid | 1928 Hz | 1613 Hz (**−16.4%**) |
| peak level | 0.653 | 0.347 (**−47%**) |

Auto-play peak 0.55 pre-limiter. Whole suite green (10 sketches + jam).

## The failure worth keeping

The first version measured a centroid drop of only **7.6%** — the note got
quieter and slower to speak but barely darker, which is not what "worn out"
should sound like. Cause: **two of the four wear channels were cancelling
each other.** The rattle was a bandpass burst at 1.2–3.8 kHz, so as a note
got more worn the rattle *added* high-frequency energy, precisely undoing the
filter's dulling. Moving the rattle down to 240–860 Hz — a tired key should
clunk, not hiss — more than doubled the measured drop to 16.4%.

Generalising: piling on degradation parameters is not the same as producing
audible degradation. Channels that all "mean" wear can still fight each other
in the output, and only measurement catches it. This is the third day running
that the bug has been *state that never properly reached the output* —
Watershed's invisible sediment, Cat's Cradle's stutter, and now this.

## What surprised me

- **The constraint encodes ordinary good advice.** Playing across the range
  keeps everything fresh, so an instrument invented to be perverse ends up
  rewarding wide melodic writing — exactly what teachers push. I did not
  design that in.
- **Degradation is only musical when it's uneven.** Obsession low spreads the
  wear thin and nothing happens; Obsession high concentrates it and you hear
  the piece being forced off its home note. Uniform decay is just a filter
  sweep.
- **Temper feels bad to press.** It's the only undo, and using it deletes the
  instrument's memory of the performance. A button whose cost is felt is a
  more interesting design object than one whose cost is stated.
- The overall condition readout stays high (93%) while the tonic reads 100%
  worn — the average hides the thing that matters, which is itself the point.

## Next

      was played, or reload one to start on someone else's used instrument.
- [ ] Sympathetic wear: playing C3 should tire C4 a little, the way a real
      mechanism shares fatigue. Would make the map smoother and more organic.
- [ ] A "break-in" phase where light early use *improves* a note before it
      degrades — real instruments get better before they get worse, and that
      curve would make the arrow of time far more interesting than monotonic
      decline.
