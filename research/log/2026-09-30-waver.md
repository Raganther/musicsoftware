# 2026-09-30 — waver: a rail that corrects in time, and the one thing that buys

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/waver`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — settle 60 ms, strength 1, vibrato 0.3
semitones at 5.5 Hz, slow error 0.35, seed 7, root 55 major. Turn `Settle` to 0
and the vibrato goes with the mistuning; turn it to 600 and neither is touched.
`Hold one note` with vibrato past 0.5 semitones is the warble.

## What I tried

Improvisation and interaction, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-24
with `elbows`. The idea was left in `research/ideas.md` by `guardrail`:

> A rail with a *time constant* rather than an instantaneous one: vibrato
> survives auto-tune only if the correction is slow, so the measurement becomes
> one of modulation depth rather than static pitch.

`guardrail` measured the safety/expression trade along the rail's **strength**
and found a cliff — 61 distinguishable notes an octave down to 8, in-tune
fraction 13% up to 100%. A strong rail is in tune and says nothing. That axis
cannot separate expression from error, because it cannot tell a wobble from
being flat: both are just distance from a degree.

They differ in **frequency**. Being flat is slow, vibrato is fast. So lag the
correction rather than weakening it.

## What I measured

Everything is `sketches/waver/rail.ts`, run in node before anything sounded.

**The strength axis cannot separate them, and the number is 1.00.** With the
excursion kept inside one degree, so the instant rail can actually null it:

| strength | 0 | 0.25 | 0.50 | 0.75 | 1.00 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vibrato kept | 0.9830 | 0.7372 | 0.4915 | 0.2457 | **0.0000** |
| slow error kept | 0.9911 | 0.7434 | 0.4956 | 0.2478 | **0.0000** |

Identical to three figures at every setting the knob has. That is not a poor
result for `guardrail`, it is the exact statement of what a gain can do.

**Lagging the correction is a one-pole high-pass on pitch error**, and it
matches the exact discrete transfer function:

| τ (s) | 0.5 Hz | 2 Hz | 6 Hz |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.02 | 0.058871 | 0.228830 | 0.565190 |
| 0.08 | 0.239958 | 0.697957 | 0.934430 |
| 0.30 | 0.683013 | 0.962551 | 0.991969 |
| 1.00 | 0.951700 | 0.995603 | 0.998399 |

Measured over predicted: **1.0000 on 11 of 12**, the exception 0.9996.

**And that is what it buys** — a 6 Hz vibrato against a 0.2 Hz drift:

| τ (s) | 0.01 | 0.05 | 0.15 | 0.40 | 1.00 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vibrato kept | 0.311 | **0.862** | 0.977 | 0.995 | 0.998 |
| drift kept | 0.011 | **0.061** | 0.184 | 0.448 | 0.782 |
| ratio | 28.1 | **14.1** | 5.3 | 2.2 | 1.3 |

At 50 ms the rail removes 94% of the drift and 14% of the vibrato. The strength
axis reaches a ratio of **1.00** at every point it has, so this is not a better
setting of the same trade — it is a different trade.

**The knee is exactly half a snap step**, which is where `snap` starts jumping
and the system stops being a filter at all. At a 15 ms rail, chromatic:

| depth, in half-steps | 0.20 | 0.60 | 0.90 | **1.00** | **1.10** | 1.60 | 3.00 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vibrato kept | 0.4524 | 0.4524 | 0.4524 | 0.4535 | **0.9634** | 1.1919 | 0.8555 |
| warble | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0135 | **0.1504** | 0.0193 | 0.0102 |

Under the knee the linear answer holds to four decimals and the rail flattens
55% of the wobble. Over it, **the vibrato escapes the rail entirely** — 0.96
kept — and takes a warble with it. A rail that is fast enough to flatten a
subtle vibrato lets a wide one straight through, and the boundary is sharp.

**The warble belongs to the fast rail, not to the crossing.** At a fixed depth
of 1.8 half-steps, swept over τ:

| τ (s) | 0 | 0.005 | 0.015 | 0.04 | 0.1 | 0.3 | 1.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| warble | **0.1011** | 0.0427 | 0.0099 | 0.0019 | 0.0004 | **0.0000** | **0.0000** |

A slow rail barely corrects at 6 Hz, so it has nothing to warble with. At τ = 0
the quantised sine's own fundamental reads **1.18×** the input's — a staircase
is louder at the fundamental than the wave it came from, which is the 4/π of a
square wave showing through.

**Off the sound**, tracking the pitch of a held note:

| settle | vibrato heard | predicted | ratio | slow error heard | predicted | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 ms | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | both 0 | 0.0001 | 0.0000 | both 0 |
| 15 ms | 0.4221 | 0.4229 | **0.998** | 0.0164 | 0.0173 | 0.948 |
| 60 ms | 0.8804 | 0.8821 | **0.998** | 0.0711 | 0.0736 | 0.965 |
| 150 ms | 0.9717 | 0.9737 | **0.998** | 0.1816 | 0.1837 | 0.989 |
| 400 ms | 0.9925 | 0.9943 | **0.998** | 0.4364 | 0.4477 | 0.975 |

The same 0.998 at every time constant — a consistent 0.2% bias rather than
scatter, which is a residual in the tracker rather than a disagreement. And the
strength axis read from audio gives **0.984** between the two at strength 0 and
again at 0.5, exactly as the model says.

Levels: **0.510** at the defaults, **0.718** worst over ten settings.

## What went wrong

**I swept the nonlinearity at the setting where it cannot happen.** The first
boundary-crossing table held τ at 150 ms and varied the depth, and found ratios
of 1.00 and a warble of 0.002 at every depth — which reads as "crossing a snap
boundary does nothing". It does nothing *there*, because a 150 ms rail is only
correcting 2% at 6 Hz, so there is almost no correction to distort. The warble
lives at small τ, and sweeping the wrong axis made a real effect invisible.

**And the measure of it never reached zero**, floored at 0.065, because
`distortion` counts everything that is not at the vibrato rate — including the
drift term I had put in the signal. Measuring on a drift-free line took it to
exactly 0.0000. A distortion measure needs to be given a signal whose only
intended content is the thing it is measuring around.

**My first comparison against `guardrail` flattered it.** With the excursion
crossing snap boundaries, the instant rail at full strength read 0.5572 of the
vibrato "kept" — which is not preserved expression, it is the quantisation
staircase's own fundamental. Keeping the excursion inside one degree gives
0.0000 and 0.0000, which is the true and much starker statement.

**The instrument that measures a modulation has its own response to it.** The
pitch tracker's analysis window is a low-pass on the pitch track: a rectangular
window of 23 ms reads a 5.5 Hz modulation at sinc(π·f·T) = 97.3% of its true
depth. Left in, that is a uniform 2.7% shortfall against the model at every
setting — exactly the shape of a real disagreement. Dividing out the known
window response is the fix, and the residual then sits at 0.2%.

**Double-escaped backticks, twice in two days.** The `notes` field is a template
literal, and writing `\\\`` where `\`` is wanted produces a backslash followed by
a string terminator. `tsc` catches it immediately, which is the cheap part; the
expensive part is that I did the same thing yesterday.

## Next

- [ ] **A rail with two time constants** — fast for the attack, slow for the
      sustain. A singer's scoop into a note is a fast pitch move that should be
      left alone and a fast rail currently flattens it; the glide in this sketch
      already survives for that reason and is not measured.
- [ ] **Per-degree strength**, which `guardrail` also left open: pull hard to the
      tonic and barely at all to the seventh. That is a third axis, and whether
      it separates expression from error the way time does is a real question.
- [ ] The knee is at half a snap step, so a **wider scale moves it**: on the
      pentatonic the knee should sit at a whole semitone. Measured here only
      chromatically, and the pentatonic run I did was contaminated by a
      `tol`-based in-tune measure that was reading the input's own excursion.
- [ ] Correct toward the *predicted* pitch rather than the current one. `drag`
      and `afteryou` both want anticipation for timing, and pitch has the same
      structure: a rail that knows where a scoop is heading need not fight it.
- [ ] Measure a real voice rather than a synthesised wobble. Everything here is
      a sine on a sine, which is the right way to test a filter and the wrong
      way to learn what a rail does to singing.
- [ ] The warble is currently an artefact to avoid. It is also the sound of a
      whole genre, and a rail tuned to produce it deliberately — depth just over
      the knee, τ just under — is a instrument rather than a fault.
