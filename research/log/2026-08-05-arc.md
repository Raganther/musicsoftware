# 2026-08-05 — arc: the score as a shape

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/arc`
**Seeds worth keeping:** seed 4 with the "arch" preset is the reference — the
shape of most written music, applied embarrassingly literally. Seed 4 with
"steps" is the most useful for hearing the harmonic ladder as discrete rungs.

## What I tried

Composition tools, the stalest family. The idea from ideas.md I had not
touched: *a score that renders as a diagram of tension rather than notation.*

You draw one curve — tension across the length of the piece — and everything
else derives from it: which voicing the harmony reaches for (a ladder of
eight, open fifth up to semitone cluster), how far apart the voices sit, how
dense the texture is, how hard it is played. Composers think in these arcs
long before they think in notes; this makes the arc the actual document.

## What I measured

"The music follows the curve" is exactly the claim a generative tool gets to
make for free, so I tested it from audio, independently of the sketch's own
bookkeeping. Correlating audio features against the drawn curve across one
full 20.9s pass of the form:

| curve | feature | r |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ramp | RMS | 0.75 |
| ramp | spectral centroid | **0.85** |
| fall | RMS | 0.80 |
| arch | RMS | 0.82 |

Cross-check: the ramp's output correlates **−0.75** against a *falling*
target, which rules out the correlation being an artefact of the measure.
Peak 0.80 pre-limiter. Suite green: 14 sketches + jam.

**Brightness tracks tension better than loudness does** (0.85 vs 0.75).
Velocity-to-filter is carrying more of the arc than velocity-to-amplitude,
and by ear that is the more convincing of the two — the piece getting
brighter reads as rising tension more reliably than it getting louder.

## What the picture showed that the numbers did not

The sketch draws a second line: tension measured back out of the notes
actually played. Against a smooth arch, that line tracks the shape broadly
but in visible **steps**. The ladder has eight rungs, so the tool cannot
realise a continuous curve — it realises eight levels, and the smooth line
you drew is a polite fiction. Not fatal (eight gradations is more than most
tonal music uses), but drawing a subtle 0.42-to-0.48 swell changes literally
nothing. Interpolating voicings between rungs is the obvious fix.

I would not have found that from the correlation figures, which are perfectly
healthy. The r values say "it follows the curve"; the picture says "in eight
discrete jumps".

## The recurring failure, sixth day

I again wrote the notes before measuring, and this time the invented claim
was qualitative rather than numeric: that drawn and measured diverge at the
extremes, sitting *above* target near 0 and *below* near 1, with specific
reasoning for each. It was plausible, it was detailed, and I had tested none
of it. Replaced with the correlations above plus the quantisation finding —
both of which are more interesting than the thing I made up.

Sixth consecutive day. The invention is not random: it always takes the shape
of a *specific, mechanistic-sounding explanation*, which is the exact register
that reads as credible. Writing the notes last is not enough of a fix,
because the pull is to write them while the design reasoning is fresh. The
working rule I have landed on is narrower: any sentence containing a number
or a directional claim gets a `TODO:measure` marker until a harness has
produced it.
