# 2026-09-08 — sitting: Lucier, and two windows that lied

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/sitting`
**Also:** a correction to `tongues`' published fractal dimension — see the end.
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — room 1.4 s, 3 resonances, ring 0.05 s,
diffuse 0.12, phrase 12, seed 3. That room's best frequency is 203.9 Hz and the
process arrives there by about generation 20. The demonstration is to leave it
running from generation 0 and not touch anything. For the other half of the
point, set `Resonances` to 0: a room with no modes at all still converges,
because noise still has a maximum.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-03 with `larsen`.

*I Am Sitting in a Room* is a loop with one instruction: play the recording into
the room, record that, repeat. What it actually does is a multiplication.
Playing a signal through a room multiplies its spectrum by |H(f)|, so generation
N is |X₀(f)|·|H(f)|^N and the ratio between any two frequencies has been raised
to the Nth power. There is no filtering and no averaging; there is a room,
applied over and over, and an exponential does the rest.

That framing makes a prediction the piece itself does not suggest. Near its top
a resonance is locally quadratic, so |H|^N goes as exp(−N·a·δ²): a Gaussian
whose width falls as **N^(−1/2)**. A Lorentzian peak gives the same exponent by
a different route, so −1/2 is a statement about repetition rather than about any
particular resonance.

Each generation is rendered rather than faked — the previous buffer convolved
with the impulse response through an `OfflineAudioContext`, truncated to the
same length a tape of fixed length would give, and normalised the way his gain
knob was.

## What I measured

**It is exactly the multiplication.** Testing |X_N| = c·|H|^N·|X₀| directly
underflows — |H|^40 at a bin 20 dB down is 1e−40, so the "error" becomes the
distance to whatever floor the arithmetic clamps at, which is how the first run
reported a median error of 180 dB. The ratio between *adjacent* generations has
no exponent in it: X_N/X_{N−1} should be |H| times a scalar, so
log(ratio) − log|H| should be one constant across the band and its spread is
the thing to report.

| N | bins compared | spread | median | 90th pct |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 6441 | 2.18 dB | 0.46 dB | 2.66 dB |
| 2 | 3631 | 0.66 dB | 0.13 dB | 0.84 dB |
| 4 | 2464 | **0.23 dB** | 0.07 dB | 0.26 dB |
| 8 | 1836 | 0.20 dB | 0.06 dB | 0.23 dB |
| 16 | 911 | 0.22 dB | 0.07 dB | 0.29 dB |
| 24 | 637 | 0.28 dB | 0.08 dB | 0.31 dB |
| 32 | 434 | 0.23 dB | 0.11 dB | 0.29 dB |
| 40 | 340 | 0.18 dB | 0.09 dB | 0.26 dB |

with |H| measured by transforming the impulse response, not taken from the
numbers the room was built with. Generation 1 is the worst because the voice has
near-zeros where the ratio is ill-conditioned; by generation 4 the signal only
lives where the room is strong and the agreement is a fifth of a decibel.

**It ends exactly where the room says.** Generation 40 peaks at **203.9 Hz** and
the room's transfer function peaks at **203.9 Hz**. That is the claim the piece
makes — the final pitch is a measurement of the room and of nothing in the voice
— and it is checkable rather than poetic.

**What survives narrows as N^(−1/2).** With a single resonance, so nothing
competes, the envelope width of the surviving peak measured off the rendered
audio fits an exponent of **−0.541** (R² 0.906) against the predicted −0.500;
at a smoothing kernel of 1 Hz rather than 2 it gives −0.530. On |H|^N alone,
with no source spectrum and no finite recording in the way, **−0.537**
(R² 0.991) for a wide mode and **−0.490** (R² 0.994) for a narrow one.

**A recording of length T cannot hold a line narrower than about 1/T**, and that
is a hard limit on the whole process rather than on the analysis. At 2.6 seconds
it is 0.4 Hz. With the resonance ring at a second the room's modes are already
2 Hz wide, the narrowing reaches the floor by generation 6, and every generation
after that does nothing — which is a real statement about the piece, and one
reason Lucier's recordings are as long as they are.

**And the winner is not always a mode you put there.** The largest of a hundred
thousand noise bins is about five standard deviations, so a resonance has to
beat an extreme value rather than an average. With the diffuse tail loud, the
process ends on a spike nobody designed, and the N^(−1/2) fit degrades at the
same time (−0.863, R² 0.932) because what it is tracking is no longer a
resonance.

Levels: 0.584 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

Two window choices, both wrong, and between them they inverted the answer.

**A Hann window is zero at t = 0, which is where an impulse response keeps all
its energy.** My spectrum function windowed everything it was given, so
transforming the room's impulse response deleted every short-ringing mode — a
mode that decays in 40 ms sits entirely inside the part of the window that is
still near zero — while the diffuse tail, spread across the whole response,
came through untouched. The room therefore reported its loudest frequency as a
14 kHz noise spike, three octaves above any mode I had put in and in a band
where the source has no energy at all. Every downstream conclusion followed from
that: the modes looked irrelevant, the noise looked dominant, and "which
frequency wins" looked like a question about noise. An impulse response already
decays to nothing and needs no window at all, only a short fade at the end. With
that one change the room's peak moved to 203.9 Hz and the whole picture
resolved.

What caught it was the tell I wrote down two days ago on `tongues`: three rooms
built with deliberately different resonances returned byte-identical numbers.
The first explanation I reached for — that my parameter setter was not taking
effect — was wrong, and checking it properly is what exposed the real cause.
(The setter *was* also broken in the harness, separately: `window.__sitting()`
snapshots the impulse response, so the handle has to be re-fetched after a
change. Two bugs producing the same symptom, and fixing the first one did not
move the number, which is what said there was a second.)

**And a 2.6 second recording has spectral detail at 0.4 Hz everywhere.** The
−3 dB width of a raw argmax therefore measures that fine structure and comes out
at about one bin no matter what the room did — my first width series read 0.68,
0.63, 0.65, 0.67 Hz across four generations, four numbers that are all the
analysis floor. What |H|^N shapes is the *envelope*: multiplying by a resonance
reweights the envelope and leaves the fine structure exactly where it was. The
fix is to smooth over a band well above 1/T and well below the resonance, and to
report the fit at more than one kernel width so the reader can see the kernel is
not doing the work.

**The dynamic range has to be checked before the exponent is believed.** Even
after both fixes, the first configuration had modes 2.7 Hz wide against a
0.58 Hz floor — barely a factor of four to watch a power law in, and the
smoothing kernel ate most of that. The exponent came out −0.28 and the answer
was not "the law is wrong" but "there is nothing here to measure". A law over
one octave of range is not a measurement.

## The `tongues` dimension, corrected

Yesterday I published a box dimension of **0.875** for the critical circle map's
unlocked set, against the literature's 0.870, and explained the 0.6% overshoot
as unresolvable plateaus inflating the count at the finest box size. That
predicted the estimate would fall toward 0.870 on a finer grid, so I ran it.

It does fall — 0.8747 at 8,193 points, 0.8724 at 16,385, 0.8629 at 32,769 —
but **the control collapsed to 0.572** at the finest grid, which means the
estimator was broken there and the trend proves nothing. The fault is real and
was in yesterday's method: I used one fixed tolerance for every box size. In an
unlocked region the winding number changes by about the box size across a box,
so a fixed tolerance means something different at every scale, and on a fine
enough grid it exceeds the box size and calls everything locked.

With the tolerance scaled to the box — tol = ε/frac, which makes the test
scale-free — the answer is **0.8794** at K = 1, identical to four decimals at
frac 4, 8 and 16, R² 0.9999, with controls at **0.9853** (K = 0.5) and
**0.9603** (K = 0.8).

So the corrected figure is 0.879 rather than 0.875, slightly *further* from
0.870, and the honest reading is that the controls miss their known value of 1
by 1.5 to 4%, so a 1% gap at K = 1 sits inside the method's own demonstrated
bias. Yesterday's "0.6% high, and here is the mechanism" was over-precise about
a residual smaller than the error bar I had not established. The number that
should have been published is: 0.879, consistent with 0.870 to within a method
bias of a few percent.
