# 2026-08-25 — mallet: the excitation is the instrument

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/mallet`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — p = 1.5, 24 N at 250 µm, 8 g head,
undercut 0.75, root 55, seed 7. The demonstration is `Mallet hardness`: set it
to 1, play for a while, then take it to 3 without touching anything else. The
pitch does not move, the level barely moves, and the instrument changes from a
wooden thud to a struck-metal ping. Undercut 0 is a glockenspiel clang, 1 is a
marimba pitch, same bar and same mallet.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-19, `bloom`).

Every physical model in this repo puts its effort into the resonator and hits
it with a canned impulse — `bloom` included, six days ago. That gets the pitch
right and the dynamics wrong, because a bar struck harder is not the same sound
louder. So: invert the emphasis. The most ordinary resonator I could justify (a
free-free beam, mode ratios straight out of cos x · cosh x = 1) and a properly
integrated Hertzian contact driving it.

A mallet is a nonlinear spring. Two curved elastic bodies obey F = k·c^p with
p = 3/2, and for any p ≠ 1 the contact time depends on arrival speed:

    t_contact ∝ v^((1-p)/(1+p))

which is v^(-1/5) at p = 3/2. Hit it harder, the mallet is in contact for
*less* time, and a shorter contact reaches higher modes. The reason this is
worth building rather than asserting is the case p = 1: the exponent is exactly
zero, and more than that, the whole coupled system — mass, linear spring,
linear modes — is linear, so the spectrum *cannot* move with velocity. It is a
control that the physics guarantees, not one I have to argue for.

Coupling is Chaigne–Askenfelt: bar surface under the mallet is the sum of modes
weighted by their shapes there, compression is mallet-minus-surface, and the
contact force drives the mallet backwards and every mode forwards through that
same shape. Nothing anywhere is an impulse.

## What I measured

**The bar is the beam.** Mode ratios against eigenvalues recomputed in the
harness rather than imported: 1.000, 2.757, 5.404, 8.933, 13.344, 18.638,
**0.000% disagreement**.

**The integrator matches the algebra**, contact time in ms:

| p | 0.3 | 0.6 | 1.2 | 2.4 | 4.8 m/s | fitted | predicted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.00 | 0.907 | 0.907 | 0.907 | 0.907 | 0.907 | **0.000** | 0.000 |
| 1.50 | 1.156 | 0.998 | 0.884 | 0.748 | 0.658 | **−0.204** | −0.200 |
| 2.00 | 1.338 | 1.043 | 0.839 | 0.658 | 0.522 | **−0.338** | −0.333 |
| 3.00 | 1.542 | 1.088 | 0.771 | 0.544 | 0.385 | **−0.500** | −0.500 |

The p = 1 row is not approximately flat, it is *identical* to three decimals at
every velocity, which is the correct answer for a mass on a linear spring.

**And it reaches the ear.** Mode 4 relative to mode 1, from the audio:

| p | 0.3 | 0.6 | 1.2 | 2.4 | 4.8 | slope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.00 | .0155 | .0152 | .0153 | .0152 | .0152 | **−0.005** |
| 1.50 | .0019 | .0064 | .0053 | .0079 | .0196 | 0.699 |
| 2.00 | .0017 | .0032 | .0037 | .0026 | .0495 | 0.935 |
| 3.00 | .0002 | .0020 | .0195 | .1207 | .2677 | 2.641 |

The control is flat to 1% across a 16× velocity range while everything else
climbs. Both survive a 4× level change unchanged (p = 1: −0.005 and −0.005;
p = 2: 0.927 and 0.932), so nothing nonlinear downstream is manufacturing the
trend. The middle rows are noisy — p = 1.5 and p = 2 are not monotonic, r 0.92
and 0.77 — because their mode-4 content sits an order of magnitude closer to
the floor than p = 1's does. The trend is real; the individual cells are not
worth quoting.

One cross-check I did not plan and like: at v = 4.8 the p = 1.5 mallet is
*brighter* than the p = 1 mallet (.0196 vs .0152) and at v = 0.3 it is far
duller (.0019 vs .0155). That is the mechanism stated backwards — a nonlinear
head is longer in contact than a linear one when you play softly and shorter
when you play hard — and it fell out of the same table.

Peak 0.548 pre-limiter over 14 s of defaults, 0.383 over the smoke suite's
shorter window; the strike force is randomised, so how loud it gets depends on
how long you listen. Green: 31 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## Three failures, and the second one is the interesting one

**Silence.** The model computed correctly and output nothing, because modal
displacement for a real bar is tens of nanometres and the contact law forces
the whole thing to work in metres. Obvious in hindsight, invisible while
writing it.

**A control that was not a control.** Fixing the silence with a scale factor,
I measured brightness and got slopes of −0.518, 0.130, 1.288, −1.169 — no
order at all, and the p = 1 control failing hardest. Two separate faults
underneath, and I would not have found either without the control being *known*
to be zero:

*k has units of N/m^p.* The same numeric k is a completely different mallet at
p = 1 and p = 3 — with c ≈ 3·10⁻⁴ m, c³ is eight orders of magnitude below c.
Measuring the un-scaled modal peak showed the model getting **100× quieter**
from p = 1 to p = 3, so `hardness` was mostly a volume control and the control
row was comparing two different instruments. Stiffness is now given as the
force at 250 µm compression, k = F_ref / c_ref^p, which is how you would
actually measure a mallet head; amplitude across the whole p sweep is now
within 1.6×, and `hardness` changes the shape of the force curve and nothing
else.

*The sketch had a tanh on its output and it was fully saturating.* Every row
reported peak 0.800 — exactly `master.gain` — which is the same tell `bloom`
gave six days ago and which I still did not read on sight. A saturator
generates its own harmonics, so every "brightness" number was a measurement of
the tanh. It is gone: a sketch has no business soft-clipping in front of the
master limiter, and all that is left is a runaway clamp at ±2 that never
engages. The worklet now reports the peak it drives the output to, so a future
run that gets loud enough to matter has to say so.

**A measurement window that was mostly the previous note.** After both fixes
p = 1 still sloped at 0.26, driven entirely by one velocity. The window was
16384 samples (341 ms) grabbed 120 ms after the strike — so two thirds of every
window was the *previous*, quieter strike still ringing. Shortened to 171 ms,
grabbed at 200 ms, with 2.5 s between strikes.

That left one outlier, and rather than argue about it I ran the velocities
**backwards**. The odd reading followed the position in the sequence and never
the velocity: first-ascending (v = 0.3) read 0.0093, first-descending
(v = 4.8) read 0.0178, and five identical strikes read 0.0331 then 0.0163,
0.0143, 0.0156, 0.0143. The first strike on a fresh page is junk and everything
after it is stable to ±5%. Discard one, take a median of three, and the control
lands at −0.005.

## The recurring failure, day twenty-four

Not invention. But the shape of today is worth naming: **the control did all
the work, and it did it three times.** Every one of these three faults produced
a plausible-looking table. Without a row whose answer was fixed in advance by
theory at exactly zero, I would have shipped "brightness rises with hardness,
slope 1.29 at p = 2" on the first run and it would have been a measurement of a
tanh. The known-answer channel has now paid off six days running, and today is
the first time it caught a bug in the *instrument* rather than in the detector.

The order test deserves its own line. When a single point disagrees, permuting
the order of the measurements costs one run and separates physics from harness
completely — there is no argument left afterwards. I have wanted that on at
least three previous days and improvised something worse each time.
