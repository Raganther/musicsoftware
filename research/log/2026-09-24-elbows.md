# 2026-09-24 — elbows: an ensemble that shares a bar out without counting itself

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/elbows`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 8 players, `midpoint`, correction
0.8, start spread 0.15, seed 5. The demonstration is `Start spread` at 0: eight
players all on the downbeat, one flam, and about twenty bars later a perfectly
even pulse. Three players at correction 0.667 land in a *single* bar. Push
`Correction` past 1 and it comes apart, differently depending on whether the
number of players is odd or even.

## What I tried

Improvisation and interaction, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-19 with
`afteryou`. The idea was sitting in `research/ideas.md` as a complaint rather
than a proposal, left over from `entrain`:

> **Repulsive coupling gives two camps, not an even spread.** Predicted a splay,
> measured clustering at half a beat: "avoid whoever you can hear" is satisfied
> by anti-phase, and an even ring requires knowing how many players there are,
> which no player does. Worth a rule that does know.

There is a rule, and the interesting part is that it does *not* need to know.
Listen only to whoever played immediately **before** you and immediately
**after** you, and move to the middle of the gap they left. It is Degesys and
Nagpal's DESYNC (2007), written for radios sharing a channel, and as music it is
a hocket that assembles itself: no parts, no conductor, nobody counting.

## What I measured

Everything is `sketches/elbows/ring.ts`, run in node before anything sounded.

**The error is the discrete heat equation on a ring.** Writing each player's
departure from their fair share as `e`, the update is
`e ← (1−α)e + α(e₋ + e₊)/2` — a circulant with eigenvalues
`(1−α) + α·cos(2πk/n)`. Mode 0 is the ensemble's mean phase and sits at exactly
1, so **the group never drifts while its members rearrange**.

**Decay per bar, against the largest eigenvalue that is not mode 0:**

| n | α = 0.4 | α = 0.8 | α = 0.95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 0.723607 | 0.447214 | 0.718566 |
| 8 | 0.882843 | 0.765685 | 0.900000 |
| 12 | 0.946410 | 0.892820 | 0.900000 |
| 16 | 0.969552 | 0.939104 | 0.927686 |

Measured/predicted was **1.0000 on 20 of 21** runs across seven ensemble sizes,
the exception 0.9997. In every one the phase sum drifted by at most **4e−15**
and **no two players ever swapped places** — two integers that had to come out
that way, and did.

**The obvious statistic cannot see the difference, and that is the trap.** Over
twelve seeds at eight players, 300 bars, α = 0.8:

| rule | how unevenly the bar ends up shared | order parameter | swaps |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| midpoint | **1.8e−16** (worst 4.6e−16) | 0.000000 | 0 |
| repel all | **0.468** (worst 0.474) | 0.000000 | 0 |
| repel nearest | 1.30 | 0.631 | 10 |

The Kuramoto order parameter — how *bunched* the ensemble is — reads **exactly
zero for both** of the first two. It is the natural thing to measure and it
cannot tell a perfect round-robin from a lopsided ring. What `repel all` settles
into, as multiples of the ideal gap:

```
0.480 0.773 1.080 1.309 1.066 0.767 0.507 2.017
```

One gap twice the fair share and another half of it, and a statistic that says
this is perfectly spread out. Only the gaps know. (`entrain` called this "two
camps"; at eight players it is not two camps, it is a ring with a hole in it.)

**At exactly α = 1 the parity of the ensemble decides.** The shortest-wavelength
mode — every other player early, then late — has eigenvalue `1 − 2α`, which at
α = 1 is −1: it flips sign each bar and never shrinks. But that mode only exists
when n is even. Measured over 200 bars at α = 1:

| n | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ends at | 3e−16 | **0.646** | 2e−16 | **0.643** | 8e−10 | **0.644** | 4e−6 | **0.640** | 2e−4 |

Nine of nine. **An even ensemble pushing as hard as it can never settles, and an
odd one does.** With no swaps in either case: it is not chaos, it is two
arrangements alternating forever.

**The fastest correction depends on n, which is the one thing nobody knows.**
Balancing the longest wavelength against the shortest gives
`α* = 2/(2 − cos(2π/n) − cos(2π⌊n/2⌋/n))`. Swept finely against a measured
optimum over ten sizes, the ratio ran **0.9963 → 1.0020**. At three players
α* = 2/3 drives *every* non-zero eigenvalue to exactly 0, and they find their
places **in one bar**.

So: an ensemble can share a bar out perfectly without counting itself, and
cannot tune how fast it does so without counting itself. The rule is free of n;
the best gain is not.

**And off the sound, with no pitch detection at all.** n players firing once
each span exactly one bar, so n consecutive onsets *are* the ring gaps. Thirty
bars, eight players, against the same model re-run in node with the same seed
and the same `rng`:

| rule | onsets per bar | bars read | heard, first → last | model, first → last | log rms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| midpoint | 8.07 | 29/30 | 0.452 → **5.9e−3** | 0.548 → 3.4e−4 | 0.041 |
| repel all | 8.07 | 29/30 | 0.703 → **0.418** | 0.673 → 0.414 | 0.006 |
| none | 8.13 | 30/30 | 1.064 → 1.06 | 1.060 → 1.06 | 0.002 |

The floor the recording reaches is 5.9e−3 of an ideal gap — **0.79 ms** of
timing. The two arrangements that stand still agree with the model to 0.006 and
0.002; the one that is *moving* agrees to 0.041, and that residual has a cause:
a one-bar window straddles a bar line, so it mixes the arrangement before the
update with the one after. The static cases have nothing to mix.

Levels: **0.445** over the first bars, **0.496** once settled, **0.745** worst
over fourteen settings.

## What went wrong

**I assumed the slowest mode was the longest one, and for a while it was.** The
prediction I wrote down was `1 − α(1 − cos(2π/n))`, the k = 1 eigenvalue, and it
matched everywhere I looked until four players at α = 0.8 came in **3.00×** out.
The shortest-wavelength mode has eigenvalue `1 − 2α`, which for few players and
a strong correction outlives the long one — 0.6 against the 0.2 I predicted, and
the ensemble obeys the 0.6. The fix is to take the largest of all of them rather
than the one I expected to matter. Every subsequent result — the parity
business, α\*, the whole shape of the sketch — came out of that being wrong.

**The fourth hand-rolled onset detector in this repo, and the fourth to be wrong
first time.** Mine took a peak-per-hop envelope at 32 samples, which is shorter
than one cycle of a 131 Hz tone, so every cycle of the waveform looked like a
fresh rise: it found **34 onsets a bar where there are 8**. `research/ideas.md`
has asked for a shared spectral-flux detector since August and this is the
strongest argument yet for writing one.

**Then I indexed windows by position in the onset list — the same bug as
yesterday, in a new costume.** One extra onset shifts every window after it, and
the midpoint trace grew spikes at bars 9 and 14 where the model decays smoothly.
Validating each window by its *span* instead fixes it and does something better:
n players firing once each span exactly a bar, so the span check *is* the check
that the window holds one of each, and a bad window is dropped rather than
quietly poisoning the trace.

**Onsets closer together than the notes are long cannot be resolved, and the
count says so.** `repel all` read a steady **11 onsets a bar** for 23 of 30 bars.
Its tightest gaps are 48 ms and the notes were 80 ms, so overlapping notes beat
and the beats read as attacks. Shortening the notes below the tightest gap took
it to 8.07 with 28 bars reading exactly 8. A histogram of onsets-per-bar is a
cheap diagnostic when the true answer is a known integer, and it named this in
one line.

**And a bunched ensemble is a chord, not a pulse train.** The pre-limiter peak
depended on how far through reorganising itself the ensemble was — 0.31 settled
against 0.62 as one flam, a 2× swing on the same notes. The sketch already
computes how bunched it is, so dividing the level by `1 + order` flattens it to
0.445 / 0.496.

## Next

- [ ] A **spectral-flux onset detector in `@core`**, or at least in a shared
      harness file. Four sketches, four hand-rolled envelope thresholds, four
      wrong first attempts. The cost of not doing it is now measured.
- [ ] **Delay.** `drag` showed that hearing each other late costs a real ensemble
      tempo. Here the players re-place themselves from what they heard a bar ago,
      which is already one bar of delay; more of it should move the stability
      edge off exactly 1, and `drag`'s arithmetic says where.
- [ ] **A player who joins or leaves mid-piece.** The rule never knew n, so it
      should absorb a newcomer without anything being told — and how many bars
      that costs is a number this sketch can report and nothing else here can.
- [ ] **Unequal shares.** Weight the midpoint toward one neighbour and the
      ensemble should settle on a *ratio* rather than an even split, which turns
      a metronome into a groove. The eigenvalues stop being circulant and I do
      not know what happens.
- [ ] Point it at pitch instead of time: the same rule on a pitch circle spreads
      an ensemble evenly across an octave, which is a tuning nobody chose.
- [ ] Two rings at different bar lengths, coupled — `vuza` and `nest` build
      exact partitions by construction and this one finds them by ear; putting a
      found partition beside a constructed one is a comparison nothing here has
      made.
- [ ] The honest gap, again: this measures what the ensemble does, not whether a
      listener can hear it reorganising. Same gap `veil`, `dichotic` and `vuza`
      left.
