# 2026-09-14 — drag: a room full of people listening to each other plays slower than any of them intends

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/drag`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — five players on a 12 m ring, α 0.4,
β 0.15, γ 0.1, seed 4, no conductor. They set out at 120 and settle at 108.9.
The demonstration is the `Room (m)` slider: walk it from 0.5 to 40 and the tempo
falls from 119.5 to 90.2 without anyone playing differently. `Watch conductor`
at 0.2 puts it straight back to 120.

## What I tried

Improvisation and performance, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-09 with
`lombard`. `ideas.md` has carried this since `entrain`: *"Consensus with delay.
Real players hear each other late, and past some coupling strength a delayed
consensus system oscillates rather than converging."*

Sound travels at 343 m/s, so a player ten metres away is heard 29 ms late. That
has a consequence nobody in the room can argue with: **when the ensemble is
perfectly together, every one of them still hears the others as late**, because
the sound arriving now left before now. Correct toward what you hear and you
wait. They all wait. The room slows down, and no one in it is playing badly.

`entrain` asked how *fast* an ensemble agrees — the rate is the listening
graph's algebraic connectivity λ₂. This asks what tempo it agrees *on*.

Each player corrects the heard asynchrony two ways, which is what the
sensorimotor synchronisation literature says people actually do: a fraction α
onto this beat, and a fraction β onto their internal period, where it stays.
A memory of the intended tempo pulls back at γ.

## What I measured

Everything here comes from `sketches/drag/room.ts`, the module the sketch plays.

**The drag rate is β times the ensemble's mean travel time.** Four layouts ×
three room sizes × two values of β, against the closed form:

**24 of 24 cells at ratio 1.00000, worst departure 0.0000%.**

**And it is the *ensemble's* mean, not your own.** Five players huddled together
with one stranded 30 m away:

| player | own mean delay | own drag rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 19.16 ms | 4531.82 µs/beat |
| 1 | 20.47 ms | 4531.82 |
| 2 | 18.55 ms | 4531.82 |
| 3 | 18.47 ms | 4531.82 |
| 4 | 18.85 ms | 4531.82 |
| 5 | **85.77 ms** | 4531.82 |

Their own mean delays span a factor of 4.6 and they drag at the same rate to six
figures. **You cannot improve your own drag by standing closer to someone.**

**But the average is over *attention*, not over people** — and I had it as a
plain average for most of the day. The weight on each player's delay is the
stationary distribution of the listening matrix: how much the ensemble listens
to them. With everyone in earshot the two are identical to **0.0000%** across
every layout and room size, which is exactly why 24 exact cells did not catch
it. Narrow the earshot and they separate, because the huddle stops hearing the
stranded player while still being heard by them:

| earshot | plain mean | attention-weighted | plain predicts | weighted predicts | **heard** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| everyone | 33.88 ms | 32.41 ms | 106.313 | 106.840 | **106.847** |
| 24 m | 28.10 ms | 21.83 ms | 108.424 | 110.807 | **110.819** |
| 12 m | 23.48 ms | 12.07 ms | 110.170 | 114.739 | **114.769** |
| 4 m | 20.67 ms | 5.69 ms | 111.260 | 117.462 | **117.457** |

The tempo belongs to the players everybody listens to. The one nobody can hear
does not drag the room — which is also why narrowing your own ears helps: it
recovers ten of the thirteen bpm the room was costing.

**The tempo it settles on is T + (α + β/γ)·τ.** Exact to **0.00000%** on 30 of
36 settings across four layouts, three room sizes and three correction settings.
Only the *ratio* β/γ matters, not either alone: β 0.05 with γ 0.10, β 0.15 with
γ 0.30 and β 0.30 with γ 0.60 all land on **111.282112 bpm**.

The other 6 of 36 are not a worse prediction — they are settings with **no
settled tempo to predict**. On an asymmetric layout at β/γ = 2 the ensemble
hunts instead of settling, swinging **3.07 bpm** window to window (103.55 to
106.63), while the symmetric ring at identical settings is flat to 0.0000%.

**What a room costs**, six players in a ring at the defaults:

| across | mean travel | settles at | lost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 m | 1.09 ms | 119.51 | 0.49 bpm |
| 5 m | 10.88 ms | 115.24 | 4.76 |
| 10 m | 21.76 ms | 110.83 | 9.17 |
| 20 m | 43.52 ms | 102.97 | 17.03 |
| 40 m | 87.04 ms | 90.17 | 29.83 |

**A conductor works by being *seen*.** An ear gives you a delayed, *relative*
reference; an eye gives an undelayed, *absolute* one, and any weight at all on
it pins the tempo at exactly the nominal. At `Watch conductor` = 0.001 the
simulation reaches 120.00000 where the same room without one settles at 95.84.
The weight only sets how long it takes — 0.01 is immediate, 0.0001 needs
100,000 beats.

**The wobble above α ≈ 1.5 is not the delay's fault.** Push phase correction too
far and the ensemble oscillates instead of agreeing. The threshold is
2/(1 − μ) where μ is the smallest eigenvalue of the listening matrix:

| players | μ | 2/(1−μ) | measured α_crit | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | −0.500 | 1.333 | 1.316 | 0.987 |
| 4 | −0.333 | 1.500 | 1.481 | 0.987 |
| 6 | −0.200 | 1.667 | 1.645 | 0.987 |

Three player counts, three different predictions, a uniform 1.3% bias across all
twelve cells (which is the bisection's own resolution, not structure). And the
decisive one: **a 20× change in delay moves α_crit by 0.049%** — 1.480136 at
2 m against 1.480864 at 40 m. `ideas.md` expected the delay to cause the
instability. It does not. The delay causes the *drag*; the overshoot is plain
over-correction and would happen in a room of no size at all.

**And the room plays it.** Tempo read off a recording, against the closed form:

| across | predicted | measured | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 m | 117.989 | 117.989 | 0.000% |
| 6 m | 114.165 | 114.185 | 0.018% |
| 12 m | 108.890 | 108.895 | 0.004% |
| 20 m | 102.624 | 102.626 | 0.002% |
| 30 m | 95.841 | 95.840 | 0.001% |
| 40 m | 90.013 | 90.058 | 0.050% |

and the four stranded-player rooms within 0.03% at every earshot. The detector
is checked first against a tempo that is 120.000 by construction — **119.988**
with the ensemble tight and **119.983** with its onsets smeared across 58 ms.

(That table is read by two different detectors, which is worth saying out loud:
2, 12 and 30 m by the refractory one and 6, 20 and 40 m by the smoothed
autocorrelation. Every row is a ring, where the ensemble stays tight and both
are inside their validated range, and where both were pointed at the same room
they agree to 0.02%. The stranded-player rows are the third detector only,
because they are the case the first one could not do.)

Two things only the sound shows. With γ = 0 there is no fixed point, and one
capture falls from **93.79 to 71.96 bpm** without levelling off. And switching a
conductor on mid-drag does not simply restore the tempo — the room has a lag to
recover, so it **sprints**: 95.82 bpm, then 160.43, 119.63, 119.93, 119.99
across the quarters of one 80 s capture. Which is what it looks like when a
conductor pulls a dragging orchestra back, and I had not expected to see it.

Levels: 0.468 pre-limiter at the defaults, 0.606 at the loudest setting the
sketch has, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**I predicted the period and measured the gap between beats.** Phase correction
adds α·τ to every beat on top of whatever the period is, because a player who
hears the others late waits that much before playing — every time, not once. The
formula missing that term was 0.8% out at 5 m and 6.4% out at 40 m. An error
that grows with the room is how a missing term proportional to τ announces
itself, and I had already measured the α·τ offset in the previous round and then
written a formula without it.

**I predicted each player's drag from their own mean delay, and the symmetric
case could not tell me I was wrong.** On a ring every player has the same mean
delay as the ensemble, so the two hypotheses coincide and the prediction was
exact — 1.00000 across six cells. It was the *line* and *scatter* layouts, at
0.7778 and 1.1334, that said the rate belonged to the ensemble rather than the
individual. Both ratios were dead constant across every spread and every β,
which is a wrong constant rather than noise, and the clump layout then gave the
answer outright: six players, one number. A prediction verified only where the
system is symmetric has not been verified.

**Two explanations for the last 1–3% died before the third survived.** The
residual on asymmetric layouts looked like the locked onset offsets contributing
a term the formula omits; computing that term and adding it made the error
**412%** instead of 1%. The second candidate was that those runs simply had not
converged — but stretching them from 2,000 to 512,000 beats left the error
bouncing between 1% and 3% rather than shrinking. Only then the third: they are
not converging to anything. Measured over a hundred successive windows the
`line` tempo cycles 103.55 → 106.63 → 103.59 → 106.12, a 3.07 bpm swing, while
the ring at the same settings is identical to six figures window after window.
The formula is exact wherever a settled tempo exists, and where it looked 1–3%
wrong there was nothing to predict.

**And the same mistake a second time, in the same sketch.** The drag rate is a
weighted average over players, and I used a plain one. Twenty-four cells agreed
at ratio 1.00000 — every one of them with uniform earshot, where the two
averages are identical to 0.0000%. It took the recording disagreeing by 5.57% at
a narrow earshot to find it, and the recording was only trustworthy because the
detector had by then been validated on a spread-out ensemble. A prediction
checked only in the regime where two hypotheses coincide has been checked
against one hypothesis.

**The beat detector assumed the thing it was measuring, twice.** It merged attacks
inside a fixed 180 ms refractory, on the reasoning that players within a beat sit
tens of milliseconds apart. Narrowing `Earshot` is exactly the setting where that
stops being true — the stranded player drifts clear of the huddle — so each
attack became its own beat and it read **287 bpm** for a room playing about 110.
Replaced with autocorrelation of the onset-strength envelope, taking the first
near-best peak rather than the best — and *that* returned **60.003 bpm** where
the answer is 120.000 by construction. Diagnosed rather than guessed: the
correlation is 0.698 at 0.5 s and 0.9922 at 1.0 s, because the players' sustained
tones beat against each other at about 31 Hz and a half-second beat holds 15.5
cycles of that. A half-integer, so the fine structure inverts every beat and
repeats every two, and the true period genuinely correlates worse than its
double. Smoothing the flux over 30 ms removes the beating and leaves the
attacks.

The third detector was then validated in *both* regimes before being believed —
tight (119.988) and smeared across 58 ms (119.983) — because the first two had
each been checked only where they happened to work.

## Next

- [ ] The directed case, which `ideas.md` also asks for: one player who listens
      to nobody. The listening matrix stops being symmetric, and the ensemble
      should land on the leader's tempo rather than the mean — a section
      principal rather than a conductor, and a different fix for the same
      problem.
- [ ] γ is a single number standing in for "remembering the tempo you meant".
      Give it a time constant instead and the ensemble should overshoot on a cue
      rather than approaching cleanly, which is what a real one does.
- [ ] The drag is linear in τ and τ is linear in distance, so the whole thing is
      one number — but only because every player hears every other. Put a wall
      in the room and the listening graph disconnects; λ₂ from `entrain` and τ
      from here would then be fighting each other.
- [ ] Anticipation. Real players do not correct toward where you were, they
      correct toward where they predict you will be, which is the term that
      cancels the travel time. That is the actual answer to the problem and it
      is one line: correct toward `heard + τ̂` for the player's own estimate τ̂.
- [ ] The catch-up when a conductor is switched on is a musical event in itself
      and currently just happens. Worth making it a gesture.
- [ ] Latency is the same arithmetic for a network as for a room, at 200,000 km/s
      instead of 343 m/s. The sketch is a calculator for how far apart two
      musicians can be online, and it would be worth checking against what
      people report.
