# 2026-08-29 — crab: the score is a palindrome, so why can you tell?

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/crab`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 12 notes, a note every 2 ticks, bite
0, room 0, retrograde up by 0, seed 9 dorian. That configuration is a genuine
palindrome in the signal, so the `Backwards` toggle is close to inaudible, and
that is the thing to sit with for a minute before touching anything. Then take
`Bite` to 1 and flip `Backwards` again. Seed 44 has the nicest line.

## What I tried

Wild/strange, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-23, `staircase`).

A crab canon plays a line against its own retrograde. Put both voices over the
same span, starting together, and the whole texture is symmetric about its
midpoint: what sounds at t sounds at T−t with the voices swapped. Machaut wrote
one in the fourteenth century and called it "ma fin est mon commencement".

The score is therefore an exact palindrome, which makes the interesting
question empirical rather than theoretical: **if the score is symmetric in
time, why can you hear that it is running backwards?** Two candidates, and this
sketch exists to put them on the same axis:

- **The note's own envelope.** A note that arrives suddenly and decays slowly
  becomes, reversed, one that swells and stops dead. `Bite` runs from a
  symmetric raised cosine — the only shape that reverses onto itself — to a
  hard pluck.
- **The room.** Reverb is causal; energy follows a note and never precedes it.
  Everyone recognises backwards reverb instantly, which suggests it should
  dominate. "Suggests" is not a measurement.

## What I measured

**The score mirrors exactly**, 50 of 50 across ten seeds and five shapes. And
the control works: transposing the retrograde breaks it, which it must, because
at step k the pair is {line[i], line[m]+up} while at the mirrored step it is
{line[m], line[i]+up} — the same set only when up = 0. That was a design bug
found by writing the check: `spread` defaulted to 7 and the default
configuration was not a palindrome at all.

**The acoustic measure** is the correlation of the recorded envelope with its
own reverse, with the mirror axis found by search so nothing depends on knowing
where the cycle starts. The mono sum is what gets measured — the palindrome
swaps the voices and they are panned oppositely, so either channel alone reads
as broken symmetry when it is not.

| | mirror r | | mirror r |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bite 0.00, no room | **0.9161** | symmetric bell, room 0.000 | 0.9174 |
| bite 0.25 | 0.8216 | room 0.175 | 0.9149 |
| bite 0.50 | 0.6793 | room 0.350 | 0.8809 |
| bite 0.75 | 0.5444 | room 0.525 | 0.5897 |
| bite 1.00 | **0.4432** | room 0.700 | **0.6069** |

**The envelope costs 0.4728; the room costs 0.3105.** The difference is 0.16
against a measurement spread of 0.0005 over three repeats, so it is real.

**The note's own envelope carries more of the arrow of time than the room
does.** I did not expect that — backwards reverb is the famous giveaway, and it
is the effect every listener can name. But the shapes of the two curves differ
in a way that explains the intuition: the room does essentially nothing until
about mix 0.35 and then falls off a cliff, while the envelope degrades smoothly
from the very first nudge. A little reverb is free. A little asymmetry is not.
What people recognise is a *lot* of backwards reverb, which is a different
claim from reverb being the stronger cue.

The baseline is 0.916 rather than 1.0. I did not close that gap and do not want
to imply I know why; the remaining asymmetry is small enough not to affect the
comparison, since both arms start from it.

## What went wrong

Three attempts at the same measurement, and every failure was in the envelope
estimator rather than in the sketch.

**The frame has to hold more than one carrier period.** At 8 ms frames and a
165 Hz root that is 1.3 periods, so the "envelope" was partly tracking the
waveform — and a triangle wave reversed is not the same triangle wave, which
puts a ceiling on any symmetry measurement. This is the third time this repo
has paid for that exact mistake; `continuum` and `staircase` each cost a run.

**It has to be zero-phase.** A causal smoother has a direction in time built
into it, and the direction of time is the thing being measured. The final
version rectifies and applies a *centred* moving average of one carrier period,
twice.

**Blocky frames alias against the note grid.** Sweeping the frame length gave
0.87, 0.89, 0.96, 0.96, **0.79**, 0.91 — non-monotonic, because at a frame rate
that does not divide the note spacing the frames straddle note boundaries
differently on each side of the mirror axis. That put ±0.05 on the answer.

And that last one matters more than the fix, because of what the noisy version
said. The first run reported the envelope costing 0.4611 and the room 0.5076 —
**the room winning, by 0.046, inside its own ±0.05 of noise.** I nearly had a
result that was the opposite of the truth and that agreed with the folk
intuition, which is the worst possible combination: a wrong answer that nobody
would question. Measuring the method's own repeatability first would have
caught it immediately, and now does — three repeats, spread 0.0005, printed
above the comparison it licenses.

**And a prediction I got wrong and can show wrong.** I reasoned that a note
starting at step k and lasting d occupies [k, k+d], whose mirror is
[T−k−d, T−k], so the sound could only be symmetric when d equals the step
spacing. Measured across nine note lengths, r at d = st was 0.7937 — the
*lowest* in the middle of the range, not a peak. The derivation missed that the
mirror axis is free to sit half a note-length away from the score's axis, which
is exactly what the axis search finds. The construction is symmetric at any
note length; only my chosen axis was not.

## Next

- [ ] The third cue: pitch. A rising line reversed is a falling one, and
      contour asymmetry may beat both of these. It needs a line that is *not*
      its own retrograde to test, which this sketch cannot currently make.
- [ ] Attack asymmetry and decay asymmetry separately. `Bite` moves both at
      once, and they are not obviously equal.
- [ ] Ask the room the sharper question: is it the *tail* that gives it away,
      or the pre-delay? Reverse only the tail and keep the onset causal.
- [ ] A table canon — the line against its retrograde *and* its inversion and
      the retrograde-inversion, all four at once, which is the Bach puzzle
      canon and is exactly one more voice here.
- [ ] Measure the palindrome per critical band rather than broadband. The
      envelope cue and the room cue probably live at different frequencies.
- [ ] Every measurement in this repo should print its own repeatability before
      the comparison it is used for. Today that was the difference between a
      result and its opposite.
