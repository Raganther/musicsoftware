# 2026-07-28 — jam mode: the sketches become a band

**Touched:** `src/runtime/jam.ts` (new), `src/runtime/host.ts`,
`src/core/record.ts` + `capture.worklet.js` (new), `src/main.ts`

## What I tried

Making every sketch playable *simultaneously*, synced, mixable — a live rig
rather than a gallery. Plus the performance layer: scene snapshots, tap
tempo, and recording the master to WAV.

## The load-bearing observation

Sync cost nothing. Every sketch already runs on the one global transport and
the one AudioContext — a decision made on day one so that ideas would be
comparable. The only reason sketches couldn't play together was that the
shell mounted one at a time. Jam mode is mostly *removing* that restriction:
`mountSketch` gained a `dest` (per-channel chain) and `applyTransport: false`
(so the last-added sketch's bpm doesn't clobber the jam). The sketches
themselves needed **zero changes** — teardown discipline and per-sketch buses
were built for solo mode, and multi-mount is what they turn out to buy.

## Decisions

- **Channel strip = fader + bipolar DJ filter + mute/solo + meter.** The
  filter (left LP sweeps down, right HP up, centre transparent, double-click
  resets) is the one sound-design control that works on any sketch without
  the sketch's cooperation. It's shell-level, so every future sketch gets it
  for free.
- **Scenes snapshot everything** — every channel's params, level, filter,
  mute, plus bpm and swing. Recall matches channels by sketch id. Keys 1-4
  recall, shift stores, and an empty slot stores on plain click so the first
  save is discoverable. This is the jam element: build a change on a muted
  channel, bring it in on the beat.
- **Record taps post-limiter** — the file is exactly what the ears got.
  A capture worklet posts blocks to the main thread; stop assembles a
  16-bit stereo WAV. Capped at 6 minutes.
- **QWERTY layers all listening channels.** Accepted for v1 and documented
  in the help: it's half feature (layered synths are a real technique), half
  missing per-channel input routing. The keymap conflicts were real though:
  't' and 'r' are note keys, so tap is 'b' and record is shift+R.
- **Duplicate channels are refused.** Two instances of one sketch would
  share a ParamStore key and fight over persistence. Flash the existing
  strip instead.

## What surprised me

- Two generative sketches on the same clock but *different pattern lengths*
  (drift's 16/12/8 rings against loom's bars) phase against each other
  exactly like a polymetric piece — the jam sounds composed before you touch
  anything. The sync isn't just technical; it's musical structure for free.
- The mixer's mute is a better performance control than any sketch param.
  The cheapest gesture — silence — reads as the strongest musical intent.
