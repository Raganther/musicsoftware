# 2026-09-15 — lattice: a row of tone holes has a ceiling, and it is not the note's

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/lattice`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 8 holes 4 mm wide, 50 mm apart, 3 mm
wall, a 7.5 mm bore, root 48, seed 6. The demonstration is `Holes open`: walk it
0 → 7 and the note climbs from 131 to 324 Hz while the red band on the impedance
plot — the lattice cutoff — does not move at all. Then take hold of `Hole radius`
and watch the whole ceiling slide while the note stays put.

## What I tried

Synthesis, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-10 with `rosin`.
`ideas.md` has asked since `overblow` for *"real tone holes: a row of them with
open/closed state, so the fingering system is the instrument."*

`overblow` had one vent — a hole at a position, which shortens the tube. A row of
them is a different object: a **periodic structure**, with a stopband and a
passband. Below a cutoff the wave cannot get past the open holes and turns back;
above it the lattice is transparent and the sound runs down the whole tube.

The consequence is the interesting part, and it is a real fact about woodwinds:
that cutoff is set by the *holes* — their width, their spacing, the wall they are
drilled through — and **not by which ones you are covering**. So every note on
the instrument shares one spectral ceiling, and that fixed ceiling is a large
part of why a clarinet is recognisable from the bottom of its range to the top.

The bore is a transfer-matrix chain: sections of pipe with tone holes shunted
across them, and the notes are its impedance maxima, because a reed is a
pressure-controlled valve and plays where the bore pushes back hardest.

## What I measured

Two routes to the cutoff, kept apart on purpose. `cutoff()` solves the infinite
lattice's Bloch dispersion relation. `resonances()` chains the real, finite,
fingered bore. Neither knows about the other.

**The self-check first, and it earned its place immediately.** For a lossless
reciprocal cell, half the trace of the transfer matrix must be a real number —
anything else means the sign conventions in the pipe sections and the hole
impedances disagree, and then nothing downstream means anything. First run:
**6.55e−3**, which is small enough to have been ignored and was a real fault (the
open hole's radiation resistance was making the "lossless" cell lossy). Fixed:
**0.00e+0** over eighteen cells.

**A cylindrical reed pipe resonates at odd multiples of its fundamental, and the
cutoff is where it stops.** The same instrument, five fingerings:

| holes open | fundamental | the series breaks at | vs. dispersion relation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 188.5 Hz | 1261 Hz | −0.3% |
| 4 | 212.5 Hz | 1294 Hz | +2.4% |
| 5 | 242.5 Hz | 1300 Hz | +2.8% |
| 6 | 282.5 Hz | 1269 Hz | +0.4% |
| 7 | 338.5 Hz | 1278 Hz | +1.1% |

**The fundamental moves by a factor of 1.80 and the ceiling by 2.8%**, against a
dispersion-relation cutoff of 1264 Hz computed without reference to any of it.

**And it tracks the lattice when the lattice changes.** Nine geometries — hole
radius, spacing, wall thickness and bore radius, each varied alone — with the
resonance-departure route against the Bloch route: worst departure **9.0%**, best
1.8%. The errors are mostly positive, meaning the finite bore stays harmonic a
little past the ideal band edge. I checked whether that shrinks as the lattice
gets longer, which is what a finite-size effect would do, and it does not: the
five fingerings above run −0.3%, +2.4%, +2.8%, +0.4%, +1.1% with no trend in the
number of open holes.

**It scales the way the holes say.** The long-wavelength result is
f_c ∝ (b/a)/√(s·t_e); over thirteen geometries the model agrees with it to
between **1.1% and 6.7%**, worst on bore radius. I guessed the residual was the
long-wavelength approximation failing as k·s grows and tested it — the
correlation between k·s at the cutoff and the error is **0.312**. Not a story. It
is unexplained.

**An irregular lattice takes its ceiling from its worst cell.** With
`Irregularity` at 1.0 the individual cells' cutoffs span 896–1451 Hz while their
mean barely moves. Over six settings the instrument's ceiling correlates with
the **lowest** cell's cutoff at **r = 0.875** and with the mean at **0.250**
(worst error 15% against the lowest, 29% against the mean). One sloppy hole leaks
and the whole instrument loses its top — which instrument makers have always
said, and which is pleasant to watch fall out of a transfer matrix.

**From the sound.** The sketch plays at the frequencies the bore model computes:
over four fingerings, **0.04%, 0.11%, 0.02%, 0.01%**. And the bore suppresses its
even harmonics by **20.7, 20.4 and 16.4 dB** at three fingerings, which is a
cylindrical pipe closed at one end doing what it should.

Levels: 0.554 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**The ceiling is not audible as a clean spectral knee, and I spent three
detectors finding that out.**

The first walked the harmonic series and reported the ceiling at **exactly 2·f0
for all eight fingerings**. A wrong answer that clean names its own bug: a
cylindrical reed pipe suppresses its even harmonics by 20 dB, so "the first
harmonic that falls well below the ones under it" is n = 2, every time, forever.

The second walked the *odd* harmonics and reported **exactly 3·f0 for seven of
the eight**. Same shape of error one level up: the natural rolloff from the
fundamental to the third harmonic is 15–21 dB in this synthesis, which is as big
as the cutoff cliff, so "find the biggest step down" has nothing to distinguish
them. A cliff-finding rule needs the cliff to be bigger than the slope, and that
is a property of the signal, not of the rule — worth checking before relying on
it rather than after.

The third fits two straight lines to the log-log spectrum and takes the
breakpoint, and this time I validated it first on synthetic spectra with knees I
chose: recovered to **0.3–3.3%**. Pointed at the instrument it returns anything
between **324 and 1512 Hz** across fingerings, with a fitted slope above the knee
of −5 to −8 dB/octave against −8 to −20 below.

So the detector is fine and the answer is that there is no knee. It is visible in
the model too, and I should have looked there first: above the cutoff the bore's
impedance does not stay low, it **bounces** — 0.03, 0.09, 0.19, 0.10, 0.16 of the
fundamental's peak. The cutoff is sharp in *where the resonances stop being
harmonic* and blunt in *the radiated envelope*. The bore stops **organising** the
spectrum up there without **removing** it, and those are different things that I
had been treating as one.

**And one of the three detectors was measuring my own arbitrary choice.** The
harmonic amplitudes were `|Z|^0.55`, an exponent picked by ear, which compressed
the model's own 20 dB collapse at the cutoff into 10 — blunting precisely the
feature under test. Changed to exponent 1, which is both more faithful and less
arbitrary: the harmonic follows the impedance. It did not rescue the knee (324 →
1512 Hz became 324 → 1512 Hz), which is how I know the bluntness is the physics
and not the taste.

## Next

- [ ] A real reed. The amplitudes here are assigned from |Z| rather than arising
      from a nonlinear valve driven by it, and that is exactly the step that
      would put the cutoff into the radiated sound properly — the reed's
      harmonic generation is what the bore then has to support or not.
      `rosin` built a stick-slip state machine for a bow; a reed is the same
      shape of object.
- [ ] Cross-fingerings, which need per-hole state rather than `open` counting
      from the bell. They are the whole art of baroque woodwind and they work
      *because* a closed hole downstream is not nothing — the compliance term is
      already in the model and currently does nothing visible.
- [ ] The register hole. `overblow` and `cone` both have the octave/twelfth
      break; here it would be a hole small enough to be a stopband of its own,
      and the cutoff arithmetic should predict where to put it.
- [ ] A conical lattice, which is a saxophone. `cone` has the modal bore and
      this has the holes; the two have not met.
- [ ] What sets the 1.1–6.7% spread against the long-wavelength closed form?
      The k·s story is dead. The end corrections (0.75b inner, 0.85b outer) are
      the obvious next suspect and are one sweep away from being ruled in or out.
- [ ] The cutoff falls with the instrument's size, so a bass clarinet's ceiling
      really is lower than a soprano's. Whether the *ratio* of ceiling to lowest
      note is what makes a family sound like a family is a question this can
      answer and I did not ask.
