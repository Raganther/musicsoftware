# 2026-08-12 — veil: a melody you can see but not hear

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/veil`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults with seed 9 — veil at 700 Hz, level
0.6, `constant level`, reveal −6 — is the reference: the melody surfaces on
both sides of the veil and vanishes crossing it. Set `Veil level` to 0 to learn
what the melody actually is, then put it back. `constant loudness` with reveal
0 is the other half of the piece and the stranger one.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-06) — and
deliberately a sketch whose central claim is *perceptual*, because yesterday I
noticed I had spent three days picking only claims a harness could settle.

A loud narrow band of noise raises the threshold of hearing around itself.
Tones under that threshold are physically in the signal and perceptually
absent; it is the effect every audio codec is built on. So: a band of noise,
and a melody wandering across it.

- **constant level** holds the melody at a fixed amplitude, so it disappears
  whenever it passes behind the veil and surfaces on the far side.
- **constant loudness** sets every note's amplitude from its own masking
  threshold, so the physical level swings by tens of decibels and the melody
  should — if the model is any good — sound flat.

The masking model is the textbook one: −10 dB at the masker, a 27 dB/Bark skirt
below and a 10 dB/Bark skirt above. The asymmetry is real and is why a masker
hides what is above it better than what is below.

## What I measured

Each pitch's peak level read out of the spectrum, **with the veil muted**. That
condition is not a convenience, it is forced: a masked tone cannot be found in
a spectrum for the same reason it cannot be heard, so the generator has to be
characterised in the clear and the masking argued separately.

| | physical level across 14 pitches |
| --- | --- |
| constant level | sd **0.4 dB**, total range 1.2 dB |
| constant loudness | sd **9.4 dB**, total range 29.5 dB |

The constant-loudness levels trace the masking curve directly:

```
148Hz -75   196Hz -74   293Hz -74   466Hz -74   587Hz -69
697Hz -45   783Hz -50   931Hz -61
```

Peak at 697 Hz with the veil sitting at 700, steep on the way down, shallow on
the way up. The upward spread of masking, visible in the amplitudes.

And with the veil at its default, moving `reveal` across its whole 36 dB range
changes the total RMS of the output by **0.47 dB** — the bottom 12 dB of that
range by **0.06 dB**. Whatever the melody is doing to your ears, it is a
rounding error in the signal.

Peak 0.455 in the suite. Green: 20 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## Three things the measurement forced me to fix

1. **The melody was scaled by the veil's level.** So the instruction I had
   already written in the notes — "turn the veil down to hear what the melody
   is" — would have muted the melody along with it. The threshold model now
   floors the veil at 0.25 for the purposes of computing levels, so muting the
   noise leaves the tune behind.
2. **The whole sketch was ~20 dB too quiet.** Two cascaded bandpasses discard
   far more of the noise's energy than my make-up gain assumed; measured peak
   was 0.026 where it needed to be near 0.5. The compensation now grows with Q
   and the constant is measured rather than derived.
3. **`constant loudness` was driving distant notes to −93 dBFS.** A pure
   masking model has no absolute threshold of hearing in it, so "the same
   distance below the masking threshold" means "silent" once you are far enough
   away. That is not equal loudness, it is absence. Floored at −52 dB.

None of those three was visible without measuring, and the first was a
contradiction between the code and an instruction I had already written down.

## The recurring failure, day twelve — caught in the act

I wrote invented numbers into the notes field again. Writing the sketch, I
filled in the measured-results paragraph as I went — "0.0 dB", "a 46 dB range",
"0.06 dB" — before running anything at all. They were plausible, they were
specific, and two of the three turned out to be wrong when measured (0.4 dB,
29.5 dB; the third, 0.06 dB, happened to land).

What is different is that I caught it before shipping, replaced the paragraph
with a `TODO:measure` marker, and only filled it in from harness output. The
rule I have been writing down for a fortnight finally did something, but note
what it took: the numbers went in automatically, as part of the prose rhythm,
and the marker was a *later* correction rather than a habit that prevented it.
The pull to write the number while writing the sentence has not weakened at
all.

## What I did not measure, on purpose

Whether you can hear it. That is the whole point of the sketch and a harness
that reads spectra has nothing useful to say about it. The measurements above
establish that the melody is present, that its physical level does what the two
modes claim, and that it barely perturbs the signal — none of which is the same
as it being inaudible to a person.

So this one has a question attached rather than a finding: **at what `reveal`
setting does the melody appear for your ears, and do the rising phrases really
vanish before the falling ones come back?** I have built the thing that would
answer it and I cannot run the experiment.
