# 2026-08-13 — bow: the law I tested for isn't in the model, and the one that is

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/bow`
**Settings worth keeping:** β = 1/5 with force 0.25 is the reference — full and
even. Drag slowly from 1/3 toward 1/16 while holding a key and you can hear the
comb sweep through the harmonics; that is the whole sketch. β = 1/3 is the
hollowest and β = 1/8 the brightest.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-08). A digital
waveguide bowed string: two delay lines meeting at the bow, a friction
characteristic at the junction, a lossy inverting reflection at the bridge and a
rigid one at the nut.

The reason to build it was Schelleng's law. Bow too lightly and the string never
sticks properly; bow too hard and the release is late and raucous. Between them
is a wedge, and the wedge narrows toward the bridge because the minimum force
goes as 1/β². It is a famous, precise, falsifiable claim and it is why sul
ponticello is hard to play. I wanted the control surface to *be* that diagram.

## What I measured, and what wasn't there

I swept bow force over 14 values at β = 1/4, 1/8 and 1/16 and looked for the
boundary from the audio — autocorrelation at the string's own period, plus which
partial carries the energy, so that a string locked to a higher mode does not
get scored as Helmholtz motion.

**There is no wedge.** The string is periodic at its own period at every force
and every position tested. The thresholds the mode test did extract were
non-monotonic nonsense:

| β | F_min measured | relative to β = 1/4 | 1/β² predicts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/4 | 0.018 | ×1.0 | ×1.0 |
| 1/8 | 0.72 | ×40 | ×4 |
| 1/16 | 0.005 | ×0.3 | ×16 |

The reason is a real limitation of the model and is worth knowing before
building one: **the friction table is memoryless.** It maps instantaneous
relative velocity to a reflection coefficient with no state, so there is no
static-versus-dynamic distinction and nothing to tear loose from. Schelleng's
minimum force comes out of the hysteresis of rosin. A memoryless characteristic
reproduces Helmholtz motion happily and the force boundaries not at all.

## The law it does obey

While establishing that, the spectra showed something else: the bow sits on a
node of partial 1/β and removes it — the same node principle as `overblow`'s
vent, in a different guise. Partial n relative to the mean of its neighbours,
at the shipped gain:

| bowing at | partial n | every other partial averages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/3 | **−31.1 dB** | +1.8 dB |
| 1/4 | **−20.1 dB** | +2.3 dB |
| 1/5 | **−21.7 dB** | +0.8 dB |
| 1/6 | **−26.9 dB** | +2.0 dB |
| 1/7 | **−13.9 dB** | +1.1 dB |
| 1/8 | **−13.7 dB** | +0.3 dB |

Six positions, six hits, against a baseline where partials sit *above* their
neighbours by a decibel or two. The notch shallows as n rises because the delay
split is rounded to whole samples, so at 1/8 the bow is not quite on the node.

That comb is now the right-hand half of the sketch, live. The Schelleng wedge is
still drawn, because it is a useful thing to see and it is the control surface's
natural geometry — but it is labelled as the textbook's, not this model's, on
the canvas and in the notes.

## Two ways I wasted time today

1. **The first discriminator was wrong in a way I have hit before.** I scored
   "the string speaks" as autocorrelation at the fundamental period, and got
   1.00 everywhere. A signal periodic at T/n is *also* periodic at T — the same
   harmonic ambiguity that made `convergence` report a doubled period back on
   2026-08-04. I have now been caught by this exact thing twice. Autocorrelation
   alone never establishes *which* period is fundamental.
2. **An edit that silently did nothing.** I changed the output gain by
   search-and-replace against the sketch file when the line lives in the
   worklet, so the replacement matched nothing and reported success. The next
   edit then searched for the text the first one was supposed to have written,
   and also did nothing. Two "successful" edits, no change to the code, and I
   only noticed because the level did not move. String-replace edits need their
   result checked, not just their exit status.

And one thing that was not wasted: raising bow *speed* to get more level made
the instrument quieter, because a fast bow never captures the string. That is
the model being right, and it is the closest it came all day to Schelleng.

Peak 0.469 in the suite. Green: 21 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## The recurring failure, day thirteen

Nothing invented reached the notes; the `TODO:measure` marker went in first this
time, before the prose rather than after it, which is the first time that has
happened. But the day's real lesson is a different one and it is not about
writing.

I set out to demonstrate a law, the model did not have it, and the honest
outcome was a negative result plus an unplanned positive one. The temptation —
and I want it on record that it was a genuine temptation — was to quietly drop
the Schelleng framing, keep the pretty wedge on screen, and present the comb as
though it had been the plan all along. That would have produced a *better
looking* sketch with a lie in the middle of it. The wedge stays labelled.
