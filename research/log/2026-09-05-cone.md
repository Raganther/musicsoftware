# 2026-09-05 — cone: why a saxophone overblows an octave

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/cone`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — truncation 0.05, first mode MIDI 50,
blow 0.34, reed stiffness 0.62, bore impedance 9, seed 11. Set `Truncation` to 0
and push `Register vent` to 1: an octave. Take truncation to 20 and do the same:
a twelfth. Everything in between is neither, which is the whole point.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family — last visited 2026-08-30 with `wolf`.

`overblow` built a cylindrical reed instrument here on 2026-08-08 and measured
its register break at 2.98 against a predicted 3. The cone is the other half of
that story and has been sitting in `ideas.md` unbuilt since, with a note
recording a specific failure: the cheap way of getting one — flipping the
far-end reflection in a waveguide — does not oscillate at all. A cone is not a
sign flip.

The difference is a boundary condition. Pressure in a cone goes as
p = (A/r)·sin(kr + φ) rather than as a plane wave, so a pressure release at the
open end and zero flow at the truncated apex give

    kL + arctan(k·r₀) = nπ

with two limits worth knowing. A complete cone (r₀ → 0) gives kL = nπ, a full
harmonic series. A cylinder (r₀ → ∞) gives kL = (n − ½)π, odd harmonics only.
Everything between is a real truncated cone, which is what a saxophone actually
is — nobody plays an instrument with a needle-sharp apex.

So I built the bore modally, with the mode frequencies solved from that
transcendental equation, and put a reed on it. The frequencies are derived; what
the reed does with them is not.

pressure, the resonances match the roots — solved by Newton in the harness where
the sketch bisects — across truncations from 0 to 20:

This is plumbing and I want to be clear that it is: the resonators are where I
put them, so agreeing with my own equation proves the bore is the bore I claim
and nothing more. Nothing below means anything without it.

**The register break follows the geometry, and that part is not plumbing.** A
vent spoils the first resonance so the reed has nothing to lock onto but the
second; which mode it then picks is the nonlinear dynamics deciding, and it
could have chosen the third, or refused to move at all.

Worst error **0.060** on a ratio running 2.00 to 2.95. A complete cone breaks at
an octave and a cylinder at a twelfth.

The line I like most is the last one. **2.986 sits on top of the 2.98
`overblow` measured**, from a completely different model — a digital waveguide
rather than a modal bore, written a month earlier for a different reason. Two
independent ways of being a clarinet, agreeing to a hundredth. That is the sort
of cross-check I cannot arrange deliberately.

**Blowing harder does not change register**, here or on a real instrument. The
first version of the experiment swept eight blowing pressures on five bores and
found no jump anywhere: the pitch stays on the first mode and simply gets
louder. I had assumed pressure would do it and it does not, which is why
instruments have register keys and why `overblow` used a vent too.

**There is a genuine oscillation threshold.** Bore impedance 4 gives a tail RMS
of 9.6e-4 — nothing — and impedance 6 gives 2.8e-1. A reed sustains only when
the bore's peak impedance exceeds its losses, and the cliff between those two
numbers is that condition showing itself.

A late accident made that reading better than it was. Raising the output gain by
1.32× to bring the sketch's level into the target band and re-running the sweep
moved the impedance-6 number by exactly 1.32× — it is a signal, scaling with the
gain applied to it — while the impedance-4 number moved by only 1.10×, because
most of what it measures is breath noise passing through a bore that is not
oscillating and never was. A threshold you can see in the *scaling* as well as
the magnitude is a better threshold than one you infer from a big gap.

Levels: 0.51 pre-limiter at the defaults — 0.508 and 0.517 on two smoke runs.

## What went wrong

Three failures, and each one taught me something about modal bores rather than
about JavaScript.

**An all-pole resonator lags 90° at its own resonance.** The first bore summed
two-pole resonators, which means the total had zero phase *between* the modes
rather than on them — so the reed closed the loop somewhere in the gap and
oscillated at 1.3 to 1.8 times the first mode, at every truncation and every
pressure. A bore's input impedance is real at a resonance; the model has to be
too. Adding the (1 − z⁻²) numerator that makes each mode a bandpass fixed it,
and the sounding pitch snapped to 1.00 of the first mode.

**Then it stopped oscillating entirely**, because normalising every mode to unit
peak gain had removed the loop gain that made it sing. That turned out to be
worth having: the peak input impedance is a real physical quantity, so it is now
a parameter with a measurable threshold rather than an accident of whatever the
resonators happened to scale to.

**And the best one: my normalisation silently cancelled the vent.** Dividing
each mode by its own peak means a mode spoiled to a sixty-seventh of its Q comes
back out at exactly the same height — so opening the register vent changed
nothing, at any truncation, and the table read 1.00 all the way across. A mode's
peak impedance is proportional to its Q, so the normalisation has to use a fixed
reference and let the damping actually move the peak. One line, and the whole
experiment appeared.

What these have in common is that each one produced a *plausible* wrong answer
rather than an obviously broken one — a pitch at 1.4×, a silent bore, a vent
that does nothing — and in each case the thing that identified it was having a
predicted number to compare against rather than a sound to judge.

## Next

- [ ] The cone's modes are stretched, not harmonic, at any real truncation. That
      is why saxophone fingerings work in both registers only approximately, and
      the sketch could measure the mistuning in cents against equal temperament
      as the truncation grows.
- [ ] A real vent, as a hole at a position, the way `overblow` did it — the
      damping stand-in gets the effect without the geometry, and a positioned
      hole would predict *which* modes survive rather than being told.
- [ ] The reed has no mass. Adding one gives a second oscillator that can pull
      the pitch away from the bore's mode, which is exactly what embouchure
      does, and it would let the sketch bend notes.
- [ ] Measure the playing frequency against the bore's mode: a reed does not
      play exactly at a resonance but slightly off it, and by how much is a
      function of the reed's own stiffness.
- [ ] The oscillation threshold against damping should be a straight line in the
      right coordinates — the impedance has to beat the losses, so threshold
      impedance times damping ought to be constant.
