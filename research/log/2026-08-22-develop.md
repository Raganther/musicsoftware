# 2026-08-22 — develop: the textbook operations are shortcuts, but not the famous ones

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/develop`
**Settings worth keeping:** seed 6 at 5 notes is the clearest demonstration —
a three-move path where you can hear each deformation land. Seed 57 and 79 give
five-move paths, which is about as long as stays followable by ear. The thing
to actually do is toggle `Only move notes` while it plays: the same start and
goal, and the piece gets twice as long and stops sounding like development at
all.

## What I tried

Composition tools, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-17, species).

The question a composer actually has is not "what note comes next" but "how do
I get from this idea to that one". Schoenberg's answer was developing
variation — you deform one motif into another a step at a time, and the
deformations are a small shared vocabulary: invert it, play it backwards,
rotate it, open the intervals out, move one note.

That is a search problem, and stating it as one makes it testable. Give the
thing a motif and a goal and let breadth-first search find the shortest
sequence connecting them. The path is the piece: you hear every intermediate
motif, each one a single legal move from the last.

A motif is stored as semitone offsets from its own first note, so transposition
costs nothing and only shape matters. Breadth-first rather than anything
cleverer because "shortest" is the whole claim and an approximation would
quietly hollow it out.

## What I measured

**Correctness first**, against an oracle with the operations and the search
written out again from their definitions: **24 paths, 0 steps that were not the
operation named, 0 longer than the oracle's shortest.**

**Then the claim.** Every textbook teaches invert and retrograde; the implied
promise is that they get you somewhere faster than nudging notes. Same pairs,
two vocabularies, oracle BFS both times:

| motif length | full vocabulary | notes only | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 notes | 2.88 moves | 5.30 | **1.87× longer**, 13 of 60 pairs unreachable |
| 5 notes | 3.08 moves | 5.39 | **2.05× longer**, 24 of 60 unreachable |

The unreachable column is the better half of that result. Nearly half of these
goals cannot be reached at all by single-note edits within twelve moves —
because no sequence of one-note nudges reverses a shape, it only walks toward
it, and the walk runs out of budget.

**From the audio**: all four motifs of a three-move path heard complete and in
order, 5–6 times each, first appearing at notes 0, 5, 10 and 15 — exactly path
order. Pre-limiter peak 0.497. Green: 29 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean
teardown.

## The result I did not expect

I assumed inversion and retrograde would be the load-bearing operations,
because they are the ones with names everybody knows. Removing each in turn and
re-searching the same 40 pairs:

| removed | cost |
| --- | --- |
| rotate | +0.63 moves |
| narrow | +0.60 |
| widen | +0.35 |
| invert | +0.25 |
| retrograde | **+0.23** |

The two famous ones are the two that matter least. In hindsight the reason is
plain: inversion and retrograde are **involutions**. Each is its own undo, so
each connects a motif to exactly one other motif — one extra edge per node.
Rotation cycles through as many states as there are intervals, and widen and
narrow are open-ended in both directions. An operation's value in a search is
how much of the space it opens, and self-inverse operations open almost none.

That is a statement about this operation set in this space, not about music.
Retrograde may well be worth more to a listener than to a search — a listener
recognises it, and recognition is not distance. But it is a real finding about
the structure, and it is the opposite of what I would have written down.

## The recurring failure, day twenty-one

Nothing invented, and the known-answer channel earned its place for the fourth
day running. But it caught a subtler thing this time and it took two attempts.

The audio check reported **0 of 4 motifs found** while the sketch was doing
exactly the right thing. The cause: my reading-collapser merged consecutive
identical pitches into one note, and the very first motif is `57 57 53 54 51` —
it *begins* with a repeated note. Fixing that to merge only readings adjacent
in the sample stream got one motif of four. Still wrong, and for the same
reason one layer down: with a 0.12 s decay there is no actual silence between
two repeated notes, so no reading is ever dropped between them and the merge
happens anyway. Shortening the notes to 0.06 s for the measurement gave 4 of 4.

The lesson worth keeping: **a detector that cannot represent a repeat cannot
measure music that contains one.** I have now built envelope and pitch
detectors on four consecutive days and every one of them has had a failure mode
that produced *plausible* output — too many onsets, too few, magnitude
compressed, repeats swallowed. None of them announced itself. The only reason
any of them got caught is the known-answer channel, and the only reason that
works is that it is built before the interesting measurement rather than after.

One process note: I shipped a doc comment claiming the search was
bidirectional. It was never bidirectional — I wrote the comment while planning
and the code came out unidirectional because the operations are not all
invertible. Caught by re-reading before commit, but it would have been a lie in
the repository, and it is the same class of error as an invented number.

## Next

- [ ] Rhythm. The vocabulary deforms pitch shape only, so augmentation and
      diminution — half of what developing variation means — are absent. That
      is the single biggest hole and it doubles the state space.
- [ ] Weight the moves. A shortest path treats "move note 3 up a semitone" and
      "invert the whole thing" as equally expensive, which is musically absurd.
      Costs would make the paths sound more like development and less like a
      puzzle solution.
- [ ] Let the user draw the start and the goal instead of taking what the seed
      gives. Everything downstream already re-derives.
- [ ] Show the *runners-up*: at each step, the other moves that were one longer.
      That is the thing a composer would actually want to browse.
- [ ] A path that has to pass through a given motif in the middle — two searches
      joined, and the compositional equivalent of a waypoint.
