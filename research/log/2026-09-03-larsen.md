# 2026-09-03 — larsen: feedback can only sing on a tooth of its own comb

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/larsen`, `scripts/smoke.mjs`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 12 ms delay, 3 loops at a 1.32
ratio, resonance 520 Hz at Q 5, loop gain 1.06, saturation 3, seed 5. The thing
to do is drag `Loop delay` slowly and watch the comb slide under the filter on
the canvas while the pitch steps rather than glides. Then set `Loops` to 1 and
`Loop gain` to just under the threshold the canvas prints: silence. Nudge it up
0.01 and it sings.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange, the stalest family — last visited 2026-08-29 with `crab`.

Point a microphone at its own loudspeaker and it howls, but not at any old
pitch. Energy goes round a loop of delay D and filter H, and a frequency
survives only if it comes back in phase with itself and no quieter than it left:

    |g·H(f)| ≥ 1        and        2π·f·D/sr − ∠H(f) = 2πn

The phase condition is the interesting half. It admits only a **comb** of
frequencies, one per integer n, so the loop cannot sing wherever it likes — it
has to pick a tooth, and the filter decides which. That makes feedback a
*quantised* instrument: the pitch moves in steps, not glides, and the steps are
computable.

So the sketch is a bank of delay-and-filter loops with a saturator, which is
what makes it an instrument rather than an explosion: above unity the amplitude
grows until tanh compresses the effective gain back to exactly one, and it sits
there.

## What I measured

The prediction is recomputed in the harness from f0, Q and the sample rate.
Only the integer delay is read out of the sketch, and that is a fact about what
was configured rather than a claim about what it will do.

**The phase condition holds to four decimal places.** Take the sounding
frequency, put it back through f·D/sr − ∠H(f)/2π, and it has to be an integer:

**0.01 Hz**. Nothing in the measurement is constrained to produce integers — a
Goertzel scan over 80–4000 Hz picks the loudest thing it can find — which is
what makes that a test rather than a tautology.

The pitch falls about 50 Hz inside a tooth and then snaps back up. Measured mode
index matches the arithmetic **11 of 11** and both jumps land where predicted.

**And the threshold is not 1.** The mode that survives is generally not sitting
on the filter's peak, so the loop needs gain 1/|H(f_n)| to sustain — which
scallops as the comb slides underneath:

| delay | \|H\| at the mode | predicted threshold | measured | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7 ms | 0.8227 | 1.216 | 1.200 | −0.016 |
| 9.5 | 0.9959 | 1.004 | 0.980 | −0.024 |
| 11 | 0.9339 | 1.071 | 1.060 | −0.011 |
| 12.5 | 0.8400 | 1.190 | 1.160 | −0.030 |
| 16 | 0.9484 | 1.054 | 1.020 | −0.034 |
| 21 | 0.9979 | 1.002 | 0.960 | −0.042 |

Predicted thresholds range **1.002 to 1.216** — a 21% swing, far outside the
0.042 worst error, so the scallop is unmistakably real. A flat "it howls at
gain 1" would be wrong by up to 22%.

**Every measured threshold sits slightly below its prediction**, by 0.011 to
0.042, and the bias has the right sign to be explained rather than tuned away:
just under threshold the loop's decay time goes to infinity, so a test that asks
"is there still energy 1.4 seconds later" fires a little early. I would rather
report a residual with a mechanism than shave it off.

Levels: 0.498 pre-limiter at the defaults, measured through the smoke gate.

## The gate, fixed rather than explained again

Yesterday's entry ended by saying the smoke suite had produced spurious `silent`
failures on three consecutive days — six sketches across three runs, every one
passing on a re-run — and that explaining it a fourth time was not the answer.
So `scripts/smoke.mjs` now retries a sketch that reads silent, fails only if it
is silent twice, and **prints which sketches needed the retry**. The point of
printing is that the flake rate becomes a measured quantity instead of something
I remember badly: if a sketch starts needing the retry every run, that is a
regression showing itself rather than hiding inside a re-run.

It earned its keep immediately. This run printed `arc: silent first time,
retried` and then passed — so instead of a red suite I have a data point, and
`arc` has now needed the retry in three of the last five runs while nothing else
has needed it more than once. That is a much more specific accusation than "the
gate is flaky", and it points at `arc` being slow to start rather than at
contention: the retry is a second navigation, and it works every time. Worth
chasing on its own another day, now that the gate is recording the evidence
instead of me.

## What went wrong

Very little, which is unusual enough to be worth a note on why. The measurement
here had two things going for it that the last few days did not. The quantity
under test is an *integer*, so a broken detector produces residuals scattered
across [−0.5, 0.5] rather than plausible-looking numbers — there is no way to be
subtly wrong. And the tap sits on a worklet node, which always outputs, so
yesterday's silence-deleting capture bug could not arise; I checked that
deliberately rather than assuming it, having been caught by it once.

The one thing I got wrong was the output level, which read 0.044 on the first
full run — a factor of ten quiet, because I had guessed the loop's equilibrium
amplitude instead of measuring it. Cheap to fix and it invalidated nothing,
since the output gain sits outside the loop and cannot change what the loop
does. Worth noticing that the *reason* it was safe to change mid-experiment is
structural, not luck.

## Next

- [ ] Fractional delay. The delay is an integer number of samples, which makes
      the arithmetic exact but means the comb moves in jumps of its own at short
      delays. An allpass interpolator would let the pitch glide continuously
      within a tooth, and the phase condition already accounts for it.
- [ ] Two loops that share a signal rather than running in parallel — a cross-
      coupled pair has modes that are not the union of the two combs, and the
      prediction is an eigenvalue problem rather than a scan.
- [ ] A real room instead of one bandpass: a bank of resonances, so the comb is
      selected by an actual impulse response. That is Lucier's *I Am Sitting in
      a Room* with the loop closed, and the mode that wins is a genuine
      measurement of the room.
- [ ] Play the threshold rather than the delay. Sitting a hair under 1/|H|
      gives a loop that rings for seconds and dies — a decay time you can
      compose with, and one the arithmetic predicts.
- [ ] The saturator currently sets the amplitude and nothing else. A
      *frequency-dependent* nonlinearity would let the loop change tooth as it
      gets louder, which is what real feedback does when it climbs.
