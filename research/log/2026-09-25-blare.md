# 2026-09-25 — blare: the brightness is in the air, not in the reed

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/blare`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — bore length 0.35, push 0.55,
dynamics 0.7, seed 3, root 41 pentatonic major. The demonstration is
`Propagate linearly`: same notes, same dynamics, and the loud ones stop
blaring. `Hold one note` + `Test tone` puts a bare sine through the line so you
can walk `Push` up until σ passes 1 and watch the waveform go vertical.

## What I tried

Synthesis, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-20 with `chatter`. The
repo has four bores now — `overblow`, `cone`, `lattice`, and the waveguide in
`bow` — and **every one of them propagates linearly**. Their timbre belongs to
the excitation and the resonances, and playing louder only makes them louder.

Air does not work that way. A compression is hotter than a rarefaction, so it
travels faster, so the crest of a wave catches up with the trough ahead of it
and the waveform **shears** as it goes. Far enough and a sine is a sawtooth.
That is the whole of why a trombone blares when it is pushed and a flute never
does, and nothing here had it.

The lossless simple-wave (Poisson) solution is implicit:

```
u(σ, φ) = u₀(φ + σ·u)
```

and everything — amplitude, pitch, bore length — enters only through the one
dimensionless number σ = β·û·ω·x/c₀². So they are the *same knob*, which is a
claim with an experiment attached.

## What I measured

**The closed form is not what I implement, which is what makes it a test.**
Fubini (1935) gives the nth harmonic of an initially sinusoidal wave as
`(2/nσ)·Jₙ(nσ)`. The model solves the implicit equation by Newton; the worklet
solves it by a warm-started fixed point on a delay line whose read position
depends on the value being read. Neither contains a Bessel function.

In node, over harmonics 1–14:

| σ | worst absolute error | worst relative error |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.10 | 3.2e−15 | 2.4e−10 |
| 0.50 | 2.1e−16 | 5.3e−13 |
| 0.70 | 3.2e−15 | **3.8e−14** |
| 0.95 | — | 9.3e−4 |

The cosine components, which a wave odd about the origin must not have, came
out at **1e−16** at every σ — the check that nothing had drifted.

**The shock is visible in three places and they agree.** The steepest slope of
a simple wave should run as `1/(1−σ)` and go vertical at σ = 1:

| σ | 0.10 | 0.30 | 0.50 | 0.70 | 0.85 | 0.95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| steepest, measured | 1.111 | 1.429 | 2.000 | 3.333 | 6.666 | 19.984 |
| 1/(1−σ) | 1.111 | 1.429 | 2.000 | 3.333 | 6.667 | 20.000 |

That is the same σ = 1 where the fixed-point iteration stops contracting — the
contraction factor is |σ·u₀′|, so **the numerical method fails exactly where the
physics does**, which is a pleasing thing for a solver to do — and the same σ
where Fubini stops being the answer.

**Past the shock the brightness saturates.** The weak-shock law is a sawtooth,
`2/(n(1+σ))`, whose *shape* has no σ in it at all. So beyond σ = 1 the spectrum
stops getting brighter and only gets quieter: you cannot blow past a sawtooth.
Fubini and the sawtooth law do not meet at σ = 1 either (0.880 against 1.000 for
the fundamental) — there is a transition region between them that neither
describes, and I have not modelled it.

**Off the radiated sound.** Holding one note, projecting onto exact harmonics
with a Hann window, and dividing out the bell's known one-pole response — which
is part of the instrument but not part of the claim:

Nine settings, three different physical causes, one number, worst 0.01%.
**A high note blares at a dynamic where a low note does not** — nobody wrote
that rule in, it falls out of ω being in σ.

**The control could have failed and did not.** At the setting where the
nonlinear line puts 0.3644 of the fundamental into the second harmonic, the
linear one puts **6.1e−7**, and the third and fifth are 3.9e−8 and 1.2e−8. That
floor is the delay line's interpolation, not the physics.

Levels: **0.454** at the defaults, **0.836** worst over eleven settings.

## What went wrong

**A fractional step count in a quadrature, and the pattern gave it away.** The
Bessel integral is a trapezium rule over [0, π] with a step count I scaled with
n and x — and `256·(n + x)` is not an integer whenever x is not. The last grid
point then lands somewhere other than π and the endpoint term is applied in the
wrong place. Every J with an integer count came out to 1e−15 and every J with a
fractional one to 1e−3, which is what the recurrence check
`J_{n−1} + J_{n+1} = (2n/x)·Jₙ` said: 1.2e−15 at (10, 20), 2.0e−15 at (24, 24),
**5.6e−4** at (12, 8.4). One `Math.ceil`.

**And before that I nearly blamed the right thing for the wrong reason.** J₁₀(20)
came out 1.0e−4 from my "published" value, and my first instinct was quadrature
error. It did not move under a 64× increase in step count, and the recurrence
closed at 1e−15 — so the code was right and **my reference value was wrong**. It
cost one cheap test to find out, and would have cost a day of chasing a
non-existent bug otherwise.

**The level went as push squared.** `push` is the amplitude the worklet
synthesises at, and I had also put it in the envelope gain, so the output went
as its square and opened a 2.4× spread between the defaults and the corners.
Using `0.45 + 0.55·push` for the envelope closed it to 1.8×.

**A comb with feedback g rings at about 1/(1−g).** At the top of the `Bore ring`
range that is 33×, and the master read **2.317** — the only clipping in the
sketch's whole range and entirely in a knob that has nothing to do with the
physics under test. Scaling the drive by (1−g) made the bore's ring a timbre
instead of a level.

**Then the same measurement bug twice in one session.** The `Bore length`
slider rounds to its step, so a requested 0.0773 becomes 0.075 and comparing
against the requested σ read as a 2.87% model error. I fixed it by reading the
value back — for that one control. The next run had `Root` clamped at its
maximum of 56 when I asked for 60, so the harness projected the spectrum at
261.6 Hz where nothing was playing and reported the model **92.36%** wrong.
**Read back every control, not just the one that already bit you.**

## Next

- [ ] **The transition region, 1 < σ < 3.** Fubini stops at the shock and the
      sawtooth law only takes over well past it, and the two do not meet —
      0.880 against 1.000 at σ = 1. What the fixed point does in between is
      whatever it does, not what the physics says, and shock-fitting by the
      equal-area rule is the honest version.
- [ ] **Steepening should be cumulative over round trips**, not a single pass.
      A real bore's wave brasses a little more each time it goes up and back, so
      a long instrument at a given loudness should be brighter than a short one
      at the same pitch — which is the trombone/trumpet difference and is one
      delay line away.
- [ ] Put the line inside `lattice`'s tone-hole bore. Its cutoff is set by the
      holes and its brightness would then be set by the air, and whether the
      steepening pushes energy *past* the lattice cutoff is a sharp question
      about whether a loud clarinet is loud or bright.
- [ ] **A real lip valve.** The driver here is a one-sided clip standing in for
      a lip — honest but not a model. A self-oscillating outward-striking valve
      would let the *pitch* bend with embouchure, which is what makes a brass
      instrument a brass instrument, and `rosin` and `bow` both want the same
      object.
- [ ] Losses. Real steepening competes with viscothermal absorption, and the
      ratio of the two is the Gol'dberg number — whether a bore reaches its
      shock before it damps is a single dimensionless comparison and it decides
      whether an instrument can blare at all.
- [ ] The **flute** side of this, which is the same sentence read backwards: a
      flute's jet is the nonlinearity and its bore is linear, so it saturates in
      amplitude instead of brightening. Nothing here does an air jet.
- [ ] Sweep σ against perceived brightness rather than harmonic number — the
      centroid of a 1/n series depends entirely on where you truncate it, which
      makes "brighter" a claim about bandwidth as much as about the wave.
