# 2026-08-26 — entrain: an ensemble with no conductor

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/entrain`
**Settings worth keeping:** PENDING

## What I tried

Improvisation/performance, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-20,
`groove`).

`conduct`, eleven days ago, gave an ensemble a leader and measured how far
behind it ran. This takes the leader away. Every player starts at its own
tempo, hears onsets from some of the others, and nudges itself — and the only
thing that differs between runs is **who can hear whom**.

That question has a sharp answer, which is the reason to build it rather than
assert it. Near agreement this is linear consensus on the listening graph, so
the tempo disagreement decays at a rate set by the graph's **algebraic
connectivity** λ₂ — the second-smallest eigenvalue of its Laplacian. Same
players, same ears, same correction strength; only the wiring changes, and the
wiring alone should predict the rate. At eight players the four shapes have
textbook λ₂ spanning 52×:

| | λ₂ | closed form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| line | 0.152 | 2(1−cos(π/n)) |
| ring | 0.586 | 2(1−cos(2π/n)) |
| star | 1.000 | 1 |
| all | 8.000 | n |

so eight musicians in a ring, listening only sideways, should take roughly
seventy times as long to agree as eight all listening to each other. That is a
claim about drum circles and it is measurable.

Two corrections, deliberately separate. Hearing a beat from someone I listen
to, I move my *next* beat toward theirs (phase), and I adjust my *period* by
how far they have drifted since I last heard them (tempo). The second is
exactly T_i += β(T_j − T_i), which is consensus and nothing else. Writing it as
a **drift** rather than as an offset is what makes it survive any phase
relationship — with the phase pull negative the players sit deliberately apart,
and an offset-based rule would read that as a tempo error and run away.

The simulation is event-driven and runs ahead of audio time inside the
transport callback: pop the earliest pending onset, schedule it against `e.time`,
let everyone who listens to that player update, advance it by its period.

## What I measured

**λ₂ is the graph's, not the sketch's.** The sketch finds it with a cyclic
Jacobi solver; the harness never asks, it uses the closed forms. They agree to
six decimals for all four shapes.

**The rate is the graph's.** Phase pull off, so the tempo rule is the only rule.
β chosen per graph to keep β·λ₂ small, since the true per-beat factor is
|1 − β·λ₂| and the rate is −ln of it:

| graph | λ₂ | β | rate/beat | **rate/β** | λ₂ | r |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| line | 0.1522 | 0.05 | 0.00767 | **0.153** | 0.152 | −1.0000 |
| ring | 0.5858 | 0.05 | 0.03006 | **0.601** | 0.586 | −1.0000 |
| star | 1.0000 | 0.05 | 0.05169 | **1.034** | 1.000 | −1.0000 |
| all | 8.0000 | 0.005 | 0.04178 | **8.356** | 8.000 | −1.0000 |

Four graphs 52× apart in λ₂, agreeing with it to within 4.5%, each a straight
line on a log axis to four decimal places. A chain of eight players really does
take fifty times as long to agree as eight who all hear each other.

The path row took three attempts, and the fix in the end was to stop deriving a
fit window and start sweeping it. The disagreement is a sum over eigenmodes each
decaying at its own λ, so the λ₂ rate is only visible once λ₃ has gone — and of
these four graphs the path is the only one whose λ₂ is not repeated:

    path P₈   0, 0.152, 0.586, 1.235, 2, 2.765, 3.414, 3.848   all distinct
    ring C₈   0, 0.586, 2, 3.414, 4, 3.414, 2, 0.586           λ₂ twice
    star S₈   0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 8                           λ₂ six times
    K₈        0, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8                           λ₂ seven times

so only the path has a λ₃ running ahead of its λ₂ — 3.85× ahead — and only the
path misbehaved. Sweeping the fit start over one 261 s run:

| window opens at | 0% | 15% | 30% | 45% | 60% | 75% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rate/β | 0.206 | 0.159 | **0.153** | **0.153** | **0.153** | **0.153** |
| r | −0.9498 | −0.9973 | −1.0000 | −1.0000 | −1.0000 | −1.0000 |

A rate still falling as the window moves later has a faster mode in it; a rate
that has stopped moving is the answer. That is self-diagnosing, which my earlier
approach — computing a start beat from an assumption about the initial mode
amplitudes — was not. Disagreement fell 1.48e-2 → 3.34e-9 s over the run, with
zero clipped corrections.

**And it is audible.** Five players, star, 26 s captured off the pre-limiter
master and split into one band per player by complex demodulation:

| player | Hz | scheduled | heard | matched | scatter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 220 | 52 | 52 | **52** | 0.1 ms |
| 1 | 370 | 52 | 52 | **52** | 0.1 ms |
| 2 | 659 | 52 | 52 | **52** | 0.1 ms |
| 3 | 1109 | 52 | 52 | **52** | 0.1 ms |
| 4 | 1760 | 52 | 52 | **52** | 0.0 ms |

at a constant 19 ms offset, which is the envelope detector's own lag. Every
onset the model scheduled is in the recording and nothing else is. The heard
beat then goes from a spread of **5.62e-3 s** over the first 8 seconds to
**1.74e-15 s** over the last 8 — they do not approximately agree, they agree.

**Do they land on the mean of where they started?** Consensus on a connected
undirected graph preserves the sum, so they should. Six players, 26 s:

| phase pull | graph | mean of naturals | agreed on | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | star | 0.267646 | 0.266892 | −0.282% |
| **0.18** | star | 0.267646 | 0.275628 | **+2.982%** |
| 0 | all | 0.267646 | 0.266556 | −0.407% |
| 0.18 | all | 0.267646 | 0.267679 | +0.012% |
| 0 | ring | 0.267646 | 0.267600 | −0.017% |
| 0.18 | ring | 0.267646 | 0.266780 | −0.323% |

With the phase term off the error is under half a percent everywhere. With it
on it reaches three percent. So the answer is yes, *but only when the tempo
rule is the only rule* — once the phase correction is also moving beats about,
the ensemble is no longer a pure consensus system and where it lands depends on
how it got there. The residual half-percent with phase off is the update
asymmetry: I update on hearing you, so a fast player is heard more often than a
slow one and pulls slightly harder while the spread is still large.

**Repulsion does not give an even spread.** This was a prediction and it was
wrong. Negative phase pull, eight players, 40 s, gaps around the beat:

| graph | pull | order r | gaps (even would be 0.125 eight times) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ring | −0.12 | 0.033 | 0.076 0.025 **0.372** 0.032 0.020 0.027 **0.426** 0.023 |
| ring | −0.30 | 0.230 | 0.327 0.075 0.003 0.162 0.205 0.177 0.029 0.021 |
| all | −0.12 | 0.154 | 0.083 0.021 0.052 **0.411** 0.021 0.004 0.071 **0.337** |
| all | −0.30 | 0.179 | 0.073 0.031 **0.301** 0.243 0.077 0.017 0.225 0.033 |

They scatter — the order parameter falls from 1.000 to 0.03–0.23 — but into
**clusters**, not a splay. The clearest row is ring at −0.12: two gaps of 0.37
and 0.43 with six tiny ones between, which is two tight groups about half a
beat apart. That is what pairwise repulsion should give and I should have
predicted it: "get away from the players you can hear" is satisfied by
anti-phase, and anti-phase for a whole ensemble means two camps, not an even
ring. An even splay is what you get from a rule that knows how many players
there are, and no player here knows that.

Musically the two-camp state is the better outcome anyway. It is a backbeat
nobody wrote.

## What went wrong

**A clip guard that fired on the one case it should have ignored.** I guarded
the phase correction against moving a beat into the past by clamping to
`t + 0.1 ms`. The algebra says the correction can never move a beat before `t`
— `next + φ(t − next) = (1−φ)·next + φ·t ≥ t` — so the guard should be an
assertion that never fires. It fired 141 to 1433 times per run, because any
player whose beat fell on the *same instant* as the one being heard triggered
it, and the clamp then walked that beat 0.1 ms later. All-to-all coupling
converges to exactly that state, which is why it was worst there. Clamping to
`t` instead took the count to zero in every configuration.

**A level that was right about half the piece.** Divided by sqrt(n), the master
hit **2.317** — because the sketch's whole subject is players *stopping* being
independent, and once they agree, n pings land on one instant and sum
coherently. Divided by n it read 0.084, because when they are scattered the
peak is one ping. Both conventions are correct for one half of the piece. The
gain now divides by `1 + (n−1)·r` with r the smoothed order parameter, so the
peak stays put while the texture changes — which is also the right musical
answer, since an ensemble should not get six times louder for agreeing.

**An offset estimator that reported its own tolerance.** The onset alignment
scanned offsets and kept the first one with the most hits. At a 30 ms
tolerance many offsets tie, so it returned the most negative of them and
reported a 29 ms "scatter" against a 30 ms window. Breaking ties on the
residual instead took the scatter to 0.1 ms.

**A count that made the detector look broken.** The model's onset log is a
rolling buffer that reaches back before the recorder was armed, so 77
"scheduled" onsets were compared against a 26 s capture containing 52. It read
as a third of the onsets being missed. Clipping the log to the capture window
gave 52 of 52.

**A hypothesis I invented and then killed.** The first rate table came out
11–22% fast on the sparse graphs and 9% slow on the complete one, and the size
of the error ordered perfectly by the graph's diameter — 7, 4, 2, 1. That is
exactly what within-beat propagation would look like: a player that has already
updated this beat hands its new period to the next listener before the beat is
out, so information crosses more than one hop per beat and the longest chain
gains most. It is a good story and it is wrong. A structural effect like that
would not shrink when β shrinks. Running every graph again at β/3 took the
excess from 15.5% to 2.7% (ring) and 16.2% to 3.7% (star), so it was the
discrete-time correction all along: the true per-beat factor is |1 − β·λ₂| and
the rate is −ln of it, which only equals β·λ₂ when β·λ₂ is small.

The diameter ordering was a coincidence of four points. I had the test that
distinguishes the two before I had the story, which is the only reason the
story did not end up in the notes.

**And the fit had to start later than I thought.** At β/3 the *line* went the
other way — 403% fast — and the reason is in the spectrum rather than the
dynamics. The disagreement is a sum over eigenmodes each decaying at its own λ,
so the λ₂ rate is only visible once λ₃ has died. Of these four graphs only the
path has a λ₃ faster than its λ₂:

    path P₈   0, 0.152, 0.586, 1.235, 2, 2.765, 3.414, 3.848   all distinct
    ring C₈   0, 0.586, 2, 3.414, 4, 3.414, 2, 0.586           λ₂ repeated
    star S₈   0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 8                           λ₂ repeated
    K₈        0, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8                           λ₂ repeated

and the path is precisely the row that misbehaved. The fit now starts once the
λ₃ component has fallen to 1% of the λ₂ one, which is `ln(100)/(β(λ₃−λ₂))`
beats and is 20 for every graph whose λ₂ is repeated.

## The recurring failure, day twenty-five

Not invention, and the known-answer channel worked again — the detector was
checked against the model's own schedule before a single tempo number was
believed, and it came back 52 of 52 at 0.1 ms.

The new thing today is that I caught myself *building a theory to explain a
measurement error*. The diameter story was plausible, ordered all four data
points correctly, and was completely wrong. What saved it was that the
distinguishing experiment — does the excess survive shrinking β — was cheap and
I ran it before writing anything down. The rule this suggests: when a residual
has a tidy explanation, the explanation should come with the experiment that
would kill it, in the same breath.

## Next

- [ ] Delay. Real players hear each other late, and consensus with delay is a
      different system: past some coupling strength it oscillates instead of
      converging. That is the flutter a big ensemble gets in a live room and
      it is one parameter away here.
- [ ] A section leader, properly: a directed graph where one player listens to
      nobody. The Laplacian stops being symmetric, the sum is no longer
      preserved, and the ensemble should converge on the leader's tempo rather
      than the mean — a different prediction from the same machinery.
- [ ] Let the listening graph change while it plays. Musicians look up and
      look away; λ₂ becomes a function of time and the convergence rate should
      track it.
- [ ] Weight the edges by how loud each player is, so the ensemble's wiring is
      a mix decision. Then λ₂ is something you can perform.
- [ ] Feed a real player in through MIDI as an extra node with an edge to
      whoever they choose. The measurement stays exactly the same.
- [ ] Two graphs at once — tempo consensus on one, phase on another. There is
      no reason the ear you use to tell *when* has to be the ear you use to
      tell *how fast*.
