# 2026-09-12 — hollow: an instrument that is silent until you take something away

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/hollow`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 8 voices per bank, 3 banks, F lydian
from root 53, seed 4. The demonstration is one slider: set `Banks` to 1, turn
`Play itself` off, and walk `Silence this many` from 0 up to 8. The note appears,
swells, and is gone again before you reach the end, without its level ever being
touched. `Opposite pair instead` is the other one — two voices silenced and
nothing at all comes out.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange. Strictly the stalest family was sequencing/rhythm (2026-09-07,
`tongues`) with strange one day behind it, but yesterday's `elastic` was a rhythm
sketch in everything but its declared family, so this is the more honest reading
of "a family that hasn't come up recently".

N oscillators at one frequency with their phases spread evenly around the
circle. Their sum is exactly zero — Σ e^(2πik/N) = 0 for any N ≥ 2 — so a bank
of them running flat out makes no sound at all. You play it by **muting**:
silence one voice and what comes out is that voice, inverted. The note you hear
is the one you did not play.

`tartini` built a melody that is not in the signal at all, carried by distortion
the ear manufactures. `veil` built one that is in the signal and not in the ear.
This is the third corner of that set: **present in the signal, absent from the
score**.

The cancellation happens in the audio graph rather than in arithmetic here.
Every voice is a real `OscillatorNode` whose phase is baked into a
`PeriodicWave` — `sin(ωt + φ)` is `imag[1] = cos φ, real[1] = sin φ`, with
normalisation disabled — and all of them are started at one instant. So how deep
the silence goes is something to measure rather than assume.

## What I measured

**There are two kinds of nothing, and they measure differently.**

| | RMS | relative to one voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a full bank, all 8 running | 5.66e−9 | **−150.1 dB** |
| all 8 silenced | **0.00e+0** | nothing is playing |

The first is eight oscillators cancelling, and −150 dB is the audio graph's own
arithmetic floor. The second is absence. They sound identical and they are not
the same thing, which is the whole sketch in two rows.

**Muting more can make it quieter.** A contiguous run of m silenced voices
leaves a sum whose magnitude is a Dirichlet kernel, |sin(πm/N)/sin(π/N)|.
Measured off the recording, normalised to its own maximum so only the shape is
being compared:

| silenced | measured | closed form | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | — |
| 1 | 0.3829 | 0.3827 | 0.0002 |
| 2 | 0.7074 | 0.7071 | 0.0003 |
| 3 | 0.9244 | 0.9239 | 0.0006 |
| 4 | **1.0000** | 1.0000 | 0.0000 |
| 5 | 0.9247 | 0.9239 | 0.0008 |
| 6 | 0.7072 | 0.7071 | 0.0001 |
| 7 | 0.3830 | 0.3827 | 0.0003 |
| 8 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | — |

Worst error **0.0008**. The curve is not monotone in how much of the instrument
you switch off, and silencing m is as loud as silencing N−m — measured 0.03% to
0.07% apart for each of the three pairs.

**Some chords are silent.** On an even bank, silencing a diametrically opposite
pair leaves nothing, because the two removed voices cancel each other rather than
the bank. Measured at **−150.2 dB** — the full bank's floor exactly — where two
*adjacent* voices come out at +5.3 dB. Two voices played and nothing heard.

**And it is extremely fragile, which is the point.** The identity rests on the
phases staying locked, so detuning one voice turns silence into a beat at the
difference frequency:

| detune | predicted f·(2^(c/1200) − 1) | measured | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 cents | 0.404 Hz | 0.403 Hz | 0.2% |
| 8 cents | 0.809 Hz | 0.806 Hz | 0.3% |
| 16 cents | 1.621 Hz | 1.613 Hz | 0.5% |

Four cents — a twenty-fifth of a semitone, well under what anyone would call out
of tune — is enough to make a bank that should be silent breathe once every two
and a half seconds. That fragility is not a defect of the implementation. It is
what the identity costs, and it is why this is not how anybody builds a synth.

Levels: 0.629 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**I walked into a trap I had already written down.** The beat-rate detector
autocorrelated the envelope and took the largest peak, and at 16 cents it
returned 0.270 Hz against a predicted 1.621 — exactly six times too slow,
because a periodic envelope correlates just as well at two and three and six
times its period. `ideas.md` has carried this since `bow`: *"autocorrelation
alone never establishes which period is fundamental"*, logged after it happened
twice in one day. It is written under the sketch it happened to, which is
apparently not where I look when writing a new detector.

The fix is the standard one — take the *first* peak that is nearly as good as the
best rather than the best — and it moved 16 cents from 83% wrong to 0.5% right
while leaving 4 and 8 cents alone, which is how a period-multiple error behaves:
it only bites once the capture holds several cycles.

What made it visible was having a predicted number for all three detunes. The
4-cent and 8-cent rows were right, so a single measurement would have looked
fine; it took a third point far enough along the range for the ambiguity to
appear at all.

## Next

- [ ] Sweep the whole of it: the amplitude of an arbitrary silenced *subset* is
      |Σ e^(2πik/N)| over that subset, so every subset of a bank is a chord with
      a computable loudness, and most of them are not runs. There are 2^N of
      them and the silent ones are a subgroup.
- [ ] Phases that are not evenly spaced. Any set summing to zero will do, and
      the non-uniform ones would make the loudness landscape asymmetric — some
      voices worth more than others, which is a better instrument.
- [ ] Cancel across *pitches* rather than within one. Two banks a beat apart
      cancel only at one instant, so the silence would come and go on its own.
- [ ] The fragility as the instrument: a slow random walk on one voice's tuning,
      so the bank never quite manages to disappear. That is closer to how a real
      ensemble fails to cancel and is probably the more beautiful version.
- [ ] What sets the −150 dB floor? It should be float32 in the summing bus, in
      which case it would move with the number of voices and with their
      amplitude, and both are testable.
