# 2026-09-29 — stagger: the three-part hocket, and one head start is all it takes

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/stagger`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 3 voices, head start, seed 5, root 47
pentatonic minor, decay 0.36. The demonstration is `Sequences`: `flat` plays
homogeneous Beatty sequences at irrational densities off the seed, which is
exactly what Uspensky forbids, and you can hear it fail. Six voices gives a
period of 63 with the fastest voice on every other pulse.

## What I tried

Sequencing and rhythm, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-23 with
`vuza`. The idea has been sitting in `research/ideas.md` since `hocket`:

> Uspensky's theorem in `hocket` says no three parts can share a pulse this
> way, but that is for *homogeneous* Beatty sequences. `floor(n·r + s)`, with an
> intercept, does admit partitions into three or more — which ones is Fraenkel's
> conjecture, open for six parts and up. A three-part hocket that works.

`hocket` established both halves of Beatty's theorem: two voices at densities
d and 1−d hit every step exactly once, and three or more cannot. `nest` went
round that with a tree of two-way splits, at the cost that its voices stopped
being Beatty sequences at all. This is the smaller way round, and it needs one
extra number per voice.

## What I measured

Everything is `sketches/stagger/beatty.ts`, run in node before anything sounded.

**The search is exhaustive, not a sample.** Every partition of a period into
voices with distinct densities, each voice a Beatty sequence with any head
start, is enumerable exactly: the densities must sum to 1, a rational-modulus
sequence repeats with the period, and the head start takes finitely many
meaningfully different values. Over every period from 4 to 24 and 262
three-voice density splits:

| period | 4–6 | **7** | 8–13 | **14** | 15–20 | **21** | 22–24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| partitions found | 0 | **7** | 0 | **7** | 0 | **7** | 0 |
| densities | — | 1:2:4 | — | 2:4:8 | — | 3:6:12 | — |

Only multiples of 7, always the ratio 1:2:4, and the seven at each are the seven
rotations of one partition — so there is one, up to turning it. At four voices:
nothing at all until 15, then 1:2:4:8. That is Fraenkel's `2^i/(2^m − 1)`,
arrived at by exhaustion rather than looked up, and the conjecture that this is
*all* there is has been open since 1973.

**It tiles exactly at every voice count.** Over 200,000 pulses, 2 to 7 voices:
**0 unowned and 0 doubled**, six of six. Building the partition costs 1 ms at
three voices and 239 ms at seven, densest-voice-first.

**And the control is the theorem itself.** Homogeneous Beatty sequences at
*irrational* densities — which matters, see below:

| voices | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| best defect rate, flat | **1 in 200,000** | 0.2195 | 0.2837 | 0.4097 |
| staggered | 0 | **0** | **0** | **0** |

Two voices partition exactly — Rayleigh's theorem, and `hocket`'s result. The
three-voice figure is the best of 4,000 random density sets and never comes
near zero; `hocket`'s own 40-restart search reached 0.2400 on the same
impossibility, so two independent searches agree there is nothing there.

**The voices are real Beatty sequences, and `nest`'s were not.** The
three-distance theorem gives a genuine Beatty rhythm **at most two** distinct
inter-onset gaps. Measured:

```
3 voices:  [7]  [3,4]  [1,2]
5 voices:  [31] [15,16] [7,8] [3,4] [1,2]
```

Every voice has one or two. `nest` measured 3, 4, 7 and 8 distinct gaps, because
a Beatty sequence *of* a Beatty sequence is not one. Same impossibility, two
ways round it, and they are told apart by counting gaps.

**Off the sound, one note per pulse.** Each voice has its own pitch, so
projecting each pulse window onto each voice's frequency counts how many voices
played it:

| voices | sequences | 0 voices | 1 voice | 2+ | defects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | head start | 0 | **240** | 0 | **0.0%** |
| 4 | head start | 0 | **240** | 0 | **0.0%** |
| 5 | head start | 0 | **240** | 0 | **0.0%** |
| 3 | flat | 75 | 106 | 59 | 55.8% |
| 5 | flat | 96 | 99 | 45 | 58.8% |

Levels: **0.492** at the defaults, **0.770** worst over eleven settings.

## What went wrong

**My first control was not the theorem's control.** I compared the staggered
partition against homogeneous Beatty at the same *rational* densities, and it
failed at two voices as well — 66,667 unowned and 66,666 doubled — which looked
like a result and is not one. Rayleigh's theorem needs the densities
**irrational**: `⌊3n⌋` and `⌊1.5n⌋` fail for their own reasons, nothing to do
with how many voices there are. The rational comparison would have "shown" that
two voices cannot hocket either, contradicting `hocket`, in a table I would have
published.

**Then one number took three fixes, and each exposed the next.** Five voices
read 12.9% defects off the audio where the model says zero:

1. Each voice is a fundamental plus a partial at exactly **2.0×** — which lands
   on the fundamental of any voice an octave above, and no detector can tell
   that from a note. 31 false doubles in 240.
2. Moving the partial off the octave, to a ratio a quarter-tone from every
   equal-tempered interval, did not fix it: the new partial landed **16 Hz**
   from a different voice's fundamental, closer than a 144 ms window can
   resolve. The real fix is not in the instrument. A partial is at most 0.39 of
   its own fundamental, so a threshold of 0.5 cannot see one, and at 0.3 it was
   counting them as notes.
3. Raising the threshold then broke the three-voice case, 138 pulses reading
   empty — because the tap captured **channel 0 only** and these voices are
   panned across the field, so a right-panned voice reads a third of its
   amplitude and falls under any honest threshold. That is the trap `dichotic`
   documented, met from the other side.
4. And summing both channels to fix *that* inflated the level measurement to
   1.261. The peak that matters is per channel, because that is what the
   limiter and the DAC each see. Sum for attribution, larger of the two for the
   peak — one tap, two different right answers.

**And eighteen unescaped backticks.** The `notes` field is a template literal
and the prose is full of code spans; the first one ended the string. `tsc`
caught it, which is the cheapest possible place to find out, but the two-line
escape script I wrote then double-escaped them and produced a different syntax
error — a reminder that a fix applied in bulk needs reading back.

## Next

- [ ] **Six voices and up is where the conjecture is open.** The search here is
      complete to period 24 at three voices and 20 at four; extending it needs
      the pattern enumeration to stop being quadratic, and at six voices a
      counterexample would be a genuine result rather than a verification.
- [ ] Non-distinct densities, which Fraenkel's conjecture excludes and music
      does not. Two voices at the same density partition happily — the ruler
      sequence does it — so the conjecture's "distinct" is doing real work and
      dropping it opens a much larger space to compose in.
- [ ] **Put `stagger`, `hocket`, `nest` and `vuza` side by side.** Four
      partitions of time by four different mechanisms, and the gap counts tell
      them apart. That comparison has been asked for twice in this file and
      still has not been made.
- [ ] The head starts themselves are computed and then thrown away — only the
      owner table survives. Exposing `γ` per voice would make the sketch a tool
      for *finding* partitions rather than playing one.
- [ ] Irrational densities with intercepts, which is the general Fraenkel
      setting. Everything here is rational because the search needs a period;
      the irrational case has no period and needs a different argument
      entirely.
- [ ] A three-part hocket in *pitch*, which `dovetail` did for two hexachords
      and has a natural three-way version nobody asked about.
