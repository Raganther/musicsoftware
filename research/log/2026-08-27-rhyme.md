# 2026-08-27 — rhyme: a score with no copies

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/rhyme`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 32 slots, 5 rhymes, span 4,
everything allowed, free shape, seed 6. The demonstration is to drag a yellow
note and watch three or four blue ones move with it, then click a blue one and
be told which note owns it. `AABA` with `transpose` only is the cleanest thing
to listen to; `arch` with `transpose + invert` is the one that teaches you
something, because it pins a quarter of the piece flat and you can hear it.

## What I tried

Composition tools, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-22, `develop`).

Every tool in this repo works on a line. `cats-cradle` stores one melody as
intervals, `develop` searches between two motifs, `species` constrains a second
voice against a first. None of them has anything to say about **form** — about
bar 9 being bar 1 again up a fourth, and about what ought to happen to bar 9
when you change bar 1.

So: nothing is copied. A piece is N slots, and the only thing you write is a
**rhyme** — "this span is that span, transposed / inverted / backwards". A note
reached by a rhyme is not stored at all. It is derived.

The reason this is worth building rather than asserting is that it makes the
question *how much of this piece did you actually choose?* exact. In scale
degrees every rhyme is affine, with exactly two coefficients and both ±1:

    transpose    d[b+i] − d[a+i]      = k
    retrograde   d[b+i] − d[a+L−1−i]  = k
    invert       d[b+i] + d[a+i]      = 2c

so the score is a signed graph rather than a matrix, and the free notes are its
unpinned components — signed union-find, not elimination.

## What I measured

**Two solvers, one number.** The sketch's signed union-find against row
reduction over the rationals, across five shapes × ten seeds:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| free-note count agrees | **50 of 50** configurations |
| constraint rows holding in the realised score | **980 of 980** |
| rhymes refused | 66 — **63** genuinely contradictory, **3** needing a pitch between scale degrees, **0** refused without cause |

That last row is the one I care about most. It is easy to write a tool that
refuses things; it is the tool being *right* to refuse them that makes it a
composition aid rather than an obstacle, and every refusal was independently
confirmed infeasible.

**And it is right in the speakers.** Three configurations captured off the
pre-limiter master, pitched by YIN, with the score consulted only to say which
spans to compare:

| shape | notes identified | rhymes holding in the recording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AABA / transpose | 56 of 58 | **12 of 12** |
| arch / transpose + invert | 55 of 58 | **10 of 10** |
| canon / transpose + retrograde | 55 of 58 | **12 of 12** |

**What a rhyme costs.** 48 slots, mean of eight seeds:

| rhymes | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| free notes | 48 | 42.0 | 35.6 | 27.8 | 22.6 | 18.0 | 14.3 | 14.0 |
| bought per rhyme | — | 3.00 | 3.19 | 3.94 | 2.56 | 2.31 | 1.88 | 0.13 |

A rhyme of span 4 can remove at most four degrees of freedom and early ones
remove about three. By the fourteenth they remove almost nothing, because they
have started to overlap and mostly re-state each other. The tool says so
directly: those show up as *implied*, drawn faint.

**Too much symmetry flattens the music, literally.** A rhyme that closes a cycle
with opposite signs does not fail — it pins the component to the axis of its own
inversion, and every note in that component becomes one pitch. `arch` with
inversions pins 5.6 of 24 slots on average; free-form with everything allowed at
48 slots and 11 rhymes pins 14.8. You can hear it happen.

Peak 0.513 at the defaults.

## What went wrong

**A rhyme is one statement, and I accepted it a row at a time.** The first
version asserted each row of a rhyme independently and only called the rhyme
impossible if *every* row failed. So a rhyme with four workable rows and one
contradictory one was accepted, the union-find silently dropped the bad row, and
the score disobeyed the arc drawn over it — **146 of 1150 rows** in the first
run. Acceptance is now all-or-nothing: re-solve from scratch per candidate,
which is O(R²·L) on numbers that never leave two figures and far cheaper than
making the union-find undoable. 980 of 980 after.

**A pin can land between scale degrees.** Ask for a span to be its own inversion
about a half-integer axis and the only solution is a pitch that does not exist.
Rounding it would leave a score quietly disobeying the rhyme it is displaying,
so that is refused instead — and the oracle confirms it: 3 of the 66 refusals
are this and not contradiction.

**The oracle had to be told whose question it was answering.** After the atomic
fix, 27 of 50 configurations disagreed, always with the sketch reporting *more*
free notes. That was correct behaviour: my oracle was still greedily accepting
rows from rhymes the sketch had rejected wholesale. Which rhymes are accepted is
the tool's policy; how much freedom the accepted set costs is the mathematics,
and only the second is a claim about the world. Scoping the oracle to the
accepted set took it to 50 of 50, and checking the refusals became its own
separate measurement — a better one than the check I lost.

## The recurring failure, day twenty-six

The audio detector scored 65–85% and I spent three runs blaming it. I replaced
frequency-domain peak-picking with YIN, on a genuinely correct argument — root
55 puts this line at 123–330 Hz where a semitone is 7 Hz, and a 0.14 s Hann
window has a 14 Hz main lobe, so adjacent scale degrees sit inside each other's
leakage. The rewrite was right about the physics and changed the score by
nothing at all: **80.0 / 85.0 / 65.0 both times, to one decimal.**

Two unrelated algorithms failing *identically* is not a detector problem. It
cannot be. The fault was in the candidate list: I had built it from
`−reach…+reach`, and `reach` bounds the notes you **choose** and says nothing
about the notes **derived** from them, whose offsets carry them well outside it.
Notes with no candidate cannot be identified by any method. Fixing the list took
it to 95%.

So the measurement made the exact mistake the sketch exists to prevent —
treating a derived note as though it were a chosen one. I will take that as
evidence the tool is about something real, but the lesson is cheaper than that:
**when two independent methods give the same wrong answer, the bug is upstream
of both**, and rewriting either is time spent confirming they still agree. Three
of the last four days I have improved a detector that was not the problem.

## Next

- [ ] Rhymes over rhythm as well as pitch. Durations as ratios are affine in
      log time, so augmentation and diminution drop into the same solver.
- [ ] Let the user draw the rhyme rather than take what the seed gives —
      select two spans, pick a transform, watch the free-note count fall.
      Everything downstream already re-derives.
- [ ] Show *which* rhyme is about to make something impossible before it is
      added, so the tool warns rather than refuses.
- [ ] Second voice, with `species`'s rules as additional rows. Counterpoint and
      form in one constraint system is the obvious next altitude.
- [ ] A rhyme with a *tolerance* — "roughly this span, up a third" — which
      turns the exact solve into a least-squares one and is much closer to how
      music actually rhymes.
- [ ] Export the free notes alone as the piece's real score. That is the
      compression claim made concrete: hand someone eighteen notes and the
      rhyme list, and they have the whole thing.
