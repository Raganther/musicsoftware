# 2026-09-16 — tuplet: the rhythm you meant against the rhythm you can write

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/tuplet`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 5 notes from uniform reals, seed 3,
tuplets up to 7 one deep, shortest note 1/16, both voices playing. The
demonstration is `Nesting depth`: at 0 the two voices are 111 ms apart and you
hear two different rhythms; at 1 it is 6.5 ms and they are almost one; at 2 it
is 0.9 ms and they have merged. Nothing about the music changed — only how much
notation you were willing to spend on it.

## What I tried

Composition tools, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-11 with `elastic`.
`ideas.md` has carried the question since then: *"notation cost for `elastic`:
realised durations are reals and notation wants small denominators. How complex
a tuplet does a ratio set need, and which sets cannot be written at all?"*

`elastic` solves a rhythm from stated ratios and hands back real numbers.
Notation cannot express a real number. It expresses a dyadic fraction of a bar
scaled by a tuplet — n notes in the time of m — and tuplets nest, so everything
writable has the form 2^(−k)·Π(m_i/n_i). The denominator of anything you can
write is therefore a power of two times a product of the tuplet numbers you
allow, which is to say: **a duration is writable exactly when its denominator is
N-smooth.**

That makes notation a number-theoretic constraint, and the interesting question
becomes what it costs to get close to a rhythm you cannot write at all.

## What I measured

**The reachable set is the N-smooth set.** Checked against the definition of
smoothness for N = 3, 5, 7 and 11 over denominators up to 200 — the same set
every time, 4 of 4.

**Depth is the shortest factorisation of the odd part.** 9 needs two levels and
so does 49; 27 needs three; 11, 13 and 33 cannot be written at all with tuplets
up to 7.

**One tuplet is worth more than three halvings of the note value.** Worst
single-note error over 300 random five-note rhythms:

| shortest note | no tuplets | ≤3, 1 deep | ≤5, 1 | ≤7, 1 | ≤7, 2 | ≤7, 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/8 | 5.596% | 1.899% | 1.159% | 0.789% | 0.113% | 0.016% |
| 1/16 | 2.919% | 0.969% | 0.577% | 0.398% | 0.058% | 0.008% |
| 1/32 | 1.426% | 0.489% | 0.288% | 0.197% | 0.028% | 0.004% |
| 1/64 | 0.725% | 0.245% | 0.146% | 0.100% | 0.014% | 0.002% |

Halving the note value halves the error; one more tuplet level divides it by
about seven. So a triplet at eighth-notes (0.789%) is about level with plain
64th-notes (0.725%), and **two nested tuplets at eighth-notes beat 64th-notes by
six times** — for a notation a player can actually read.

In milliseconds at a two-second bar, which is the unit the ear works in:

| shortest note | no tuplets | ≤7, 1 deep | ≤7, 2 deep | ≤7, 3 deep |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/8 | **108.7 ms** | 16.1 ms | 2.3 ms | 0.3 ms |
| 1/16 | 58.6 ms | 8.1 ms | 1.1 ms | 0.2 ms |
| 1/32 | 28.9 ms | 4.0 ms | 0.6 ms | 0.1 ms |

Around 20–30 ms is where two onsets stop sounding simultaneous, so the top-left
cell is not a subtlety — it is a different rhythm.

**Notating is not rounding, because the bar has to close.** That is `elastic`'s
constraint arriving from the other side. Round each note independently to the
nearest writable value and the bar came out wrong in **161 of 400** random
rhythms. Handing the remainder to whichever note was rounded furthest fixes it
in 400 of 400, which is also what a person does.

**Smoothness costs about a factor of four.** Allowed *any* denominator up to
192, the best approximation to a random real averages 0.006%; allowed only the
smooth ones, 0.022%. The penalty grows with the budget — 1.17× at 12, 1.54× at
24, 1.89× at 48, 2.85× at 96, 3.87× at 192 — because large smooth numbers get
rarer the further out you go.

**What separates rhythms is exactness, not accuracy.** Durations drawn from
simple ratios are written *exactly* 105 times in 400. From uniform reals, 0 of
400. From powers of φ, 0 of 400. But when they are not exact they are all about
equally inexact: mean worst error 0.043%, 0.056%, 0.052%.

**And you can hear all of it.** Onsets read off a recording, folded over ten
bars, against the model:

| budget | den | model says | heard | heard vs model | next-best fold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no tuplets, 1/8 | 8 | 189.3 ms | 189.1 ms | 1.13 ms | 299 ms |
| no tuplets, 1/16 | 16 | 110.7 ms | 110.6 ms | 1.25 ms | 449 ms |
| ≤3 one deep, 1/8 | 24 | 42.2 ms | 43.3 ms | 1.13 ms | 399 ms |
| ≤7 one deep, 1/16 | 112 | 6.4 ms | 6.5 ms | 1.06 ms | 449 ms |
| ≤7 two deep, 1/16 | 784 | 1.1 ms | 0.9 ms | 1.18 ms | 443 ms |

The sketch plays its own notation to **1.25 ms at worst** over a 200× range, and
the alignment is unambiguous by a factor of 250. The detector is validated first
on three notes on a quarter grid, where every onset must land on an exact
quarter: worst **1.60 ms**.

With both voices sounding, the distinct onsets the ear gets per bar fall from
**7.9 to 6.6** as the gap closes from 102 ms to 1.1 ms — written and meant notes
merging into one. Directional rather than exact; at a 30 ms refractory the
detector cannot resolve the last of it.

Levels: 0.499 pre-limiter at the defaults, 0.671 at the loudest setting.

## What went wrong

**The budget was not binding and every row of the table looked the same.** The
first error sweep reported 0.092% for a rhythm written with *no tuplets at all*,
and 0.092% for every other budget too. Notation limits two things and I had
modelled one: the tuplet stack, but not the shortest note value. With the dyadic
part quietly allowed to run to 512nd notes there was nothing left for tuplets to
buy. A table whose rows are all equal is the same tell as `tongues` reading its
own resolution floor — the instrument, not the subject.

**The sketch answered with a denominator it had been told it could not have.**
Asked to write five notes with no tuplets at all, it returned denominator 5 —
which is a quintuplet. The cause was a fallback: when no reachable denominator
was large enough to hold the notes, it silently used the note count. But five
notes on a grid of quarters is not a rounding problem, it is a **refusal** —
there are four slots. `bestNotation` now says so, and the sketch says so.

The known-answer channel caught it on its first run, which is the whole reason
it was there. It also means the check I had written was itself badly chosen: I
asked for a case that has no answer and called the answer wrong. The replacement
asks for three notes on a quarter grid, which does fit, and the refusal became
its own separate test.

**The bar fold was rotated and the repeat check could not see it.** Onsets came
back 300–600 ms from the model while the bar-to-bar repeat was good to 1.1 ms —
because the capture starts mid-bar, so onset 0 is not note 0, and a rotated fold
repeats just as cleanly as an aligned one. A gate that passes on the broken case
is not a gate. Fixed with the same move `hocket` used: search every rotation and
report the runner-up as the control. The best is 1.1 ms and the next is 299 ms,
so there is nothing to argue about.

**My expectation about φ was wrong, and testing it was the only way to find
out.** φ is the hardest number to approximate by fractions, so I expected it to
be the most expensive rhythm to write. Over eight values of a two-note ratio it
comes out at 0.002% and e/2 at 0.027% — no ordering by approximability at all.
The classical result is about denominators *up to a bound*, and a tuplet budget
does not give you those; it gives you a sparse scatter of large smooth numbers,
where the continued-fraction structure of the target stops mattering. Whether it
is rational at all is the only thing that does.

**And a shell trap worth writing down:** `pkill -f tuplet-verify` matched its own
shell's command line and killed the whole compound command before the edit in it
ran. The file was unchanged, `npm run check` passed on the old code, and it took
a `grep` to notice nothing had happened.

## Next

- [ ] Point this at `elastic` directly. It produces real durations from stated
      ratios; this says what they cost to write. The pair is a complete answer
      to "can I notate what I just composed", and neither half knows about the
      other yet.
- [ ] Notate the whole *piece* rather than one bar. Tuplets have a scope, and a
      denominator chosen per bar is cheaper than one chosen for the movement —
      but changing it every bar is unreadable. That trade is the real problem a
      copyist solves.
- [ ] Irrational tempo relationships instead of irrational durations. A bar at
      1 : φ against another is `hocket`'s territory and is exactly the rhythm
      that has no notation; the usual answer is a tempo mark rather than a
      tuplet, which is a different escape hatch.
- [ ] Which *note* absorbs the remainder is currently "whoever was rounded
      furthest". A copyist would put it where it is least audible — on the
      longest note, or off the downbeat — and that is a rule worth measuring
      rather than asserting.
- [ ] Nested tuplets are not equally readable: 5-in-4 inside 7-in-4 is legal and
      nobody can play it. A readability cost that is not just depth would change
      every table here, and the honest version needs players rather than
      arithmetic.
- [ ] The smoothness penalty grows with the budget (1.17× to 3.87×). Where does
      it go asymptotically? Smooth numbers have a known density (Dickman's
      function), so this is predictable rather than merely measurable.
