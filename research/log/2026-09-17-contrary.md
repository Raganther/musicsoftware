# 2026-09-17 — contrary: two melodies in opposite directions, neither of them in the signal

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/contrary`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — mirror mode, A + B = 1200 Hz, span
0.62, root 50, seed 5. Two sine tones, one of which never moves at all. The
demonstration is `Reveal`: at 0 you hear two tones and nothing else; walk it up
and two melodies fade in going opposite ways, out of a signal that did not
contain them. `One carrier per ear` is the other one — both tunes vanish, because
the nonlinearity that makes them is in the ear and each ear now has only one
tone to work with.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-12 with `hollow`.
`ideas.md` has carried the question since `tartini`: *"two phantoms at once —
the quadratic and cubic products move in opposite directions, so one pair of
carriers could carry two melodies."*

`tartini` put one melody into a difference tone: present in the ear, absent from
the wire. Two primaries through any memoryless nonlinearity give a quadratic
difference tone and a cubic one:

    A = f2 − f1        B = 2·f1 − f2

Two equations, two unknowns, and they invert with nothing thrown away:

    f1 = A + B         f2 = 2A + B

So any pair of melodies can be carried by one pair of sines.

## What I measured

**The inversion is exact.** 20,000 random pairs round-tripped through
`carriers` and back through `phantoms`: worst error **0.00e+0 Hz**.

**Each melody comes from its own term of the nonlinearity.** In the model, with
only x²: A at −20.0 dB, B at −277.4 — the arithmetic floor. With only x³: A at
−271.0, B at −28.5. Switching a term off deletes one tune and leaves the other
standing. Repeated on a recording of the sketch, the same table:

| nonlinearity | A | B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| linear | −204.4 dB | −169.1 dB |
| quadratic only | **−29.5** | −169.5 |
| cubic only | −190.5 | **−42.7** |
| both | −29.5 | −42.7 |

**The two phantoms cannot move the same way.** ∂A/∂f2 = +1 against ∂B/∂f2 = −1;
∂A/∂f1 = −1 against ∂B/∂f1 = +2. Nudging either carrier in either direction
moved them oppositely, 4 of 4.

**Which turns the obstacle into the instrument.** Hold A + B fixed — which is
exactly strict contrary motion — and f1 = A + B never moves. Measured over 40
notes:

| | f1 moves | f2 moves | A moves | B moves | corr(A,B) | corr(f1,A) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| mirror | **0.0 Hz** | 114.8 Hz | 115 | 115 | **−1.000** | 0.000 |
| free | 201.8 Hz | 316.6 Hz | 115 | 115 | −0.748 | 0.371 |

One audible tone that does not move at all, one that slides in a single
direction, and two tunes going opposite ways underneath that no oscillator is
playing.

**Two genuinely free melodies would not hide, and that is a constraint rather
than a shortcoming.** The ear only makes these products when the carriers are
close; f2/f1 = (2A+B)/(A+B) is near 1.2 only when B ≫ A; and then f1 = A + B
simply follows B. Over 400 independent note pairs in that regime, f1 correlates
with B at **0.962** and f2 with B at 0.869 — one of the two melodies goes
straight into the audible signal. Mirrored melodies hide both, and mirrored ones
are the only ones that do.

**Nothing else lands on either tune.** Every intermodulation product
|m·f1 + n·f2| up to order five: the only ones at A are ±(f2 − f1), the only ones
at B are ±(2f1 − f2), and **no other product falls within 30 Hz** of either.

**From the sound.** Recorded off the sketch's own bus and bent *here* rather
than there, because the point is that the wire is linear:

| | in the recording | after bending |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | −164 to −211 dB | −30.6 dB |
| B | −164 to −171 dB | −43.9 dB |

**The bend brings them up by at least 120.4 dB.**

**Split the carriers between the ears and both tunes collapse.** The capture
takes one channel, so with one carrier per ear that channel holds one primary
and the products cannot form: f2 falls from −10.8 dB to −160.7, and after
bending A goes from −30.6 to **−177.3** and B from −43.9 to **−160.4**.
(`tartini` measured 38 dB of collapse for its single phantom; this is larger
because it is the signal path rather than a model of hearing.)

Levels: 0.464 pre-limiter at the defaults, 0.797 at the loudest setting.

## What went wrong

**My framing was wrong and the correlation check killed it.** I had written, in
the module's own doc comment, that the audible pair "is doing something that is
neither melody". Then I measured it: with two independent melodies **f1
correlates with B at 0.962**. The carriers track the higher tune almost
perfectly, and they have to — the ear needs f2/f1 ≈ 1.2, which forces B ≫ A,
which makes f1 = A + B mostly B. The romantic claim was false for exactly the
configuration the physics requires.

What saved it was that the constraint has a solution: hold A + B fixed, and the
thing that was leaking becomes the thing that stands still. The sketch is better
for the correction than it would have been for the claim, but I wrote the claim
into the code before measuring it.

**The "absent from the signal" floor was my own analysis window.** The first
audio run reported the melodies at −70 to −81 dB in the wire where the model
puts them at −273, and I started looking for a nonlinearity in the sketch's
signal path. The tell was in the validation line I had already printed: a
*control* frequency 40 Hz off a carrier read −66.5 dB. Two sine tones at −10.8 dB
through a rectangular window leak into every other frequency as sinc sidelobes
falling only as 1/Δf, and that leakage was the entire floor. A Hann window
dropped the control from −66.5 dB to **−136.4** and the melodies to −164 to
−211, which is the real answer.

Worth pairing with the opposite lesson from `sitting`: *do not* window an impulse
response, because it keeps its energy at t = 0. A steady tone is not an impulse
response, and here the window is the whole measurement.

**The reveal control measured nothing, because I tapped the wrong node.** The
sketch exposes `tap()` for the harness, and it returns the bus *before* the
WaveShaper. So `Reveal` at 0 and at 1.0 read −81.9 and −82.9 dB — no effect at
all, which I nearly wrote up as "the reveal is too weak". It is exactly right;
the harness was listening upstream of it. `tapOut()` now exists for what the
sketch actually emits, and the reveal goes −143.9 → −38.7 → −30.3 → −22.3 dB.

**And a scheduling one:** at 4 beats a minute over 2 steps a note lasts 3 s, and
a 2 s capture plus its overheads takes 3.05 s of wall clock — so the "hold still
for one note" check almost never passed, and one whole condition reported
`no steady note` eight times running. The capture has to be comfortably shorter
than the thing it is trying to catch, and "comfortably" means counting the
overheads, not the nominal duration.

## Next

- [ ] A third phantom. 3f1 − 2f2 is the next cubic product and is a third
      melody with its own derivative; three tunes from two tones would need
      A, B, C to satisfy one linear relation, which is a compositional
      constraint nobody has ever had to write under.
- [ ] Make the mirror axis move. A + B is fixed here, which fixes f1; letting it
      glide slowly means f1 drifts while the two tunes keep their contrary
      motion about a moving centre. That is a real form and it is one line.
- [ ] Whether any of this is *heard* is unmeasured, exactly as `veil` left it.
      The wire is clean to −164 dB and the bend recovers the tunes; whether a
      listener follows two contrary lines rather than one blurred one is a
      question this cannot answer from inside.
- [ ] The mirror melody is A reflected in *frequency*, so it sits in the scale's
      arithmetic mirror rather than the scale. Choosing A so that S − A also
      lands on scale degrees constrains S to a few values per scale, and those
      values would be worth knowing.
- [ ] `tartini` and this one now both need a carrier pair in a narrow ratio
      band. A helper that says, for a given melody range, which sums are
      playable at all would serve both — and the answer is a wedge in (A, B)
      that has never been drawn.
- [ ] Level matters and is not exposed: the cubic difference tone grows faster
      than the quadratic one with primary level, so loudness should change the
      *balance* of the two melodies. One param, and a real compositional
      dimension.
