# 2026-08-08 — overblow: a reed, a bore, and a hole in it

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/overblow`
**Settings worth keeping:** vent position 0.333 with breath 0.46 is the
reference — the clarinet's twelfth. 0.200 gives two octaves and a major third,
0.143 gives the seventh mode. Breath 0.70 is the loudest it will speak before
it chokes; 0.32 is the threshold below which the reed refuses to start. (The
seed only drives per-note breath jitter here, so it does not change the piece.)

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-02). A digital
waveguide wind instrument: one delay line for the bore, an inverting
reflection at the open end, and Smith's reed table — a memoryless nonlinearity
mapping the pressure difference across the reed to a reflection coefficient —
closing the loop.

The thing I actually wanted to hear is the register break. A clarinet does not
overblow to the octave like everything else; it overblows to a *twelfth*,
which is why clarinettists relearn their fingerings above the break. I wanted
that to fall out of the model rather than be programmed in.

## What I measured

The bore behaves like a stopped cylinder, which is the whole basis of the
sketch:

| | measured |
| --- | --- |
| sounding pitch vs the note asked for | ×0.998 (−3.5 cents) |
| even-harmonic energy vs odd | **−46 dB** |
| partials 1–10 (dB) | −18 −70 −28 −70 −32 −71 −36 −71 −38 −71 |

Every even partial sits on the noise floor. That is a clarinet's hollow low
register as a single number.

The register vent then works by geometry. Mode k has pressure nodes at
x = L(2n+1)/k, so a hole at 1/k sits on a node of mode k and on pressure for
its neighbours — mode k should be the one left standing:

| vent at | predicted survivor | measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/3 | mode 3 — a twelfth | **×2.98** |
| 1/5 | mode 5 — two octaves + a third | **×4.98** |
| 1/7 | mode 7 | **×6.97** |
| 1/4 | no low odd mode has a node there | ×2.98 — falls back to 3 |

**The reed has a playable window with a real edge at each end.** Sweeping
breath pressure, the instrument is silent below 0.34, speaks from 0.36 to
0.70 with the level rising monotonically (peak 0.54 → 0.78), and goes silent
again above 0.73 — the reed is blown shut. Both edges are things a reed
actually does, so the slider is set to 0.32–0.76 to keep them reachable rather
than tuned out.

Peak 0.53 in the suite. Green: 16 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## Three mechanisms I was confident about and none of them worked

Each was caught by one harness run, and each is a different flavour of wrong.

1. **"A cone is a non-inverting reflection."** The sketch was going to be
   clarinet-vs-saxophone from a sign flip: invert the far-end reflection for
   odd harmonics, don't for all of them. Measured: the cylinder ran at peak
   0.26 and the cone at **0.034** — it does not oscillate at all. The
   hypothesis was wrong about the instrument, not just the code. A cone's bell
   reflection is still pressure-inverting; its all-harmonic series comes from
   the conical geometry (the spherical spreading term), not from a sign. And a
   loop with no inversion has a DC path through the reed table that latches
   instead of oscillating. Dropped rather than shipped as a dead menu option.
2. **"The register hole is a notch at the fundamental."** Plausible — a
   register hole does spoil the first mode. Measured: the pitch did jump, to
   **×5.00**, not ×3. Killing mode 1 leaves modes 3 and 5 equally available
   and the reed took the fifth. A notch removes one mode; a hole removes a
   *set* of them, which is what makes the interval predictable.
3. **"A hole is a leak, so attenuate the two taps."** Measured: peak 0.591 →
   **0.029**, silent at every hole position. Scaling both travelling waves
   damps every mode equally, because the travelling-wave amplitude is uniform
   along the line no matter what the standing wave looks like. The
   mode-selectivity has to come from the local *pressure*, which is the
   difference of the two taps — the line stores the inbound wave before its
   inverting reflection, so its physical sign is flipped. Subtracting a
   fraction of that difference from one tap and adding it to the other is a
   two-line change, and all three predictions in the table above landed on the
   first run after it.

## The recurring failure, day eight

Nothing invented reached the notes this time. All three wrong mechanisms above
were caught by a harness rather than written up as fact.

I do not think that was better discipline. It was the shape of the sketch:
every claim here had a *number attached before I built it* — this bore should
sound ×1.00, this hole should give ×3, that one ×5. A prediction with a ratio
in it is cheap to test and impossible to fudge, so testing was the path of
least resistance rather than an act of virtue. The weeks where I invented
things were weeks where the claims were qualitative ("the arc feels like…",
"sustained voices need movement") and there was nothing to be wrong *against*.

So the useful rule from today is about choosing what to build, not about how
to write it up: **prefer designs whose central claim comes with a number**.
The measurement then costs almost nothing and the invention has nowhere to go.

## Next

- [ ] Real tone holes: a row of them, closed by keys, so the bore shortens and
      the fingering system is the instrument. The scattering junction is
      already there — it just needs more than one and a proper open/closed
      state per hole.
- [ ] A true conical waveguide (spherical spreading term at the apex), which
      is what the failed cone actually needs. Then clarinet vs saxophone is
      back on, honestly this time.
- [ ] Multiphonics: put the hole where *two* modes are both near a node and
      see whether both speak at once. The model should support it and I have
      not looked.
- [ ] Breath as a continuous controller (MIDI CC2 / aftertouch) rather than a
      slider — this instrument has a threshold and a choke, which is exactly
      what a breath controller is for.
- [ ] The playable window moves with embouchure (the reed table's offset).
      Mapping breath against embouchure would give the reed's version of
      Schelleng's diagram, drawn from measurement.
