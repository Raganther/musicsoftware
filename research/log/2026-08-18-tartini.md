# 2026-08-18 — tartini: a melody that is not in the signal

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/tartini`
**Settings worth keeping:** seed 5, D pentatonic minor, cubic, carrier f1
2000 Hz, glide 0.12 — a slow six-note figure whose phantom traces
A4 G4 F4 D5 C5 F5 G5. The demonstration is the pair of A/B switches, not the
tune: drag `Reveal` up and down to hear what you are listening for, and turn
`Split the ears` on and off, which should make the melody vanish and return.
Carrier 1200 Hz with balance near 1.0 is the loudest the phantom gets;
carrier 3600 puts both primaries high enough that on small speakers you may
hear nothing at all, which is its own kind of demonstration.

## What I tried

Wild/strange, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-12, veil).

`veil` hid a real melody inside noise. This is the inverse: a melody that was
never there. Play two loud sine tones and a third, lower tone appears that
nothing is emitting — Tartini described it in 1714 as *il terzo suono*, and it
is a distortion product manufactured by the nonlinearity of the cochlea. So
hold one carrier fixed and move the other so that the distortion product traces
a tune. Two steady whistles come out of the speaker. The music happens inside
the listener.

The cubic product is the one worth building on. Solving 2·f1 − f2 = m for f2
gives f2 = 2·f1 − m, so as the phantom melody rises the carrier you can
actually hear *falls*. You are listening to a contour whose audible shadow is
its own mirror image, which is a genuinely odd thing to sit with.

## What I measured

Everything by Goertzel at named frequencies rather than by peak search, because
the interesting number is the level at a place where there is supposed to be
nothing, and a peak finder cannot report that.

**The melody is not in the signal.** Over seven notes, the level at the
phantom's own frequency ran between **−84 and −191 dB** relative to the
carriers. Through x + 0.3x² + 0.3x³ the same samples give **−39 to −47 dB** —
a gain of 43 to 150 dB from a nonlinearity alone.

The control is the part that makes that number mean something. `Reveal` puts a
real tone at exactly the phantom frequency, and the same measurement then
reads:

| reveal | level at the phantom frequency |
| --- | --- |
| 0.0 | −79.6 dB |
| 0.5 | −16.4 dB |
| 1.0 | −10.3 dB |

So −80 dB is absence, not blindness.

**Each term makes the product it should:**

| nonlinearity | f2 − f1 | 2·f1 − f2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| linear | −150 dB | −177 dB |
| square only | **−27 dB** | −166 dB |
| cube only | −158 dB | **−43 dB** |
| both | −27 dB | −43 dB |

**Split the ears, and it goes.** With both primaries in the left channel, a
nonlinear left ear gives the phantom at −45 dB. Split, f2 in the left channel
falls to −164 dB and the phantom to −83 dB — a 38 dB collapse. That is the
sketch's own toggle, and it is the experiment that would falsify the whole
premise if the melody survived it.

**The phantom lands on notes.** 14 of 14 readings sat on a tone of D
pentatonic minor within 12 cents, worst deviation **0.2 cents**. And the
inversion holds: the phantom rose while the audible carrier fell in **9 of 9**
changes.

Pre-limiter peak 0.478 at the defaults, 0.618 with reveal at maximum, 0.477 in
the suite. Green: 25 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## The thing I am not claiming

Whether a listener hears any of this is unmeasured and unmeasurable from here.
Distortion products are strongly level-dependent and vary between people; too
quiet and there is nothing to hear, and a laptop speaker may not deliver a
2 kHz pair at a useful level in the first place. Everything above is about the
signal and about what a nonlinearity does to it. The perceptual claim is the
reason the sketch exists, not a finding — same standing as `veil`'s, and worth
being just as careful about.

There is a second, subtler place I could have overclaimed. The square/cube
table shows f2 − f1 arriving 16 dB louder than 2·f1 − f2, and it would be easy
to write that up as "the quadratic product dominates". It does not mean that:
both coefficients were 0.3 because I typed 0.3, and the ratio between the
columns is a fact about my curve. The real cochlea is predominantly odd-order,
which is why 2·f1 − f2 is the one that turns up in otoacoustic emission
measurements and why it is the default here. What the table actually
establishes is that the harness attributes each product to the right term —
which is worth having, but is arithmetic rather than physiology.

## The recurring failure, day seventeen

Clean. The pattern that has been running since day five — writing numbers
before measuring them — did not appear, and for the first time the reason was
structural rather than vigilant: the sketch is *about* a level at a specific
frequency, so there was no version of it I could have built without measuring
that level first. The measurement was the design.

The near-miss was the one above, and it is the same shape as day fourteen's:
not an invented number but an invented reading of a real one. A true number
(−27 vs −43 dB) attached to a claim it does not support (about ears rather than
about my polynomial). What caught it this time was asking where each input to
the measurement came from — and two of them came from me, arbitrarily.

Worth recording as method rather than as failure: the `Reveal` control was
built as a musical affordance and turned out to be the single most valuable
line in the harness, because it converts "I measured nothing" into "I measured
nothing, and here is the same measurement finding something". Almost every
absence claim in this repo could use one, and `veil` in particular should have
had it.
