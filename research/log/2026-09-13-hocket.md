# 2026-09-13 — hocket: two players can share a pulse with one number between them, and three can never

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/hocket`, `src/core/theory.ts` (+ `irrational`
and `tongues`, which now import from it)
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — share 0.618034, two voices, E
pentatonic minor from root 52, seed 7. The demonstration is `Solo voice`: set it
to 1 and you hear a limping rhythm, set it to 2 and you hear a different limping
rhythm, set it to 0 and the two of them are a perfectly even stream of notes.
`Snap to q ≤` is the other one — walk it 0, 8, 5, 4, 2 and the tune loses an
eighth, then a fifth, then a quarter, then half of itself, with nothing in the
level touched.

## What I tried

Sequencing and rhythm, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-07 with
`tongues`, six days ago, and with nothing competing this time.

A hocket is the medieval trick of splitting one melody between two players so
that each rests through the other's notes. `ideas.md` has carried the question
since `irrational`: *"two voices at densities α and 1−α: between them they hit
every step exactly once, which is a tiling canon with an irrational rhythm —
something `tiling` cannot express."*

Give a voice of density d the steps `floor(n/d)` — a Beatty sequence — and
Rayleigh's theorem (1894) says two voices at d and 1−d hit every step exactly
once, **iff d is irrational**. The interlock needs no negotiation at all: one
number, and the parts fit forever without either player knowing what the other
is doing.

`irrational` built one such word and asked what it looks like alone — complexity
n+1, maximally even, an apparent period set by how well you can approximate d.
This asks about the *pair*, which has answers a single word cannot have.

## What I measured

Everything below comes from `sketches/hocket/beatty.ts`, the module the sketch
itself plays. The first check is that its closed form — m is a hit iff
`ceil(m·d) < (m+1)·d` — is the same set as the definition `floor(n/d)`:
**11 densities, 100,000 steps each, 0 disagreements.**

**It is exact, and the complement is the only partner.**

| d | collisions | gaps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/φ² = 0.381966… | 0 | 0 |
| φ−1 = 0.618034… | 0 | 0 |
| √2−1 = 0.414214… | 0 | 0 |
| 1/e = 0.367879… | 0 | 0 |
| π−3 = 0.141593… | 0 | 0 |
| 1/2 = 0.5 | 100,000 | 100,000 |

over 200,000 steps. Holding voice 1 at φ−1 and sweeping voice 2 across 4001
other densities in [0.05, 0.95] finds **no second answer**.

**Neither voice is steady; the two of them are.** Voice 1 at φ−1 plays gaps of 1
and 2 steps only (1180 and 1909 of them); voice 2 plays gaps of 2 and 3 only
(729 and 1180). Two values each, which is the three-distance theorem. Together:
**one value — 5000 onsets in 5000 steps.** The ratio of each voice's two gap
counts is 0.6181 and 0.6178, which is d again.

**Land on a simple ratio and it fails by exactly 1/q.** At an exact p/q the pair
drops 1/q of the notes and doubles 1/q of them. Seventeen fractions from 1/2 to
55/89, in integer arithmetic: worst departure from 1/q is **1.2e−5**, one step in
85,000, and that is the window not being a whole number of periods.

So the *simpler* the relationship between the two parts, the worse the
interlock — the opposite of how ratios behave everywhere else in music, and the
sketch's best single moment: `Snap to q ≤ 2` and half the tune is gone.

**The agreement has to be good to about 1/M to survive M steps.** Put voice 2
off the complement by ε and the defect rate is **M·ε**:

| M | ε | M·ε | measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 1e−6 | 0.0100 | 0.0098 |
| 10,000 | 1e−5 | 0.1000 | 0.0998 |
| 50,000 | 1e−7 | 0.0050 | 0.0050 |
| 50,000 | 3e−6 | 0.1500 | 0.1500 |
| 200,000 | 1e−7 | 0.0200 | 0.0200 |
| 200,000 | 1e−6 | 0.2000 | 0.2000 |

Worst departure **0.8%** over the nine cells carrying at least 100 defects,
across three window lengths and five values of ε. It is a *rate that grows with
how long you listen* — the players do not drift apart steadily, they come apart
faster the longer they go, because a voice's n-th note is displaced by n·ε/d²
and the displacements accumulate quadratically.

Past ε ≈ 1/M it saturates at **0.4722** and stays there over four decades of ε.
That number is not "a mess": two *independent* streams of densities d and 1−d
would collide or leave a gap at 2·d·(1−d) = **0.4721**. The pair is interlocked
or it is statistically unrelated, with very little in between.

**A third player cannot help.** Uspensky (1927): there is no partition of the
integers into three or more Beatty sequences. Searched with 40 random restarts
and local refinement at 20,000 steps, no voice allowed below density 0.12:

| voices | lowest defect rate found | where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | **0.0000** | 0.800 0.200 |
| 3 | 0.2400 | 0.760 0.120 0.120 |
| 4 | 0.3164 | 0.637 0.121 0.121 0.121 |
| 5 | 0.4510 | 0.501 0.125 0.122 0.128 0.124 |

Three equal voices give 0.6667. Only the two-voice row is a proof of anything —
the rest is a search, and Uspensky is the reason it will not find a zero. But
the shape is the interesting part: the minimum always runs to the boundary, so
what the search wants to do with the extra voices is turn them off. At the
sketch's own default share, three voices and four voices give **the same** rate,
0.3820. The third player already ruins it and the fourth makes no difference.

**And the sketch plays it.** Read off a recording, one binary decision per step
aligned to the audio clock — no free-running onset detector, because the grid is
known.

| from the recording | measured | should be |
| --- | --- | --- |
| voice 1 alone against `floor(n/d)` | **229 of 229 steps** | all of them |
| voice 2 alone against `floor(n/d)` | **229 of 229 steps** | all of them |
| the same patterns at any other alignment | 23.6% | chance |
| both voices, steps carrying a note | **229 of 229**, 0 empty | every step once |
| quietest step, as a fraction of the loudest | 0.65 | no silence at all |
| voice 1's own gaps | 1 step ×54, 2 steps ×87 | only 1 and 2 |
| voice 2's own gaps | 2 steps ×33, 3 steps ×54 | only 2 and 3 |

Snapped to a ratio, the holes arrive at 1/q — 0.5022, 0.2489, 0.1965, 0.1659,
0.1441 and 0.1223 against 1/2, 1/4, 1/5, 1/6, 1/7 and 1/8, worst error 0.0035.
And off the complement by ε the recording has the model's missing steps exactly
— 0, 14, 26 and 57 of them at four values of ε — agreeing on *which* steps at
**100.00%** in all four cases.

Levels: 0.523 pre-limiter at the defaults, 0.699 at the loudest setting the
sketch has, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**A threshold that has to split the data cannot report "nothing is missing".**
The per-step detector placed its cut at the widest ratio gap in the sorted step
energies, which is a decent self-calibrating rule and worked on every
configuration that had silence in it. On the interlocked pair — the one case the
whole sketch exists to demonstrate, where the right answer is *every step is a
note* — there is no note/silence boundary to find, so the rule split the two
**voices** instead. Voice 2 carries 0.86 of voice 1's gain and 0.86 of its
decay; the energies differ by 1.57, the rule reported a separation of 1.5, and
the notes-per-step came back as 0.6186, which is voice 1's density.

Two conditions returning the same number is the tell, and it was there: the
"both voices" gap histogram was *identical* to voice 1 alone. `ideas.md` already
carries this from `tongues` — identical values across conditions mean you are
measuring the instrument. The fix is an absolute cut at a thousandth of the
loudest step, because a real note is within a factor of two of the loudest and
silence is exactly zero. The separation is then 1e18 when there is silence and
reported as *nothing rejected* when there is not, which is a detector that can
say "none" instead of being obliged to find some.

**The capture worklet posts channel 0, and the voices are panned apart.** Voice
1 sits at −0.45 and voice 2 at +0.45, so voice 2 reached the tap at 0.41 and
made the problem above worse. One gain node with `channelCount = 1` and
`channelCountMode = 'explicit'` sums to mono before the tap. This is latent in
every harness here that has ever tapped a panned sketch and compared voices by
level.

**The alignment search was shorter than the transport had been running.** The
offset that lines a recording up with `floor(n/d)` is the transport's step count
when the capture began, and that grows through a run: the first capture aligned
at ~6 and the second needed ~250 against a search span of 300. Voice 2 came back
at 62.01% — comfortably above the 52.8% two streams of that density would agree
by chance, which is exactly what a near-miss looks like and exactly why it was
not obviously wrong. With the span widened it is 100.00%, 0 wrong. A search
bounded by a number I picked rather than by something the run knows is a bug
waiting for the run to get long enough.

**I edited the sketch while its own dev server was running and Vite hot-reloaded
the page out from under an 18-minute capture.** `window.__capDone` came back
undefined. CLAUDE.md warns about Vite handing back a second copy of a module;
this is the same hazard from the other end. Do not touch anything under `src/`
or `sketches/` while a harness is live.

**And one thing that looked wrong and was the most interesting result in the
run.** Snapping to 2/3 produced *no* missing notes where 1/q predicts a third of
them. That is not a failure: the double nearest 2/3 is 0.66666666666666663, a
hair below it, its complement is a hair above 1/3, and that pair is not 2/3 and
1/3 — it tiles. Over every p/q with q ≤ 16 in lowest terms, exact integer
arithmetic collapses all 79 of them; as doubles, 63 collapse at the ratio's own
rate, 10 collapse at less than it, and **6 tile perfectly** (6/7, 8/9, 7/10,
10/11, 11/12, 11/15). The 1/q law is a fact about the rational, and `Snap` can
only ever hand you a double. It is the same thing `irrational` found from the
other side: how rational a number *behaves* is a question about the window, not
about the number.

## Next

- [ ] The non-homogeneous case: `floor(n·r + s)`, with an intercept. Partitions
      into three or more *do* exist once the sequences may be offset, and which
      ones is Fraenkel's conjecture — open for six parts and up. A three-part
      hocket that actually works is buildable and is the obvious next thing to
      hear.
- [ ] Beatty sequences nest: the steps voice 1 does *not* play are themselves a
      Beatty sequence of the steps, so the same split applies recursively. That
      is a three-part hocket whose parameter is a tree rather than three
      densities, and it sidesteps Uspensky entirely.
- [ ] Let the two densities drift slowly in opposite directions, so the hocket
      interlocks, comes apart at M·ε, and re-locks. The form is already in the
      arithmetic and nobody composes it.
- [ ] The tune is indexed by grid step, so it belongs to the pulse rather than to
      either player. Indexing it by each voice's own note count would give two
      melodies that only line up while the interlock holds.
- [ ] `tiling` finds entry points that fill a cycle exactly; this fills an
      infinite one with no cycle at all. They are the rational and irrational
      halves of one idea and should be able to share a search.
- [ ] What sets the six exceptions? 6/7, 8/9, 7/10, 10/11, 11/12 and 11/15 all
      have p/q just under 1 — which way the double rounds relative to the exact
      ratio ought to predict the whole table, and that is one line of arithmetic
      away from being a rule rather than a list.
