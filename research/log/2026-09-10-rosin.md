# 2026-09-10 — rosin: the shape of Schelleng's diagram, none of its numbers

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/rosin`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — β 0.07, force 0.85, bow speed 0.12,
rosin grip 1.6, corner rounding 0.35, loss 0.9992, root 43 in G minor, seed 9.
The demonstration is one slider: take `Rosin grip` from 1.6 down to 1 while it
plays and the tone collapses into a drag, because at 1 the hysteresis loop
closes and nothing on the diagram speaks at all.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-05 with `cone`.

`bow`, on 2026-08-13, built a bowed-string waveguide and went looking for
Schelleng's force wedge in it. It is not there, and the log said so plainly: "a
memoryless characteristic reproduces Helmholtz motion happily and the force
boundaries not at all." Its Schelleng diagram is the textbook's, drawn over a
model that does not produce it, and labelled as such on the canvas.

It also said *why*, which is the part worth testing: a memoryless friction curve
has no static-versus-dynamic distinction and so nothing to tear loose from, and
Schelleng's minimum force comes out of the hysteresis of rosin. That claim has
been sitting in `ideas.md` twice over — once under `bow` and once under `wolf`,
where the same missing piece is what stops a wolf note from stuttering.

So: the same waveguide with the friction curve replaced by two thresholds.
Breaking away costs μs·F, being recaptured costs only μd·F, and the loop between
them encloses area. The ratio μs/μd is a single parameter that closes the loop,
which means the control for the whole experiment is a slider rather than a
second build.

The detector is an integer. Helmholtz motion is exactly one release per period,
and `bow` was fooled twice by autocorrelation — a signal periodic at T/n is also
periodic at T — so the release count is read from the model's own state machine
and never from the sound.

## What I measured

**With hysteresis the string speaks. Without it, never.** Eight bow positions ×
28 forces, twice, 224 cells each way:

| β | with hysteresis (μs/μd = 1.6) | loop closed (μs = μd) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.020 | never speaks | never speaks |
| 0.028 | never speaks | never speaks |
| 0.040 | 0.78 … 2.39 | never speaks |
| 0.056 | 0.78 … 7.37 | never speaks |
| 0.080 | **0.59 … 5.57** | never speaks |
| 0.112 | 0.59 … 4.20 | never speaks |
| 0.160 | 0.44 … 4.20 | never speaks |
| 0.224 | 0.44 … 5.57 | never speaks |

**0 of 224** with the loop closed — and not merely no Helmholtz motion: the
stick fraction is 0.00 in every one of those cells, at every force up to 13.
The string is dragged from the first sample and never once captured. That is
the qualitative half of Schelleng arriving exactly where `bow` predicted it
would come from.

At β = 0.08 the releases per period run
`… 0.00 0.00 0.06 0.99 1.00 1.00 1.00 1.16 2.17 1.73 …` across the force sweep:
dragged, then a clean band of exactly one release per period, then two and three,
which is the crushed sound.

**But the arithmetic is not his.** Schelleng says the minimum force falls as
1/β² and the maximum as 1/β. Fitting the measured boundaries in log-log:

| boundary | measured slope | R² | Schelleng |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| minimum force | **−0.373** | **0.919** | −2 |
| maximum force | +0.184 | 0.096 | −1 |

The minimum force is the interesting failure. R² of 0.92 over six positions
means it really is a power law — it is just not the power law. A boundary that
scattered would be a measurement problem; a clean law with the wrong exponent is
the model saying something definite and wrong. The maximum force has no β
dependence worth the name.

**The duty cycle is wrong in a direction that points somewhere.** In ideal
Helmholtz motion the slip occupies exactly β of the period, so the stick
fraction should be 1 − β and should not depend on the bow force at all:

| β | stick fraction where it speaks | 1 − β | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.040 | 0.770 ± 0.151 | 0.960 | 0.190 |
| 0.056 | 0.739 ± 0.185 | 0.944 | 0.205 |
| 0.080 | 0.711 ± 0.162 | 0.920 | 0.209 |
| 0.112 | 0.669 ± 0.229 | 0.888 | 0.219 |
| 0.160 | 0.596 ± 0.245 | 0.840 | 0.244 |
| 0.224 | 0.702 ± 0.235 | 0.776 | 0.074 |

About 0.2 low everywhere, and inside a single band it *climbs with force* —
0.52, 0.61, 0.78 at β = 0.08 — where it should sit still. The slip phase in this
model is too long, which is precisely what a finite recapture threshold does: an
ideal Helmholtz release is instantaneous, and this one has to wait for |Δv| to
come back inside μd·F before the string can be caught.

**And the near-bridge silence is not a Schelleng effect.** β = 0.02 and 0.028
never speak at any force, which looks like a minimum force rising steeply toward
the bridge and is not: the measured −0.373 law puts their minimum force well
inside the swept range. At β = 0.02 the bridge-side delay line is five samples
long, which is not enough of a waveguide for a corner to travel down. That is
the simulation running out of string, and it would have been easy to report as
physics.

Levels: 0.651 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**An OfflineAudioContext finishes rendering before a postMessage is delivered.**
The first sweep configured each cell by posting parameters to the worklet, and
every one of the 176 cells ran at the constructor defaults. The symptom was not
an error: it was a grid of identical numbers, which is the third time in four
days that tell has caught something (it was `tongues`' tongue widths, then
`sitting`'s three rooms, now this). Parameters go in as AudioParams now, and the
friction state comes out on a second output channel, so the analysis never
touches the port at all.

**The two waves were swapped at the bow.** A wave arriving *from* the bridge is
travelling toward the nut and carries on to the nut; I sent it back the way it
came. That makes the two segments independent resonators that never exchange
energy, so no Helmholtz corner can go round, and nothing spoke anywhere in any
condition. One line.

**Two copies of one algorithm are not one algorithm.** The diagram is computed
on the main thread because measuring two hundred cells through an
OfflineAudioContext is a stall — and a worklet cannot import, so `friction.ts`
is a deliberate second copy of `string.worklet.js`. Compared on releases per
period they disagreed on **3 of 7** configurations, and not by rounding: whole
different attractors, 1.05 against 2.32. Two causes, both of them things I would
not have listed as part of the model:

- the port's delay lines were `Float64Array` where a worklet's are `Float32Array`
- the port ramped the bow speed per sample where an AudioParam ramp is a
  staircase that only moves at 128-sample block boundaries

Matching both made them agree to **every printed digit** — 2.321 against 2.321,
0.765 against 0.765. Neither the numeric type nor the parameter update rate is
usually thought of as part of a model's definition, and in a system this
sensitive both are.

**And the map was being drawn from runs far too short to mean anything.** The
canvas was simulating 0.7 s per cell. At a loss of 0.9992 per pass the string
takes something like six hundred periods to settle, so at 0.9 s not one cell of
the grid reads as speaking — the picture would have been a confident, uniformly
wrong diagram. Three seconds per cell, two cells a frame.

## Next

- [ ] Where the −0.373 comes from. It is a clean law and it is the model's own;
      finding what sets it is a better question than trying to force it toward
      −2, and the loss and corner-rounding parameters are the obvious axes to
      sweep it against.
- [ ] A shorter recapture. The duty-cycle error says the slip phase is too long
      because capture waits for |Δv| ≤ μd·F. A velocity-dependent dynamic
      coefficient — friction falling as the slip speeds up, which is what rosin
      actually does — would shorten it and is one line in the state machine.
- [ ] The thermal model: rosin's grip depends on temperature, which integrates
      the power dissipated in the slip. That is the physically honest version of
      hysteresis and it is what the literature uses to get Schelleng out.
- [ ] Point this bow at `wolf`. A real wolf note is a bowing phenomenon — the
      stutter is the bow losing and regaining grip as energy sloshes into the
      body — and `wolf`'s negative-resistance bow can only sustain, never
      stutter. The state machine here is the missing piece there too.
- [ ] More of the string: at β = 0.02 the bridge-side delay is five samples.
      A higher internal sample rate, or a fractional-delay allpass, would let
      the near-bridge half of the diagram exist at all.
