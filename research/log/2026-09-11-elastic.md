# 2026-09-11 — elastic: you may state how two voices relate, or state both their bars

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/elastic`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 2 voices, 5 notes each, bars of 8 and
6 steps, A minor pentatonic, seed 5. Voice 1's durations come out
`0.613 0.817 0.460 0.306 0.204` against a 2.400 s bar. The demonstration is to
turn `Link voices 1 and 2` on and sweep `…at this ratio`: the readout tells you
the bars allow **2.2695** and nothing else, and the rhythm re-derives only when
you land on it. `Stretch note 2` is the other half — one coefficient, and every
other duration moves to keep its relationships.

## What I tried

Composition tools, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-06 with `canon`.

`cats-cradle` made pitch relational: intervals are the stored data, so
transposition is structural rather than an edit. `rhyme` did it for a whole
score, so the notes you actually chose are its unpinned components. Both are
about pitch, and `ideas.md` has asked twice — once under `develop`, once under
`rhyme` — for the rhythm half, on the grounds that durations as ratios are
affine in log time and augmentation is then a single coefficient.

The reason to build it rather than assume it is the same problem is that it is
not. An interval says nothing about absolute pitch, so pitch constraints are
**homogeneous**: a relational score has one free transposition per component and
that is the end of it. A rhythm carries a constraint pitch has no analogue of —
the durations have to *add up*. Every voice must fill its bar.

So the system has two layers. The stated ratios fix every duration inside a
component up to one unknown scale, which is a linear system in log time and is
`rhyme`'s machinery. The bar constraints are then linear in those scales: one
equation per voice, C unknowns, a V × C system. That second layer is
inhomogeneous and it is where everything interesting happens.

## What I measured

**The relationships are exact.** 300 random problems, 2 and 3 voices, 3 to 7
notes each, ratios drawn from a pool of eleven simple fractions and chained so
the graph is as awkward as it gets:

- worst stated-ratio error in the realised rhythm: **4.44e−16**
- worst bar-total error: **4.44e−16**

**And the bar has already chosen how two voices may relate.** Link a note of one
voice to a note of another and C falls by one while V does not, so the V × C
system is over-determined and has a solution for exactly one ratio. Over 200
random pairs of voices:

| proposed link | outcome |
| --- | --- |
| the forced value | accepted **200/200** |
| 1% away | refused **200/200** |
| **0.01% away** | refused **200/200** |

and at the forced value the durations do not move at all — **4.44e−16** — because
the link carries no information. It was already true before anyone said it.

That is the finding and it is not a fact about this program. Two voices that must
both fill the same bar are not independently composable: you may state how they
relate, or you may state both their bars, and you cannot state both. It is why a
polyrhythm is *named* by its ratio rather than assembled from two independent
parts, and it is the thing that has no counterpart in `rhyme`, because pitch has
no bar.

**The freedom count is what the algebra says:**

| score | components | rank | free | solves |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 voices, chained, unlinked | 2 | 2 | 0 | yes |
| the same, linked at the forced ratio | 1 | 1 | 0 | yes |
| the same, linked at 3/2 instead | 1 | 1 | 0 | **no** |
| one ratio left unstated | 3 | 2 | 1 | yes |
| a cycle that does not close | — | — | — | **no** |

The third row is the whole point in one line: the graph is identical, the rank
is identical, and one has a rhythm and the other has none.

**And it all survives into the sound.** Onsets detected from a recording of a
single voice, with no reference to the scheduler at all. The detector is
validated first on a quantity that has to be an integer — **5.066 notes per bar
against 5** — and then:

| note | stated ratio to note 1 | solved | heard | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 1.3333 | 0.8170 | 0.8171 | 0.12% |
| 3 | 0.7500 | 0.4596 | 0.4610 | 0.16% |
| 4 | 0.5000 | 0.3064 | 0.3053 | 0.49% |
| 5 | 0.3333 | 0.2043 | 0.2035 | 0.49% |

with the bar itself at **0.02%** — 2.4005 s heard against 2.4000 s written.

End to end in the sketch, the bars allowed 2.26950 for the default pair: that
value gives a rhythm, 1% away gives none, 0.1% away gives none.

Levels: 0.588 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**A chain of ratios is unplayable, and it is the obvious shape.** Every note
stated against its predecessor is how you would naturally write this down, and
with four links from a pool containing 3 and 1/3 the cumulative product reaches
81:1 inside a single bar — one note takes almost all of it and the rest are a
few milliseconds. Not a bug in the solver, which realises it perfectly: a bug in
what I asked it to realise. Stating every note against the *first* note of its
voice keeps every duration inside the pool's own range, and it is the better
compositional object as well, since "this one is two-thirds of the first" is a
thing a composer would actually say. The solver handles both; only one is music.

**`forcedRatio` passed `Infinity` as a bar length.** My first version tried to
isolate a voice by putting every other note in a second voice with an infinite
bar, which makes the linear system solve Σ = ∞. The fix was to stop being clever
about it: drop the cross-voice ratios and solve, at which point each voice is its
own component and its own bar pins it, and the answer can just be read off.

**My "expected onsets" figure was floored.** The detector check first printed
"55 onsets, about 50 expected", which looks like a 10% error and is arithmetic —
26 s at a 2.4 s bar is 10.8 bars, not 10. Rewritten as notes per bar, which is
the quantity that has to be an integer, it reads 5.066 against 5. A sloppy
denominator is a good way to invent a discrepancy and then go looking for it in
the thing being measured.

## Next

- [ ] Cross-voice ratios that are *not* between first notes. The forced value
      depends on which pair you pick, so a composer choosing the pair is
      choosing which relationship to make explicit — and the others follow.
      Worth drawing all of them at once.
- [ ] Let a bar be free instead. If you want to state the voices' ratio, the
      algebra says one of the bars has to give; solving for the bar rather than
      the scale is the same system read the other way and is probably the more
      musical direction.
- [ ] Rhythmic rhyme, the way `rhyme` does pitch: "this span is that span,
      augmented", as a constraint over several notes at once rather than pairs.
      That is `develop`'s missing half too.
- [ ] Notation cost. The realised durations are real numbers; notation wants
      small denominators. How complex a tuplet does a given ratio set need
      before it can be written down, and which sets cannot be?
- [ ] The solver refuses with a reason. Saying *which* ratio to relax to make a
      refused score legal is one more linear solve and turns a refusal into a
      suggestion, which is what `species` does well and this does not yet.
