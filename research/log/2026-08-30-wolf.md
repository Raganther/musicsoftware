# 2026-08-30 — wolf: the note that will not sustain

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/wolf`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — scale from MIDI 38 spanning 12, body
98 Hz, Q 60, coupling 26 Hz, plucked. The wolf is the sixth note of the scale
and you can hear it coming. Then set `Coupling` to 0 and play the same scale:
the dip vanishes completely. That A/B is the whole sketch. Seed 2. For the
merged-peak regime take `Body Q` to 26; for a rattle rather than a warble take
`Coupling` to 60.

## What I tried

Synthesis/DSP, the stalest family — five days since `mallet` on 2026-08-25.

Every cellist knows the wolf: somewhere around F or F# on the G string the note
stops sustaining and starts stuttering, and moving a quarter-tone either way
cures it. The folk explanation is that the instrument has a bad note. The real
one is that it has two oscillators that share energy, and two coupled
oscillators cannot share a frequency:

    ÿ₁ + ω₁²y₁ = κ(b − y₁)
    b̈  + ω_b²b  = κ(y₁ − b)

    λ± = (ω₁²+ω_b²)/2 + κ ± √( ((ω₁²−ω_b²)/2)² + κ² )

The square root has a +κ² under it that never vanishes, so tuning the string up
through the body resonance does not make the frequencies meet — **they repel**.
An avoided crossing, the same one as in coupled pendulums, molecular orbitals
and level repulsion in random matrices. Here you can play it.

The reason to build this rather than read about it is that the arithmetic is
falsifiable from audio. The sketch is handed three numbers (string frequency,
body frequency, coupling); the harness recomputes the eigenvalues from those
three and never reads anything back out of the model. Then it measures the
actual spectrum. So the prediction and the measurement have somewhere to
disagree, and `Coupling = 0` is a control that must show nothing at all.

body Q 60:

**19 of 20 predicted modes found within 1.5 Hz, mean error 0.066 Hz, worst
0.160 Hz.** Closest approach: 6.67 Hz predicted, 6.44 Hz measured. Never zero.
The single miss is the upper mode at midi 40 — a minor third below the
resonance, where it has dropped under 2% of the lower one and below the
analysis floor. The physics, not a failure.

**The modes trade character.** This is the part that makes it an avoided
crossing rather than two unrelated things passing near each other. A pluck
drives the string, so peak height reads out how much *string* is in each mode:
100%/2% a whole tone below, 100%/42% at the resonance, 51%/100% a semitone
above, 3%/100% a minor third above. The lower mode starts as the string and ends
as the body; the upper does the reverse. They swap identities as they pass,
which is exactly what the eigenvectors do.

**And the note dies.** Sustain falls from 2.32 s to 0.53 s across the crossing,
4.4x, with an interior minimum at midi 44 (0.64 → **0.53** → 0.95). At the
crossing the mode is half body, so it inherits the body's damping. This is the
wolf as a player actually meets it — not "a note containing two pitches" but
"the note that will not ring".

**The control is clean and it is a real one.** With κ = 0:

- the peak sits on the string to within **0.010 Hz** at all ten notes
- every note rings for **exactly 3.20 s** — which is the `String ring` parameter
  the worklet was handed, recovered to three figures, at every note
- ratio between longest and shortest sustain: **1.00x**

That second line is the known-answer channel and it validates the decay
estimator rather than just decorating the table: a t60 measurement that returns
the t60 it was given is one I can then trust on the coupled rows.

**Body Q 60 is higher than a real cello's,** which is nearer 20-40, and the
reason is a finding rather than a fudge. At Q 26 the two modes merge into a
single peak: **only 8 of 20 separable**, and the closest approach cannot be
resolved at all. The mechanism is presumably that the coupled mode's linewidth
has grown to about the size of the 6.67 Hz splitting — the arithmetic puts it
near 6 Hz FWHM at Q 26 against 3 Hz at Q 60 — but I have not measured a
linewidth, only inferred one from a decay time, so treat that sentence as the
explanation I believe rather than the one I checked. What is measured is the
count: 19 of 20 separable at Q 60, 8 of 20 at Q 26.

The two measures come apart cleanly, which is the strongest form of the point.
Going from Q 60 to Q 26 takes spectral resolvability from 19/20 to 8/20 — and
leaves the sustain collapse essentially untouched, 4.36x against 4.46x. The
phenomenon is exactly as strong; only my ability to point at it changes.
**Audibility arrives a long way before resolvability.** Had I built only the
realistic-Q version I would have had the wolf and no way to show what it was.

Levels: 0.485 pre-limiter at defaults, 0.807 in the hottest configuration I
could find, 0.647 bowed. Two runs of the default agreed to 0.003.

## What went wrong

**A pluck is a displacement, not a velocity.** The first version set `y1 += a`
and left `y2` alone, which makes `y1 − y2` equal to the whole pluck in one
sample — an enormous initial velocity. The resulting amplitude is `a/ω_n`, and
at 98 Hz ω_n is 0.0128 rad/sample, so **asking for 0.6 got about 47** — some
80x too big, and close to the ~43 raw sum actually measured, which is what
confirmed the mechanism rather than merely suggesting it. Every configuration
read a peak of exactly 1.600, and that number is what gave it away: 1.600 is 2 × 0.8,
the worklet's runaway clamp times the master gain. A peak that is identical to
three digits across seven different configurations is not a level, it is a
ceiling. Setting both history samples is a displacement released from rest,
which is what a pluck is; raw sum went from ~43 to ~1.3.

Worth noting what this cost: the spectrum was being measured through a hard
clamp, so the coupled runs found one peak everywhere and I spent a while
wondering whether the body was simply too quiet to see. It wasn't. Nothing
downstream of a saturating nonlinearity is worth analysing.

**The window was in the wrong place, twice.** The capture was armed *after* the
pluck, so the first tens of milliseconds went unrecorded — and at the wolf that
is most of the note. And it analysed 2 s of a sound that is gone in 0.5 s, which
is not a nuisance but the phenomenon itself: the whole point of a wolf is that
it does not sustain, so a window sized for a normal note measures mostly upper
partials and floor. The onset is now found in the signal rather than assumed
from timing, and the window sits on the note.

**A beat null is not a decay.** The first sustain numbers came from the first
time the envelope fell 20 dB below its peak. At the closest approach the two
modes are near-equal in height and beat at 6.4 Hz, so the envelope has nulls
every 78 ms — and the estimator dutifully found the first null and called it the
decay. It reported **0.30 s where the decay is 0.64 s**, and the error was worst
at precisely the note the entire sketch is about. Reading the *last* crossing
instead steps over nulls and follows the upper envelope.

That correction took the headline sustain collapse from **7.81x down to 4.36x**.
The wrong number was the more impressive one, which is the direction these
errors always seem to go. The fix was checkable because the control has a known
answer: 3.20 s before, 3.20 s after, so the change fixed the beating case
without disturbing the case I could verify.

**A bit-identical repeat is not a repeatability estimate.** I ran the shipping
configuration twice and every number in the table matched to the digit. That is
worth knowing — it proves there is no timing jitter in the capture and no
run-to-run noise — but it bounds *nothing* about systematic error, because a
deterministic model measured by a deterministic analysis will happily reproduce
a wrong answer forever. The handle on bias here is the control (0.010 Hz on the
string, 3.20 s recovered from 3.2), not the repeat.

**Plucking into a ringing string is not addition.** Levels at long ring settings
were stacking without bound, because each pluck added to whatever displacement
was already there. A finger landing for the next note stops most of what is on
the string, so the pluck now keeps only half of it — repeated plucks converge on
2a however long the ring is set. The body is deliberately *not* damped, since it
keeps sounding after the string is stopped, which is true of a cello. This
compressed the gap between the quietest and hottest settings from 1.50x to
1.19x and is why a single gain can put both inside the band.

## And a gate result that was not about the gate

The first full `npm run smoke` after adding `wolf` failed four sketches —
`bloom`, `chord-loom`, `convergence`, `earshot` — all reading exactly 0. None of
them share anything with `wolf`, and `wolf` sorts last, so it cannot have
affected sketches that ran before it. The cause was **my own orphaned
processes**: harness runs leave a `vite` server behind when `srv.kill()` reaps
the `npx` shell but not its child, and I had six of them going, one for twelve
hours, plus the chromium tree from a smoke run I had killed mid-flight. Under
that load the audio thread underruns and a sketch reads silent.

Two runs settled it: `origin/main` in a separate worktree passed 35/35, and my
branch passed 36/36 once the orphans were killed. Worth knowing because the
failure mode is maximally misleading — a silent sketch looks exactly like a real
regression, and the suite has produced a flaky `bow: silent` before. **Check
what else is running before believing a smoke failure**, and kill stray servers
between harness runs.

## Next

- [ ] Bow it properly. The current bow is a negative resistance saturating at a
      fixed amplitude — enough to sustain, nothing like stick-slip. A real
      wolf is a *bowing* phenomenon: the stutter is the bow losing and regaining
      grip as energy sloshes into the body. A friction curve would make the
      stutter emerge instead of being described.
- [ ] The wolf eliminator. A cellist's fix is a mass clamped to the tailpiece —
      a third oscillator that splits the body mode again. Three coupled modes,
      one more eigenvalue, and a real prediction about where to put it.
- [ ] Sweep coupling continuously and plot the splitting against κ. It should
      go as 2κ/(2ω) for small κ and the measurement is already in place.
- [ ] The resolvability boundary deserves its own measurement: sweep Q and find
      where the two peaks merge, then check it against the linewidth arithmetic
      rather than asserting they are "about the same".
- [ ] Sustain has an interior minimum a semitone *above* the resonance, not on
      it, and the curve is asymmetric. I have not chased why and do not want to
      invent a reason.
- [ ] Every measurement window in this repo should be sized from the thing being
      measured, not from habit. Today that was a 2 s window on a 0.5 s note;
      `arc` was a 2.4 s window on a 20.9 s form. Same mistake, opposite sign.
