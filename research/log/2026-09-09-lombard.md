# 2026-09-09 — lombard: a room can run away with nobody being unreasonable

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/lombard`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — 5 players, spacing 3, assertiveness
+2 dB, spread 1.5, bandwidth 0.5 octaves, reaction 0.5, D dorian, seed 6. That
room runs away at 2.41 dB per bar and pins at the ceiling in about seventeen
bars. The demonstration is to let it fill up and then pull `Spacing` from 3 to
8 without touching anything else: the same five players, wanting exactly the
same margin over each other, drain the room instead of filling it.

## What I tried

Improvisation/performance, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-04 with
`guardrail`.

Every interaction sketch in this repo couples through pitch or through time.
`entrain` agrees on a tempo, `earshot` on a pitch, `conduct` on a beat,
`understudy` on a phrase. Nothing couples through **level**, which is the
channel real ensembles fight over, and the fight has a name: the Lombard reflex,
the reason a restaurant gets louder all evening and never quieter.

So: each player wants to be some number of decibels above what it can hear of
everyone else, where "what it can hear" is weighted by how much of the other
player's sound falls in its own band. The question is whether the room settles
or runs away — and that has an exact answer that does not depend on how loud
anyone starts, which is what made it worth building rather than asserting.

A sum of powers written in dB is very nearly the largest of them, so to first
order the update is

    L_i ← max_j ( L_j + 10·log10(w_ij) + t_i )

which is a linear system in the **max-plus** algebra, where "add" is max and
"multiply" is plus. Such systems grow at their max-plus eigenvalue, and that
eigenvalue is the **maximum cycle mean** of the graph whose edge j → i carries
t_i + 10·log10(w_ij) — computable exactly by Karp's algorithm, no cycle
enumeration and no search. That is the known answer this is measured against,
and it comes from outside the repo.

## What I measured

**The law is exact.** Growth rate against rate × maximum cycle mean, over seven
configurations:

| room | reaction | cycle mean × rate | measured | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 players, one band, +3 each | 1.00 | 3.000 | 3.000 | 0.0000 |
| 2 players, one band, +3 each | 0.50 | 1.500 | 1.500 | 0.0000 |
| 2 players, one band, +3 each | 0.25 | 0.750 | 0.750 | 0.0000 |
| 2 players, +3 and −1 | 1.00 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.0000 |
| 2 players, both content at −2 | 1.00 | −2.000 | −2.000 | 0.0000 |
| 2 players, an octave apart | 1.00 | −1.343 | −1.343 | 0.0000 |
| 5 players, mixed | 1.00 | 0.862 | **3.650** | 2.788 |

The last row is not a failure, it is the finding, and it took a second
prediction to close.

**Hearing several rivals at once is the whole story.** The max-plus value is a
strict *lower bound* rather than an estimate, because log-sum-exp exceeds max:
three comparable rivals are about 5 dB louder than the loudest of them. Correct
each player's edge weights by the softmax gap taken at the settled relative
levels and the agreement returns:

| room | reaction | plain | corrected | measured | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 players, mixed | 1.00 | 0.862 | 3.650 | 3.650 | 0.0000 |
| 5 players, mixed | 0.40 | 0.345 | 1.460 | 1.460 | 0.0000 |
| 5 players, 5 dB less assertive | 1.00 | −4.138 | −1.350 | −1.350 | 0.0000 |
| 6 players, wide bands | 1.00 | 1.175 | 4.117 | 4.117 | 0.0000 |
| 6 players, wide bands | 0.60 | 0.705 | 2.470 | 2.470 | 0.0000 |
| 6 players, narrow bands | 1.00 | −8.038 | −7.225 | −7.225 | 0.0000 |

Seven of seven at 0.0000. Two structural facts fall out and are worth more than
the agreement: shifting every player's assertiveness by −5 dB moves the cycle
mean by exactly −5 and leaves the correction at 2.788 **untouched**, so the
correction is a property of the overlap graph alone; and the reaction rate
scales the whole thing linearly, 2.788 × 0.4 = 1.115 to three figures.

**So a room can run away with nobody in it being unreasonable.** Holding
assertiveness at +2 dB throughout and varying only how far apart the players
sit:

| spacing (scale steps) | pairs alone | with crowding | runs away |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | +0.945 | +3.624 | yes |
| 2 | +0.869 | +3.044 | yes |
| 3 | +0.628 | +2.409 | yes |
| 4 | +0.266 | +1.780 | yes |
| 5 | **−0.195** | **+1.153** | **yes** |
| 6 | **−0.503** | **+0.463** | **yes** |
| 7 | −1.166 | −0.182 | no |
| 8 | −1.778 | −0.843 | no |

At spacings 5 and 6 the pairwise cycle mean is *negative* — there is no
escalating relationship between any two players in the room — and the room
still climbs. Pairwise reasoning puts the threshold at spacing 5; it is actually
at 7. Crowding is what causes the arms race, not assertiveness, and crowding is
the one thing nobody in a loud room can change.

**And the sound does it.** Measured bar by bar off the recording rather than
from the model:

| case | predicted dB/bar | heard | error | bars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| crowded, climbs | 0.409 | **0.383** | 0.025 | 15 |
| then spread out, falls | −0.369 | **−0.350** | 0.019 | 23 |

Both directions, both within 0.025 dB per bar, and the second is the same
ensemble as the first with nothing changed but how far apart the players sit.

The sketch reads 0.609 pre-limiter at the defaults and 0.78 once the room has
pinned at its ceiling, which is what a runaway sounds like: not an explosion,
everybody flat out at once.

## What went wrong

**A log-sum-exp computed as a sum of powers underflows, and it looks like the
model collapsing.** A quiet room reaches −1400 dB within a couple of hundred
rounds, at which point every 10^(L/10) term is exactly 0, the sum is 0, and the
function returns −Infinity. One configuration duly reported its growth rate as
−2,499,996 dB per round. The fix is the standard one — factor out the largest
term before summing — and it is also the formulation the theory is written in,
so the numerically right thing and the conceptually right thing were the same
edit. Worth noticing that the symptom was not a crash or a NaN but a plausible-
looking catastrophe.

**Rebuilding the room threw away the room.** Changing any structural parameter
re-ran the constructor, which reset every level to its starting value — so the
single most interesting gesture here, letting a crowded room fill and then
pulling the players apart to hear it drain, was impossible: the drain always
started from silence instead of from the ceiling. It showed up as the falling
measurement returning zero usable bars. State that a control is *about* has to
survive the control.

**Two explanations for a 6% residual, both tested, both wrong.** The heard
slopes came in consistently about 6% short of the predicted ones in both
directions. The obvious candidate was transient bars inside the fit window;
dropping the first two and then four moved the slope by 0.005 dB/bar, which
kills it. The second candidate was better: the recording measures the *sum* of
the voices while the prediction follows the loudest, so if the players spread
apart as the room moves the sum would lag. Fitting the model's own total power
over exactly the same bars gives 0.409 and −0.377 — the predicted rates, not the
heard ones — so that is dead too. The shortfall is real, consistent, small, and
unexplained, and I would rather say so than offer a third story I have not
tested.

## Next

- [ ] The residual. A third candidate: the pluck envelope is a fixed shape whose
      decay outlasts a bar, so each bar's RMS carries some of the previous bar's
      level. That is a first-order smear, which delays a ramp rather than
      flattening it — but combined with the finite fit window it may not be
      neutral, and it is testable by shortening the decay.
- [ ] Per-band listening rather than one bandwidth for everyone: a player whose
      *ears* are wider than its voice is a different node in the graph, and the
      matrix stops being symmetric.
- [ ] A player who gives up — drops out entirely when it cannot be heard, which
      removes a node and can flip the cycle mean. Ensembles do this.
- [ ] Masking properly, using `veil`'s machinery, instead of a Gaussian overlap.
      Real masking is asymmetric — low frequencies mask high ones far better
      than the reverse — and an asymmetric graph has different cycles.
- [ ] The equilibrium levels when it settles, as a mix. This is automatic
      mixing by simulated players, and the settled case is a real balance
      nobody wrote.
