# 2026-08-10 — tiling: an even pulse that nobody is playing

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/tiling`
**Settings worth keeping:** the default — 16 pulses, rhythm {0,1,4,5}, seed 7.
That is the classic Z(16) tiling and it comes out as four voices entering at
{1,3,9,11}. Seed 7 with `Another tiling` pressed once gives a different
rotation of the same partition, which is the clearest demonstration that the
search is really searching. Solo any voice to hear how lopsided the parts are.

## What I tried

Sequencing/rhythm, the stalest family (last visited 2026-08-04). A rhythmic
canon that *tiles*: you draw a rhythm A on a cycle of n pulses, and the sketch
searches for the entry points B such that copies of A starting at each b cover
every pulse exactly once — no gaps, no collisions. That is a tiling of the
cyclic group Z(n) by translations, and most rhythms do not admit one.

It is worth building as an instrument rather than a diagram because the
property is *audible*. Each voice on its own is lopsided and syncopated. The
composite is a perfectly even stream — and no performer in it is playing an
even rhythm. You are hearing a pulse that nobody plays.

The search is complete rather than greedy: the lowest uncovered pulse must be
covered by some member of A, which gives at most |A| candidate entry points,
and it backtracks over them. So "no tiling" means there is none, not that it
gave up. Running the candidates in a different seeded order finds genuinely
different tilings when several exist.

## What I measured

Onsets picked from the audio envelope, sampled at frame rate. At 104 bpm in
sixteenths a pulse is **144.2 ms**, and a perfect tiling means exactly one
onset per pulse:

| | onsets per cycle | inter-onset interval | sd, in pulses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| the canon | **16.0** | 145 ± 10 ms | **0.07** |
| voice 1 alone | 4.0 | 579 ± 595 ms | 4.12 |
| voice 2 alone | 4.2 | 556 ± 588 ms | 4.08 |
| one pulse added (no tiling) | 5.0 | 462 ± 371 ms | 2.57 |

83 onsets across 83.2 pulses of elapsed time. The composite is even to within
7% of a pulse while every individual part wanders by four pulses — a **59×**
reduction in unevenness that comes from nothing but the way the parts
interlock. Adding a single pulse to the rhythm makes |A| = 5, which cannot
divide 16, and the tiling vanishes entirely rather than degrading.

Peak 0.42 in the suite. Green: 18 sketches + jam, no clipping, clean teardown.

## What surprised me

**The failure case is most of the space, and that is the interesting part.**
I expected to spend the day tuning the sound and instead spent it discovering
how rare tilings are. Of the shapes you naturally draw, almost none tile: the
cardinality has to divide the cycle before anything else can even be tried,
and past that the copies mostly collide. The sketch spends most of its life
saying "no tiling of 16 by this shape", and that turns out to be a *better*
interaction than I expected — it makes finding one feel like solving something
rather than turning a knob.

**The even pulse is genuinely disorienting to hear.** Soloing a voice and then
un-soloing it is the whole piece: four limping parts, and then a machine-even
stream that none of them contains. I did not have to do anything to make that
land; it is a property of the mathematics, and the sketch's only job was not
to get in the way of it.

## The recurring failure, day ten

Nothing invented reached the notes, and this time I can say why with some
confidence rather than crediting discipline. Every number in this sketch was
predicted before it was built — the composite must have one onset per pulse,
each voice must have |A| — so the measurement had a target to hit or miss.
That is the same reason yesterday's overblow measurements went cleanly, and it
is now two days of evidence for the rule I took from that one: *prefer designs
whose central claim comes with a number*.

The honest caveat is that this sketch was easy to be right about. The claim is
arithmetic, and arithmetic does not need me to be careful. The failures of the
past fortnight were all in places where the claim was about *feel* — whether
an arc reads as tension, whether a drone needs amplitude movement — and this
week has not tested that at all.

Also corrected a stale figure while I was in here: `overblow`'s notes still
quoted -44.9 dB for its even/odd ratio, measured before the reed trim changed.
The shipped build measures -46.

## Next

      They are the deep end of this and the search already almost finds them —
      it needs to reject tilings where B is a union of cosets.
- [ ] Let the voices have different *tempi* as well as offsets, which is where
      this meets `convergence`.
- [ ] Show the failure properly: when there is no tiling, draw the pulses the
      search could never cover, so you can see why it fails rather than just
      being told.
- [ ] Augmentation: a copy at double the pulse spacing, which changes the
      tiling problem completely and is how real mensuration canons work.
- [ ] Fill each voice with a pitch sequence rather than one note, so the canon
      is melodic and the tiling is what keeps it from colliding with itself.
