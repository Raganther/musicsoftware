# 2026-09-07 — tongues: a rhythm that would rather be a ratio

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/tongues`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — Ω 0.62, K 0.86, drift 0.05, 3 voices,
spread 1, A minor pentatonic, seed 4. The demonstration is to watch the density
readout while Ω sweeps: it holds at a ratio for seconds at a time and then jumps,
and you can hear the plateau because nothing changes while the knob moves. Set
`Coupling` to 0 for the other half of the point — the same sweep with no
plateaus at all, which is `irrational`. `Coupling` 1.2 is past criticality and
the voices stop agreeing about which ratio they are in.

## What I tried

Sequencing/rhythm, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-02 with
`escalator`.

`irrational`, on 2026-08-28, built the Sturmian word: a rigid rotation, sampled,
which gives a rhythm at any density you like and never repeats. Its finding was
about repetition. What I noticed re-reading it is what it does *not* do — turn
the density knob and the rhythm follows continuously, through every real number
on the way. Musicians do not behave like that. Push a drummer slightly off 2:3
and you get 2:3.

One term fixes it. Give the rotation a pull toward the beat it hears,

    θ_{n+1} = θ_n + Ω − (K/2π)·sin(2πθ_n)

and it stops following the knob: the density sticks at p/q over a whole interval
of Ω and then jumps. That is the sine circle map, the plateaus are Arnold
tongues, and the curve is a devil's staircase. The reason to build it as an
instrument is that the winding number — turns per drive pulse, the quantity the
whole theory is about — is *literally* the rhythm's density in events per pulse.
You do not have to visualise the abstraction; you can hear it as how often the
drum plays.

It is also unusually well supplied with answers from outside the repo, which is
the real attraction: tongue widths, how they nest, and a universal fractal
dimension, none of which I get to choose.

## What I measured

**At K = 0 it is `irrational`.** W = Ω for all seven test values, worst error
**2.1e−13**. The nonlinear sketch contains the linear one exactly, which is the
cheapest possible check and worth doing first.

**The tongues open at the rate they should.** A p/q plateau is known to widen as
K^q. Fitting log width against log K over five couplings:

| p/q | K=0.2 | K=0.3 | K=0.45 | K=0.65 | K=0.9 | exponent | theory |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/1 | 6.37e-2 | 9.55e-2 | 1.43e-1 | 2.07e-1 | 2.86e-1 | **1.00** | 1 |
| 1/2 | 3.17e-3 | 7.11e-3 | 1.59e-2 | 3.25e-2 | 6.07e-2 | **1.96** | 2 |
| 1/3 | 2.82e-4 | 9.37e-4 | 3.11e-3 | 9.09e-3 | 2.29e-2 | **2.93** | 3 |
| 2/5 | (floor) | (floor) | 2.12e-4 | 1.30e-3 | 6.26e-3 | **4.88** | 5 |

Four tongues, four different predictions, nothing shared between the fits. The
(floor) cells matter and are discussed below.

**They nest by the Farey mediant.** Between the p/q and r/s plateaus the widest
one is always (p+r)/(q+s) — 5 of 5 pairs, searching every rational with q ≤ 12
in between rather than checking the mediant alone. So the rhythm sweeping from
1/2 up to 1/1 stops hardest at 2/3, and between 1/2 and 2/3 at 3/5, all the way
down.

**At K = 1, what is left between the tongues has dimension 0.875.** At
criticality the locked intervals fill the line to full measure and the Ω that
still refuse to settle are a Cantor set. Its box dimension is a universal
constant; the literature puts it at 0.870. Box-counted over 8,193 winding
numbers, seven octaves of box size:

| K | tolerance | unlocked | box dimension | R² |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 | 3e-4 | 1.9% | 0.733 | 0.8292 |
| 0.5 | 1e-4 | 81.0% | **0.991** | 1.0000 |
| 0.5 | 3e-5 | 81.0% | **0.992** | 1.0000 |
| 0.8 | 3e-4 | 4.6% | 0.913 | 0.9949 |
| 0.8 | 1e-4 | 61.8% | **0.967** | 1.0000 |
| 0.8 | 3e-5 | 61.9% | **0.967** | 1.0000 |
| 1.0 | 3e-4 | 19.6% | 0.865 | 0.9999 |
| 1.0 | 1e-4 | 31.6% | **0.875** | 1.0000 |
| 1.0 | 3e-5 | 32.1% | **0.876** | 1.0000 |

The K = 0.5 and K = 0.8 rows are the whole reason the K = 1 row means anything.
Below criticality the unlocked set has positive measure, so its dimension is 1
*by definition* — nothing measured, a fact. The estimator returns 0.991 and
0.967, which is what licenses reading 0.875 as a number rather than as whatever
the estimator happens to output. And the 3e−4 rows show how easily it could have
gone the other way: at that tolerance the control reads **0.733**, which would
have been a fine-looking wrong answer for the critical case too.

0.875 against 0.870 is 0.6% high, and I think the residual has a mechanism
rather than being noise: plateaus narrower than the grid spacing of 1/8192
cannot be resolved, so they are counted as unlocked, which inflates the box count
worst at the finest box size and steepens the slope. That predicts the estimate
should fall toward 0.870 on a finer grid, which I have not run.

**And the sketch plays that model rather than an approximation of it.** With Ω
held still, the density actually dispatched against the winding number computed
from a 50,000-step orbit:

| Ω | K | pulses | predicted W | played | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.500 | 0.86 | 184 | 0.50000 | 0.5000 | 0.0000 |
| 0.620 | 0.86 | 184 | 0.63125 | 0.6304 | 0.0008 |
| 0.700 | 0.86 | 185 | 0.72179 | 0.7189 | 0.0029 |
| 0.700 | 0.00 | 185 | 0.70000 | 0.6973 | 0.0027 |

Worst error 0.0029, where one pulse in 184 is 0.0054 — so every error is below
what the measurement can resolve.

**Read off the audio instead, 274 of 274 pulses agree.** Aligning to the drive
pulse times and asking one binary question per pulse — did this one fire? — the
recording matches the dispatch log exactly in all three configurations
(95/95, 89/89, 90/90), with fired pulses louder than silent ones by a factor of
60 in the median.

Levels: 0.551 pre-limiter at the defaults, through the smoke gate.

## What went wrong

**The tongue-width table was reading its own resolution floor.** The first run
gave 1/3, 2/3, 2/5 and 3/5 all as ≈4.0e−4 at several different couplings —
four different tongues, three different K, one number. That number is exactly
2×tol, and the reason is structural: the method calls Ω locked when
|W − p/q| ≤ tol, and *off* a tongue W passes through p/q with slope about 1, so
a tongue of literally zero width still measures 2·tol wide. Every one of those
cells was measuring the instrument. Fixed by raising the orbit length tenfold,
computing the floor explicitly, refusing to fit any point within 3× of it, and
printing the floor above the table so the reader can see which cells are real.
Identical values across conditions is the tell, and it is worth looking for.

**The first tongue-finder had to land inside a tongue before it could measure
one.** It scanned outward from p/q in steps of 0.004 looking for somewhere W was
already right, which cannot possibly find a tongue narrower than 0.004 — that
is, most of them at small K, which is exactly the regime the K^q law lives in.
Replaced with plain bisection on each edge, which works because W is monotone in
Ω and so needs no starting point inside. That also turned out to be much more
precise than expected: the tongue edge is a saddle-node, where W − p/q grows as
the square root of the distance, so resolving W to 1e−5 places the edge to about
1e−10.

**A free-running onset detector counted 1.55× too many hits.** The decay is
three drive pulses long, so overlapping tails give the envelope rises that look
like attacks, and the count came out at 0.81, 0.97 and 1.09 events per pulse
against predictions of 0.50, 0.63 and 0.70 — all wrong, all wrong by about the
same factor, which is the shape of a systematic fault rather than noise. The fix
was to stop asking where the onsets are. The drive pulse times are known from
the audio clock, so the real question is one binary decision per pulse, and that
went from 1.55× wrong to exact.

**A select param's value is not typechecked against the union it comes from.** I
wrote `scale: 'minorPentatonic'` where the key is `pentatonicMinor`. `tsc`
passed it, because the param value is a plain string, and `degree()` then threw
inside the clock handler on every single step — a hundred exceptions a second
behind a silent sketch. Worth knowing that the param panel's `select` is a
string, not a checked enum.

**Unescaped backticks in the notes template, for the second time in three
days.** Same failure as `cone`. The difference is that this time it cost thirty
seconds instead of a debugging session, because I ran `npm run check` the moment
the file was written rather than going to the browser first. The lesson is not
about backticks.

## Next

- [ ] The dimension on a finer grid, to test the claim above that 0.875 falls
      toward 0.870 as the unresolvable plateaus shrink. If it does not move,
      my explanation of the residual is wrong and something else is.
- [ ] Two coupled voices rather than two independent maps: let each hear the
      other, and the locking is between the *players* rather than each to the
      drive. That is a genuinely different object and closer to an ensemble.
- [ ] The tongue you are in as a *chord*: the plateau names a ratio p/q, so
      `continuum`'s trick of playing that ratio as pitch would make the locking
      audible harmonically as well as rhythmically.
- [ ] Above K = 1 the map is non-invertible and the winding number stops being
      unique — different starting phases give different densities at the same Ω.
      The sketch already runs there and it sounds like disagreement; measuring
      the spread over initial conditions would make it a result.
- [ ] Sweep Ω *through* a plateau slowly enough to hear the hysteresis at the
      edges — the tongue you leave is not the tongue you re-enter.
