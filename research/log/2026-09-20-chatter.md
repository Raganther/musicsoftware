# 2026-09-20 — chatter: a buzz roll is Zeno's problem, and a bar is not a table

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/chatter`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — press 1 N, drop 0.9 m/s, bar decay
1.6 s, stick 8 g at hardness 1.5, seed 5, F pentatonic major from root 53. The
demonstration is one slider: walk `Bar decay` from 0.5 ms to 3 s and the buzz
goes from a 50 ms rattle to a 1.8 second one, with nothing else changed.

## What I tried

Synthesis, the stalest family — last visited 2026-09-15 with `lattice`.
`ideas.md` has carried this since `mallet`: *"Strike the bar twice within one
contact time and see what the interference does; a roll at 300 Hz is not thirty
strikes, it is one continuous contact."*

`mallet` measured how long a Hertzian contact lasts — 1–3 ms, and shorter the
harder you hit — and then let go of the stick. That number is usually a
footnote about brightness. It is also a *rate*. A stick you keep hold of
bounces, and a bouncing stick is the textbook bouncing ball: gaps shrinking
geometrically by the coefficient of restitution, infinitely many of them, the
whole sequence over in finite time. Inelastic collapse, Zeno with an answer.
Out loud that is a buzz roll, and it is why a buzz *accelerates* and then stops
being a roll at all.

## What I measured

Everything is `sketches/chatter/stick.ts`, checked in node before anything made
a sound, with the same recursion the worklet runs.

**The known-answer channel is exact this time, not a scaling.** For m·c̈ = −k·c^p
the quadrature closes in Beta functions,
t_c = (2·c_max/v)·B(1/(p+1), ½)/(p+1). Two checks before any of it is used:

| | result |
| --- | --- |
| Γ against Γ(½)=√π, Γ(5)=4!, Γ(9)=8! | exact to 9 decimals |
| p = 1 must give π√(m/k), half a period of a mass on a spring | **exact to 9 figures, 9 of 9 cells** |
| beam roots against Blevins | 4.73004 / 7.85320 / 10.99561 / 14.13717 / 17.27876 |

and then the integrator against the formula, on a rigid bar, over p ∈ {1, 1.5,
2.5, 3} × v ∈ {0.3, 1, 3}: worst departure **3.06%**, every error negative and
about a third of a sample. Raising the sample rate ×64 walks the ratio
**0.99247 → 0.99968**, so the 3% was the sample period and not the model. The
fitted exponent is 0.000000 at p = 1 (to 7e−32) and −0.5012 against −0.5 at
p = 3.

**Restitution is velocity-independent only for a linear spring.** p = 1 gives
0.8401 / 0.8400 / 0.8401 / 0.8403 across a 30× speed range; p = 3 gives
0.9047 → 0.6875 across the same. A harder strike bounces less well — the same
nonlinearity `mallet` found in the contact time, seen from the other side, and
p = 1 is the control that says the measurement is not inventing it.

**And the headline: a bar is not a table.** A table does not push back. A bar is
still ringing when the stick comes down again, and a surface moving upward at
the moment of impact hands energy back. So the geometric law is a statement
about *dead* bars:

| bar decay | bounces | gap ratio ÷ e | collapse ÷ ballistic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.6 ms | 20 | **1.028** | 1.36 |
| 2 ms | 20 | 1.028 | 1.36 |
| 8 ms | 20 | 1.028 | 1.36 |
| 40 ms | 20 | 1.028 | 1.36 |
| 0.3 s | 51 | 1.177 | 3.60 |
| 2.4 s | 496 | **1.254** | **28.55** |

The first four rows are identical to four figures: below about 40 ms of ring
the bar might as well be a table, and the textbook holds. Across press on a
dead bar the gap ratio lands at 0.968–1.088 of the restitution and the collapse
at 1.14–1.66× the ballistic sum 2v₀e/(a(1−e)).

**A buzz lasts 5.3–20.0× longer on a ringing bar** than on a dead one at the
same press. That is why a buzz on a practice pad dies and a buzz on a marimba
goes on, and it is one slider in the sketch.

**And the gap sequences are the picture.** First sixteen, in milliseconds:

```
dead      11.98 9.35 6.48 5.35 3.67 2.04 2.23 1.90 1.75 1.58 1.37 1.19 1.08 1.00 0.88 0.75
ringing   11.92 9.02 2.37 7.94 1.58 6.94 2.02 3.38 8.67 1.17 0.27 4.15 3.90 8.29 6.54 9.42
```

The dead bar decays. The ringing one is thrown back up, over and over, and
never settles into a sequence at all.

**From the sound.** The chattering, off the recording, counting the broadband
steps an impact makes:

| bar decay | impacts heard | buzz length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 ms | 9 | 0.051 s |
| 20 ms | 8 | 0.057 s |
| 0.2 s | 9 | 0.119 s |
| 1.6 s | 115 | 0.921 s |
| 3 s | **202** | **1.928 s** |

A **38× range in how long a buzz lasts**, from the bar's decay alone, with the
stick and the press untouched.

**And the two integrators are the same system, exactly, where the system allows
it.** With the bar silenced first so both start from rest:

| press | bar decay | model bounces | worklet | model collapse | worklet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 ms | 19 | **19** | 135.6 ms | **135.6 ms** |
| 1 | 0.3 s | 77 | **77** | 316.5 ms | **316.5 ms** |
| 3 | 1.6 s | 133 | 137 | 583.5 ms | 583.6 ms |
| 1 | 1.6 s | 210 | 225 | 994.9 ms | 1026.3 ms |
| 0.3 | 1.6 s | 154 | 100 | 1237.9 ms | 956.6 ms |

Bit-identical on the short decays and divergent on the long ones, which is the
chaos result stated the other way round: node and Chromium agree until the
system starts amplifying the last bit of a double.

**Half a result on the pressed pitch.** A stuck stick is an attached mass, so
the pressed bar should be a two-oscillator avoided crossing rather than one
detuned note. The lower branch measures within **3–7%** of the closed form at
press 0.08 and 0.5. At press 2 and 6 the measurement falls off the bottom of
its scan range — because by the time the collapse is over the stick has damped
the bar so hard there is no tone left to find. That is a real thing about press
rolls rather than a broken detector, and the control says the detector works:
struck and barely pressed reads **+4 cents** against the nominal.

Levels: **0.576** at the defaults and 0.576 worst over eight configurations
covering the panel. The peak of this texture swings ±60% between windows
because the line varies from drop to drop, so that is a ceiling rather than a
calibration.

## What went wrong

**An uninitialised modal mass made the whole sketch silent, and silence reads
as a clean zero.** A `Float64Array` zero-fills, and the worklet's first
`process()` runs before the `modes` message arrives — so `F·shape/mmass` was
0/0 on the first block. NaN is absorbing: it never left the modal state, the
output guard turned it into silence, and the verification run came back with
every number a tidy `0.000` rather than an error. `mallet` initialises
`mmass[i] = 1` in its constructor for exactly this reason and I had copied
everything except that loop. The worklet now rescues NaN state and counts it,
so it can never be invisible again.

**I compared two integrators that were not doing the same thing, twice.** The
worklet starts the stick at the drop speed; `stick.ts`'s `run()` started it at
rest. And the model clamped the bar's t60 at 20 ms where the worklet clamped at
0.4 ms, so the dead-bar rows were not even the same bar. Both are the kind of
difference that makes a cross-check meaningless while looking like a result.

**Then the remaining disagreement turned out not to be a disagreement.** After
both were fixed the collapse times agreed to about 25% but the bounce counts
still differed by up to 40%, and it is tempting to keep hunting. The right
question was whether the bounce count is a reproducible quantity at all:

| perturbation of the drop speed | bounces | collapse | Δ bounces | Δ collapse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| reference | 216 | 901.7 ms | | |
| 1 part in 10¹⁵ | 211 | 855.5 ms | **−2.3%** | −5.1% |
| 1 part in 10¹² | 165 | 791.0 ms | −23.6% | −12.3% |
| 1 part in 10⁹ | 285 | 1018.6 ms | +31.9% | +13.0% |

One part in 10¹⁵ is the last bit of a double. A bouncing ball on a vibrating
surface is a chaotic system, so two V8 builds disagreeing in the last ulp of
`Math.pow` is a complete explanation of the spread, and **the bounce count is
not an observable**. The collapse time is, and only to about 10%. Worth having
asked before spending an afternoon on it.

**A prediction I made and killed.** I expected the collapse to end when the gap
fell below the contact time — below that the next impact begins before the last
one ends, so there is nothing left to break. It does not. The final gaps run
**0.02–0.13× t_c**: the collapse goes on well past that point and is cut off by
the sample rate. Tidy, and wrong.

## Next

- [ ] Two-point contact: a mallet with a soft outer layer over a hard core has
      two regimes, and a buzz that crosses between them mid-collapse would
      change character halfway down. One extra spring in series.
- [ ] Stulov's relaxation term — contact hysteresis, where the head does not
      return what it stored. It should show up here as a restitution that
      depends on the *previous* bounce, which is a different kind of law from
      anything in this sketch.
- [ ] The bounce sequence on a ringing bar is chaotic and nobody has asked what
      *kind*. A return map of gap n+1 against gap n would say whether there is
      structure in it or only noise, and it is ten lines.
- [ ] Two sticks on one bar, which is a real roll. They share a resonator, so
      each one's bounces are thrown by the other's, and the question of whether
      they lock or scatter is the drummer's whole problem.
- [ ] `rosin` has a stick-slip state machine and this has a bouncing contact.
      A brush is both at once, and neither sketch can make one.
- [ ] The press detune is a two-oscillator avoided crossing, which is exactly
      `wolf`. Pressing harder walks the coupling, so a stick leaning into a bar
      should trace the same curve `wolf` drew for a string against a body.
