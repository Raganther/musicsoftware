# 2026-08-31 — inertia: an instrument whose notes are decided by physics

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/inertia`
**Settings worth keeping:** the defaults — root 48, well depth 36, spacing 1,
key pull 2 Hz, damping 0.5, seed 2. Play `a w s e d f t g y h u j k`. Then take
`Well spacing` to 2.4 and play the same keys: the keyboard is still chromatic
and the instrument is now 5-EDO, so your fingering no longer maps to pitch and
you land wherever the landscape catches you. That is the sketch. For the
prettiest version of the wobble set `Damping` to 0.08 and hit a key hard.

## What I tried

Improvisation/performance, the stalest family — the last genuinely *playable*
instrument was `conduct` on 2026-08-15, and the last five days have been
synthesis, strange, rhythm, composition and synthesis again.

Pressing a key on a keyboard *assigns* a pitch. I wanted a keyboard where a key
is only an **aim**. So pitch became a particle with a position x (semitones
above the root) and a velocity; holding a key applies a spring force toward its
note; and letting go leaves damping and a landscape:

    U(x) = A·(1 − cos(2πx/s))

a row of wells s semitones apart. **The instrument's pitches are the minima of
that landscape and owe nothing to where the keys are.** At s = 1 the two
coincide and it plays normally. At s = 2.4 the keyboard is chromatic and the
instrument is 5-EDO.

The reason to build it rather than describe it is that the landscape makes hard
predictions:

- a small landing wobbles at **√A / s** Hz
- the barrier between wells is **2A**, so the pitch escapes when **|v| > 2√A**
- with damping, that threshold must sit *above* 2√A and converge onto it as
  damping falls, because climbing costs energy

and with `Well depth` at 0 there is no landscape at all, which makes a keypress
a plain second-order step response whose overshoot is exactly
exp(−πζ/√(1−ζ²)). A control that is arithmetic rather than a vibe.

## What I measured

Everything is read off the **pitch track of the recorded audio**. The
predictions come from the panel settings and are never read back from the
model's own state.

**The tracker first, against answers already known.** Eight pitches held
perfectly still, tracked and compared with what was asked for: **worst error
0.004 semitones**. Nothing below is worth quoting without that line above it.

**The control (well depth 0) is exact.**

| ζ | overshoot pred / meas | ring Hz pred / meas |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.15 | 62.1% / 61.9% | 1.483 / 1.482 |
| 0.25 | 44.4% / 44.2% | 1.452 / 1.456 |
| 0.40 | 25.4% / 24.9% | 1.375 / 1.391 |
| 0.60 | 9.5% / 9.2% | 1.200 / 1.209 |
| 0.80 | 1.5% / 1.5% | 0.900 / — |
| 1.10 | 0.0% / **0.0%** | — / — |

Worst overshoot error **0.4 percentage points**; worst ring-rate error
**1.2%**; and the overdamped case does not overshoot at all, which it must not.

**A small landing wobbles at √A/s** — worst **0.7%** across six landscapes from
A = 16 to 100 and s = 1 to 2.4.

**Escape velocity, which is the headline.**

| ζ | measured threshold | excess over 2√A = 12.00 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.05 | 12 – 12.5 st/s | **2.1%** |
| 0.12 | 12.5 – 13 st/s | 6.3% |
| 0.25 | 13 – 14 st/s | 12.5% |
| 0.50 | 15 – 16 st/s | 29.2% |

At the lightest damping the threshold brackets the undamped prediction within
half a semitone per second. The excess then rises monotonically with damping,
and **no threshold ever fell below 12.0** — the model never beats its own energy
budget. The direction is the part I care about: a single number can be hit by
luck, a monotone trend with a floor cannot.

**And a prediction I did not set out to make.** The wells are *cosine* wells,
which makes each one a pendulum, so the wobble must **slow down as you land
harder**:

| kick / escape | swing | predicted | measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.15 | 17° | 5.966 | 6.121 |
| 0.30 | 35° | 5.861 | 5.966 |
| 0.45 | 53° | 5.675 | 5.783 |
| 0.60 | 74° | 5.383 | 5.447 |
| 0.75 | 97° | 4.932 | 5.088 |
| 0.90 | 128° | 4.133 | 4.398 |

The rate falls **28.1%** from the softest landing to the hardest against
**30.7%** predicted by the exact pendulum period (2/π)·K(sin²(θ₀/2)). Every
measured value sits slightly *fast*, by 1.2–6.4%, and the bias grows with
amplitude — which is the signature of the obvious cause: damping shrinks the
swing while it is being measured, so the average amplitude is below the initial
one. I would rather report a residual with the right sign and an explanation
than tune it away.

This is also the best thing about the instrument to play: **how hard you arrive
changes the vibrato you arrive with.**

Levels: 0.496 pre-limiter at defaults, 0.807 loudest (level 1.0 with a key
held), 0.377 with the room up. Smoke: 37 sketches, no clipping, clean teardown.

## What went wrong

**The measurement fought back for most of the day, and every failure was in the
harness rather than the sketch.** Worth writing down because three of the four
are general.

**An unseeded reverb was colouring the thing being measured.** Pitch readings
came back exactly +12 semitones — sometimes for a whole run, sometimes for one
capture, never reproducibly. The worklet's own state was bit-identical across
runs (`x`, `v`, `env`, `bright` all equal to the digit), and the *spectrum* was
not: the fundamental-to-octave ratio wandered between 1.9 and 8.2, and on the
bad runs the octave was the stronger partial, so YIN was right and the audio was
wrong. `noiseBuffer()` in `@core` builds the reverb's impulse response from
unseeded `Math.random()`, so every page load gets a different room — and two
consecutive runs producing *identical* numbers, because the buffer is
module-cached, is what gave it away. The harness now taps the instrument's own
node, before the room. **Level checks belong on the master, where the ear is;
physics measurements do not.**

**My octave "fix" was worse than the bug.** I first tried to make YIN prefer a
longer period when it was nearly as periodic. But a signal periodic at T is
*also* periodic at 2T and 3T, so the rule fired on healthy frames as well: a
steady pitch came back as 19.02 semitones flat, which is exactly a factor of
three. Reverted. Octave errors are now caught downstream by continuity — a
12-semitone step between adjacent frames is never real, because the fastest
motion in any of these experiments is 0.33 of a semitone per hop — which cannot
quietly rewrite a healthy track the way the inside-YIN version could.

**And the window was too short for the lowest note.** The remaining failures
clustered at the bottom of the range: at 130.8 Hz a 30 ms analysis window held
barely two periods, and YIN read the octave for the entire capture. Steady
pitches are now measured with 60 ms and moving ones with 30 ms, because nothing
is smearing in the steady case and there is no reason to pay for a window that
long. **The window has to be sized from the thing being measured** — which is
the same sentence as yesterday's `wolf` entry, from the opposite direction.

**A 6% error that was a real effect.** The wobble law came out 6.7% low at the
two deepest wells and ~1% everywhere else. Not noise, and not a bug: my test
kick was 45% of escape speed, a 53° swing, nowhere near the small-angle limit
the √A/s law assumes. Dropping to 20% took the worst error to 0.7% — and the
discarded residual turned into the pendulum measurement above, which is now the
most musical thing in the sketch. Worth remembering that a residual with
structure is a result that has not been recognised yet.

**A unit bug found while diagnosing something else.** `Detune` is labelled cents
and was computed as `1 + cents × 0.01`, which is percent: the default 6-cent
detune was a 101-cent one, so the second oscillator sat a semitone sharp. Fixed
to `2^(cents/1200)`. The harness had set detune to 0 throughout, which is why
none of the measurements were affected and also why it survived that long.

## Next

- [ ] A landscape that is not evenly spaced. Wells at the degrees of a real
      scale would make it jam-compatible (`root` + `scale`) and let the depth of
      each well encode how strongly the tuning wants that note — but the exact
      arithmetic above is what evenly-spaced buys, so this is a trade.
- [ ] Velocity should set the kick, not the spring. Then hitting harder
      overshoots further and lands in a different well, which is a real playing
      skill and currently unavailable.
- [ ] Two particles, coupled — a duet where the voices push each other around
      the same landscape. Repulsive coupling would give something like
      `entrain`'s anti-phase result but in pitch rather than time.
- [ ] Tilt the landscape. Adding a constant force makes the wells asymmetric,
      so ascending and descending cost different amounts, which is what a real
      instrument's tessitura feels like.
- [ ] Measure the *capture basin* rather than the escape threshold: given a
      kick, which well does it end in? That is a staircase function of velocity
      and I have only measured its first step.
- [ ] `noiseBuffer()` should take a seed. Every reverb in this repo is
      currently a different room on every page load, which is invisible until
      something needs to measure through one.
