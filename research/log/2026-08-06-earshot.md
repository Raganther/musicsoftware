# 2026-08-06 — earshot: a crowd that tunes itself

**Sketches touched:** `sketches/earshot`
**Seeds worth keeping:** seed 5 at the defaults is the reference — it settles
into two clusters 578¢ apart and holds them. Seed 5 with earshot 240→260 is
the demo: nudging one slider across the knee collapses that chord to a unison.

## What I tried

Genuinely strange, the stalest family. Bounded-confidence opinion dynamics
(Hegselmann-Krause) with cents standing in for opinions: sixteen sustained
voices, each of which averages the voices within `earshot` cents of itself
each round and moves a fraction of the way there. Anything further away it
cannot hear and does not count.

The reason to build it as an instrument rather than a simulation is the
control surface. You don't play notes. You hold the pointer, which inserts a
voice that never moves and is worth three ordinary ones, and wait for the
crowd to walk to you — and you can hear them coming, because two voices a few
cents apart beat, and the beat rate falls as they approach.

## What I measured

**The knee is a ratio, not a number of cents.** Weighted spread of the
fundamental band, measured from audio after 27s (sine voices, drift off):

| initial spread | earshot | ratio | measured band spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 700¢ | 100¢ | 0.14 | 214¢ — clusters |
| 700¢ | 160¢ | 0.23 | 15¢ — unison |
| 1200¢ | 210¢ | 0.18 | 198¢ — clusters |
| 1200¢ | 240¢ | 0.20 | 9¢ — unison |
| 2000¢ | 300¢ | 0.15 | 457¢ — clusters |
| 2000¢ | 420¢ | 0.21 | 12¢ — unison |

The same 300¢ of tolerance unifies a crowd 1200¢ wide and fragments one 2000¢
wide. The transition sits near ratio **0.19** and it is a cliff: at 1200¢
initial spread, going 210¢ → 240¢ takes the result from 198¢ to 9¢, a 22×
collapse for a 14% parameter change. 0.19 is the known HK consensus threshold,
which I did not go looking for — it fell out of a sweep I ran to pick a
default.

**The zealot only works inside earshot.** Crowd at +5¢, earshot 300¢:

| held pitch | after 30s | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +200¢ (inside) | **+172¢** | 86% of the way, vs 85% predicted from pull·Z/(n+Z) |
| +500¢ (outside) | −12¢ | indistinguishable from control |
| none (control) | −31¢ | |

The first run of this test used +500¢ only and I nearly filed it as "the
gesture doesn't work". It was the model being right: nobody could hear it.

**Agreement is not the end — but only because I made it not be.** Pure
bounded confidence has an absorbing state, and as a piece it dies in it. With
`restless` 0 the loudest cluster moved twice in 69s (total travel 142¢). Two
rules fix it: a voice that has held still for ~16s eventually defects just
past its own cluster's earshot, and a voice that hears nobody listens further
until it finds someone. At `restless` 0.25 the loudest cluster changed 33
times in the same 69s (travel 18,435¢). At 0.6 it is not more active, just
blurrier — 25 changes.

**Snap-to-scale works, and doesn't finish the job.** Distance from the loudest
cluster to the nearest scale degree: 70¢ at snap 0, 26¢ at 0.5, 18¢ at 1.0.
Even at full strength the social pull holds the crowd ~18¢ off the grid, which
is a fifth of a semitone — audibly out of tune rather than expressively so.

## What the measurement changed

Two design decisions, both of which I had made from plausible reasoning and
both of which were wrong:

1. **Per-voice gain 0.55/n → 0.45/√n.** I wrote — in the code, as a comment
   justifying the level — that a converged crowd "sums in phase and loud", so
   1/n was the safe divisor. Measured peak at the default: **0.169**, against
   a target near 0.5. Independent oscillators sum incoherently, and they keep
   doing so at unison, because equal frequencies preserve whatever relative
   phase they started with. There is no moment where sixteen voices line up
   because they agree.
2. **The amplitude wobble had to go.** I gave each voice ±15% of slow tremolo
   on the assumption that sixteen sustained tones would need help breathing.
   Envelope modulation depth at unison: **0.25 with the wobble, 0.003
   without**. The decoration was two orders of magnitude louder than the
   phenomenon it was decorating — the residual beating between not-quite-agreed
   voices, which falls from ~4Hz to under 1Hz as they converge and is the
   entire sensory point of the instrument. Removed, and the note in the code
   now says why there isn't one.

## The recurring failure, seventh day — in a new place

I did not write the notes before measuring this time; the notes were written
last, from the harness output. The failure moved instead of stopping.

Both items above are the same error one layer down: a confident mechanistic
claim, asserted without a test, baked into a *design decision* rather than
into prose. "Sums in phase and loud" and "sustained voices need amplitude movement"
are exactly the register I have been inventing in the notes all week — specific,
mechanical, plausible — and writing the notes last does nothing about them,
because by then the wrong belief is already in the code and the notes merely
describe it.

So the rule needs widening. It was: any sentence with a number or a directional
claim carries `TODO:measure` until a harness produces it. It should be: any
*choice of constant* justified by a mechanism carries the same marker. Both
defects here were caught only because the level check and the beat-rate check
happened to be things I could measure — the beating one I nearly skipped,
because it looked like a perceptual claim rather than a measurable one.
